# Schedule console and Dash

The Dash level is the writer's desk across every project. Its **Schedule** tab is one console for managing a project: goals for every facet, the activity of the last year (a grid or lines), a week of shifts with the backlog beside it, analytics and a review. Beside it sit the scratchpad and the project tiles (each a shelf in **its own project's colour**, its hue or else the default, so the shelves differ and a colour changed in Project settings shows on its shelf at once).

The same console is the **Project level's Schedule tab**, over that project alone. (It replaces the Dash's Schedule, Checklist and Analytics tabs and the Project's Schedule placeholder and Analytics tab.)

Code: `writer/schedule/` (`ScheduleConsole.tsx` and a file for each tile), `lib/scheduleCalendar.ts`, `lib/projection.ts`, `lib/recurrence.ts`, `lib/shiftEdit.ts`, `lib/timeline.ts`, `writer/Dashboard.tsx` (`Scratchpad`), `writer/levels/dashTabs.tsx`, `levelBodies.tsx` (`ProjectTiles`); `styles/_schedule.scss`. A mockup of the console in the Day and Night zones is kept in [`../../../mockups/schedule-console/`](../../../mockups/schedule-console/).

## The console's tiles

The console is its own [split grid](../SplitTiles/README.md) inside the tab (saved as `scriblr.writer.schedule.tiles.<dash|project>`), with four tiles, each of which can be dragged by its title bar and resized by a divider. At Mid (the level beside the focused one) the tab is **the same console in one column** (`columns="one"` on the split grid, a pane `.wrTabPane--console` 480 to 760px high): the open tiles stack, each at least 300px high and all the same height, the console draws its own bar of tile tabs above them, and the pane scrolls when they do not fit. The Plot, Schedule and Project consoles all do this.

- **Goals** (`GoalsCard`): this week's words, words a writing day, the streak and the best streak, then each goal. A goal is for a **facet** (Outline, Plot, Draft, Revision, Project) and a scope (the project, a book or a chapter), with a daily, a weekly and a total target and an optional **deadline**. It shows its progress as a meter in the facet's colour, today's and the week's share, and a status chip: **▲ Ahead**, **● On track**, **▼ Behind** or **✓ Done**, from the pace of the last 28 days against the pace the deadline needs (the glyph, not only the colour, says which). At the Dash the account's own goals and each project's are listed, each tagged.
- **Analytics** (`AnalyticsPanel`, `Fold`): one tile for the activity, the review and the charts. **The Activity chart is first and always open** (`ActivityCard`, `ActivityControls`; never under 260px, and it takes the height the folds below it leave); under it are **Review** and **Charts**, each a **fold** (closed until opened; which are open is remembered as `scriblr.writer.schedule.analytics.open`), and the tile scrolls when they are open. The Activity chart's toggles are in the **title bar**, in **up to three rows**: the **handle and the title are always the top row**; the **facet toggles wrap into a row below them** when the bar is too narrow to hold them beside the title; and the **chart and shade toggles wrap into a third row** below those, as **squares** (the same 26px squares as every icon button). In a wide tile they all sit on the top row, on the right. The toggles are smaller and closer than a button in a tile's body: the facets to show (several, all on to begin with, at least one stays on), the chart as two icons (**Grid**, **Line graph**) and, for the grid, what the shades are measured against: **%** of the daily goal or **#** against the busiest day (a facet without a daily goal uses the busiest day). There is no legend and no footnote under the chart.
  - **Review** (`ReviewCard`): a week or a month of each facet against the one before it, and how many days had any work.
  - **Charts** (`AnalyticsCard`): words drafted a month by book; the outline's and the plot's growth by kind; revision flags applied and resolved a week; and when in the week the work happens (the days whose events are still kept).
- **Agenda** (`ScheduleCard`): Day, Week, Month, **Year** and **Checklist** (the list of days) views like a calendar; see below.
- **Backlog** (`Backlog`): its own tile, beside the schedule, top to bottom: a **search** box with facet chips, a project choice (when more than one), a sort (priority, due date, newest, manual) and a done choice (hide, done today, show all), all but the search remembered; then **Today** (the tasks on today's shifts, the one running now first, then the routines due today), **Overdue** (tasks past their due day and routines past their range, marked), the tasks by **priority** (High, Medium, Low), the routines inside their range (low priority) and the paused ones, **None** (the tasks without a priority) in a fold that opens by itself when nothing else is waiting, the done tasks, and, folded, the routines coming up. Any sort but priority lists the tasks flat.
  - A **task** is a row: the priority flag, a tick, its name with what is known (due day, estimate, the facet's dot, the node it is about, the shift it is on, "Carried" when it came back from a shift that ended with it undone) and a thin bar for its progress. The flag shows the priority by its shape (filled for High, half-filled for Medium, dashed for Low, faint for None) and, pressed, **opens into the four** to pick from. The row **opens in place** into its notes, priority, due day, estimate in minutes, facet, node (and an aim counted from when it was added) and **subtasks**; each change is made at once. The grip drags it onto a shift in the Agenda; with the manual sort a second grip reorders (a pointer drag, or Alt+Up and Alt+Down).
  - The add box takes a **quick-add line**: `Revise ch 3 !high ~2h fri @chapter 3` is a task "Revise ch 3" of high priority, two hours long, due the next Friday, about the node "chapter 3"; what it understands is shown under the box before Enter. A routine is added with the same **repeat picker** a shift uses (Every day, Weekdays, Weekly on chosen days, Custom, After an interval).
  - A one-off shift that has ended with tasks unticked sends them back here, marked "Carried", at the top of their priority; routines never carry over.

## The Project tab

The Dash's **Project** tab (and the Project level's own **Project** tab, the same console for the open project) is where a project's plan is **defined**: the Backlog, the Agenda and the Goals read the same planner, so what is defined here shows there. It is a split grid of four tiles (`ProjectConsole`, saved as `scriblr.writer.plan.tiles.<dash|project>`) with a rail of tile tabs in its title bar. At the Dash a choice there (**Plan for**) picks the **account**, one of the projects or the **template**; the account's plan is kept in the settings store, a project's in its `project.json`. At Mid the tab shows, for each project, the phase it is in with how far it is, the next milestone with the days to go, and the counts (`ProjectSummary`).

- **Plan** (`PlanTimeline`, the first tile; its id stays `phases`): the project's phases **on rows**: a row for the **project**, one for each **book** under it and one for each **chapter** under its book (a chapter's row is shown once it has a phase, or for every chapter with the page icon). A row has a head (a fold chevron, its level icon, its name and how many phases it holds, and a plus that adds a phase to it) and the **cards** of its phases in their **sequence**. Two views, switched by two icons: **Dates** puts a lane of bars on a chart of days above the cards, and **Sequence** shows just the cards, each with its milestones. In the Dates view three icons zoom the chart to **months**, **weeks** or **days** (4, 14 or 44px a day; the chart scrolls sideways, by the wheel too, and every lane scrolls together).
- **A phase card** (`PhaseCard`, `.wrPhaseCard`): a grip, its title (with the pass number of its kind, "Revision · 2"), the **result** it builds to, a status chip (**Planned** dashed, **Active**, **Done** filled) and how far it is (a thin bar and a percentage: the work in its milestones and tagged with it, its milestones and its goals, done over all of them). It opens in place into its title, result, description, a status choice, its **start** (setting it pins the phase there, with a lock icon to pin or free it) and its length in **days**; once everything is done it offers **Mark the phase done**. Deleting a phase takes its milestones with it and lets go of what was placed in them. Its **milestones** are listed under it (always in the Sequence view, while open in the Dates view). The phase's dates **follow the sequence**: each phase begins the day after the one before it ends, as long as it is, until one is pinned; a done phase never moves. Phases of no row (their book or chapter was deleted) are listed apart so none is lost.
- **A milestone** (`MilestoneSection`, `.wrSection`) is a section of its phase and a **deliverable**: a grip, a tick (**achieved** when everything placed in it is done, or when ticked by hand), a title, its date, earlier and later icons and delete; a field for the result it delivers, its length in days (empty shares what is left of the phase) and the day it ends on, with a lock to pin it, and the **work placed in it** as chips (an icon for a task, process, exercise or routine, its name, a tick when done, and an icon to take it out), with a thin progress bar. A process counts done when every task it made is done, an exercise or a routine when it has been done since the phase began. The sections are dated one after another across the phase.
- **Kinds, repeats and apply to all.** A **kind** (`PhaseKind`, edited in the **Phase kinds** fold under the rows, `KindsPanel`) is a recipe for a phase that comes round, like a revision pass: a title, a result, a description, a length and milestones (each with a title, a result and a length). A row with kinds has a **New pass of…** choice in its head; a pass starts from the kind (its result, description and length, and a section for each of its milestones), is its own card with its own dates, status and work, and is numbered among the passes of its kind on its row ("Revision · 2"). **A pass follows its kind in every field it has not changed itself**: editing the kind carries to each such pass as it is made (a milestone the kind gains is added to every pass, one it loses is taken out), while a field a pass has changed is marked, and a **reset** icon (the restore arrow) beside the card's note puts that field back to what the kind says (a milestone has one too, in its head). A phase can be **kept as a kind** (the bookmark icon: it becomes the first pass of it), and deleting a kind leaves its passes as plain phases. In an open card, **Repeat** (the copy icon) puts the same phase again, next in its row, as a plain card with its sections (and none of the work or progress), and **Apply to other rows** (the stack icon) opens a list of the books and chapters under the row, each a checkbox (a row that has a phase of that name already is unchecked and says so) and a tick icon that stamps a copy onto each checked row, still a pass of its kind; a chapter phase has nothing to apply to.
- **The projection** (`ProjectionChip`, `.wrProj`; the sums in `lib/planProjection.ts`) says when the work left would be done. **The time of a piece of work** is its own estimate first (a task its minutes, an exercise its duration, a process what is left of the tasks it made or else the sum of its steps); work with no estimate takes **the average of the estimated work of its kind in the project** (else 30 minutes); **a routine counts its time every time it comes round** before the milestone's date; **work that is done counts nothing**, so the sum shrinks as work is ticked off. That is set against **the time scheduled**: a week of it is the average of the project's shifts over the **next four weeks** (a repeating shift by its pattern, rest days left out, one-offs and pauses as they fall), **by facet** (work of a facet against shifts of that facet, work with no facet against any), and for a book or chapter row **the shifts aimed at that book or chapter** (a book: its chapters too), falling back to all of the project's when none is. It gives the **weeks** of the scheduled week the work needs, and by walking through the shifts the **working days and shifts** it takes and **the day it ends on**. A milestone's work is walked through after the previous milestone's, a phase after the phase before it in its row (and not before its planned start nor today), so **a phase that runs late pushes the next**; phases, and the project, book and chapter rows, add up their milestones the same way (a row is its own phases). The chip reads "12 h · 3 wk · ends Nov 28 · ▼ Behind 5d": the glyph (**▲** ahead, **●** on track within a day, **▼** behind) says how the day it ends on is against the day the plan gives it, so the colour is not all there is to it; its tooltip says it in full. It is on every milestone, phase card and row head; with no shifts it says **no shifts scheduled**, and work no shift of its facet can take says **no time scheduled for it**. In the Dates view a **hatched tail** after a bar shows where the phase is projected to run past its end. A drafting phase (one with Draft work) with a draft goal also has a second line, **the words left against the recent words a day** ("50,000 words left of 80,000; at 600 a day, about Jan 24"). The plan itself is never changed by it.
- **Dragging stays in its row.** Drag a phase by its grip onto another card of its row to put it there, a milestone onto another milestone (or a phase card, for last) of its row, a piece of work onto another milestone of its row; a drop on another row is **refused** with a line that says why (a `role="status"` notice with a dismiss icon), and the target shows a dashed, faded outline while the drag is over it. Work that is not placed yet (a card from the Work tile or a task from the Backlog) can be dropped on a milestone of any row. In the Dates view a bar is dragged sideways to put the phase in another place in its row (the others are dated again), its right edge changes its length, and with the keyboard the arrows move the focused bar a place, **Alt** and the arrows its length a day, **Shift** a week.
- Under the rows a fold, **Project dates and rest days** (`ProjectDates`), holds the project's start and end and its rest days (a name and two days each, with add and delete); the Agenda's Settings popover edits the same data. The chart shades the rest days and draws a line for today.
- **Goals**: the Goals card of the Schedule console, for the one plan, with a **Phase** choice on a goal.
- **Work** (`WorkTile`): four folds, each with an add box and cards that open in place. **Processes** (`ProcessCard`): a title and description, the facets it is for (the facet icons) and the phases, the **milestones it is placed in** (chips), the **milestones it needs done first** (until they are, it shows a **Blocked** chip with a lock and cannot be dropped on the Agenda), its **step groups** (each **In order** with numbered steps, or **Any order** with bullets; a step has a label and a minutes estimate) and its **links** to other processes (*Comes before*, *Comes after*, *Goes with*; one link is kept on both sides, read from each). A **Stage n** chip shows its place in the sequence the ordered links make; processes that go with each other share a stage, and ones linked in a circle are named under the fold. **Exercises** (`ExerciseCard`): a prompt, minutes, times a week, the facets, phases and the milestones it is placed in, and a log (**I did it** counts one and notes the day). **Routines**: the label, its facet, phase and milestone and the repeat picker (with *After an interval*). **Tasks**: the Backlog's task row, with a phase choice and a **milestone** choice, and the phase and milestone beside the task; a task of a repeating shift is left to the Agenda's checklist. Every routine, process and exercise card has a **grip** (20px) to drag it onto the Agenda; a process that needs milestones not yet done has a **lock** in its place, which says which.
- **Template** (`TemplateTile`, the filled folder with a plus cut out of it: `FolderPlusIcon`) and **the project template**. The project template is **one plan for the account** (a planner kept in the settings store as `scriblr.writer.projectTemplate`), edited by choosing **Template** under *Plan for* at the Dash. It is edited with the same tiles, but its dates are **days from a project's start**: a phase has **Starts on day** and **Lasts (days)**, a milestone a **day**, and there is no timeline, status, progress or tick (nothing in it is blocked, and its cards have no grip, since the template is not on the Agenda). The **Template** tile, on the Dash and in a project's own tab, works on whichever plan is chosen: **From the template** lists what the template has, a group for each kind (phases, milestones, processes, exercises, routines, tasks, goals) with a box for the whole group and one for each item; picking a process **brings the milestones it needs**. A **Starts on** day is asked (the project's own start, else today) and the **copy** icon makes **copies, never links**: phases and milestones are dated from that day, a routine counts from it, a task starts afresh (no shift, no due day, not done, no node), an exercise has no log, a goal drops its deadline and its book or chapter, and a link between processes is kept only when both were copied. A project with no dates of its own takes the start day as its start and the end of the last phase copied as its end. A line under the card says what was copied and, while nothing else has changed the plan, an **Undo the copy** icon takes it back. **Save to the template** does it the other way: the plan's own items (the tasks not yet done) are picked and added to the template, their dates turned into days from the project's start (or its earliest date). A **new project** can start from the template: when the template has something in it, a switch under the new project's title, **From the template** (on to begin with), gives it a copy of all of it, dated from today.
- The cards are the tile's fill-4 colour; **Active** and **Blocked** phases and processes have a 3px edge in the level's ink; a button is an icon, as everywhere in the console. A project's plan is **one copy shared by every console** (`schedule/planStore.ts`), because the backend replaces a project's whole planner on a save: two consoles that kept their own would overwrite one another.

## The activity grid

`.wrSchedCal`. The Activity tile's margin round the calendar is **5px** (the other tiles' is 14px). The grid has **two orientations**, by the shape of its tile: **landscape** when the tile is wider than tall, **portrait** when it is taller than wide or square. A day is a cell. The grid **scrolls along its long side** (the wheel, a swipe, touch): sideways in landscape, down in portrait. There is no scrollbar (see [interaction](../../interaction.md)).

**Landscape.** A cell for each day of the last **52 weeks**, a week to a column with the oldest at the left, the week starting on **Monday** (or Sunday: a setting). One letter beside **every** row (M T W T F S S), **between the monthly bars and the grid**. Above the columns each month is a **rectangle** with its name **in capitals**, over the columns of the weeks that begin in it; the name is **abbreviated to fit** its rectangle (whole where it fits, three letters, then one), the whole name in the tooltip. The cells are **square** (8px to 40px) while the tile is tall enough, and are **squished in height** (down to 2px, 8px wide) when it is not, so the calendar is **never cut off vertically**. The **plain wheel scrolls it sideways** (at either end the page gets the wheel), and **resizing the tile keeps the current week at the right edge** (it does not stay at the left).

**Portrait.** The grid is an actual **calendar**. The day letters are across the top and under them each **month**, the newest first, is a **divider with its name in capitals** over its weeks, the newest week first (so **the current week is the top row**), each week a row of seven with a day in every place the month has: a week that spans two months is a row in both, each holding only its own days. The cells are **square** and as wide as the seven columns allow, 8px to 40px, so the months run down past the bottom of the tile: the wheel and touch scroll it **down** through them. The older months are bars under the weeks, **grouped by year**: a divider with the year, then that year's months' bars, newest first, each with its month's number at its right (see below).

**Today** has a 2px outline in the level's ink (white) **inside** its cell, with a 2px gap of the tile's colour before the shade, so it shows on the lightest step too. Cells are square-cornered, like everything on a level.

A cell is shaded in four steps of the **colour of the facet with the most activity that day** (the facet with the highest level; the larger share of its measure when levels tie; the accent hue where no facet applies), saturated **mid tones** (OKLCH lightness 0.67, 0.72, 0.77 and 0.82 at chroma 0.17, so every hue steps alike; `CAL_STEPS` in `theme/zoneLooks.ts`), never white or black, the step being the **highest** of the selected facets' levels: against the daily goal where there is one (a goal met is the darkest step; the hover says "goal met"), else against that facet's busiest day. The hover names each facet's count: "Sat, Oct 31 · Outline 2 nodes · Draft 1,808 words".

Before the 12 months, each older month is a **bar**, stacked by facet (each facet scaled by its own busiest month so that words and nodes can share a bar), its length the share of the busiest month's average a day. In landscape a bar is one column wide and seven rows tall, with a rectangle over the bars for each **year** and the **month's number** (1 to 12) under each bar; in portrait a bar is one row across the seven columns, newest first under the weeks, **grouped under a divider for its year**, with the month's number at the right. The day letters stay at the left edge of what is on view in landscape (`position: sticky`), and across the top in portrait.

## The line graph

`.wrSchedLine`. One line per facet that has a **total goal**: the share of that goal done over time, 0 to 100%, with a daily point for the last 12 months and a monthly one before. Past today the line carries on **dotted** at the last 28 days' pace until it reaches 100% or the deadline (a vertical rule). Each facet's end marker has its own shape (circle, square, diamond, triangle, cross), so colour is never the only cue. Hover, or the left and right arrow keys on the focused chart (Shift for a week), read every value on a day.

A facet's **goal-met ring** (`.wrSchedRing`) circles the line, in the facet's own colour, at the first day it reaches 100% (`goalMetOn`); its hover says "Draft goal met 2026-10-02", and the tooltip of a day from then on ticks the facet's percentage. A facet that has not reached its goal has no ring.

## Facet colours

`--wr-f-outline`, `-plot`, `-draft`, `-revision`, `-project` are set on the console from five hues the user picks (Settings, in the console's header; each is the app's colour range, a gradient of every hue at the facet colour's own saturation and lightness). The saturation and lightness are one pair for every zone (`FACET_COLOR` in `theme/zoneLooks.ts`), light enough to read on the level's dark fill, so a line, a dot, an icon or a bar reads on the tile (3:1) whatever hue is picked; the contrast page audits every facet hue on every level hue.

**Facet icons** (`FacetIcon`, `.wrFacetIcon`, 20px, in the facet's colour) are the icons of the levels: **Outline** (boxes in boxes), **Plot** (the indented lines), **Draft** (the quill), **Revision** (the stacked pages) and **Project** (the folder). They stand where a facet's **name is written beside them**, as a chip or a legend row: the facet toggles of the Activity tile, the Backlog's facet filter (whose **All** has a stack, in the chip's own ink), the shift panel's facet choice, the colour rows of Settings, a goal's title, the Review's rows and the line graph's day tooltip. They are hidden from assistive tech, as the name beside them says it. The **Template tile** has its own icon, `FolderPlusIcon`: a filled folder with a plus cut out of it; the Project tab and the Project facet keep the plain folder outline. A **coloured dot** stays where there is no room or no name: a shift on a calendar's month cell, a task row's facet, a project's colour.

## The schedule

- **The tile is full height.** The day and week timeline (and the month grid) stretch to the height of the Schedule tile and scroll inside it; there is no fixed height. The timeline has **a row for every 10-minute slot** (144 a day, 20px each), each labelled with its time ("6:10 AM") on **one line**, never wrapped; the whole hours are brighter and bold, with a stronger line above them. It opens at 6 AM.

- **The year view** is twelve small calendars (their names in capitals), the days of the week as letters and each day a button: a day with a shift is **shaded in the colour of its first shift** (the same 26% mix as a shift's block, so it reads), has a **bar at its foot** when it has more than one, is **underlined** when a goal or the timeline is due, and is **outlined** when it is today. A click opens that day; Previous and Next move a year.
- **Shifts** are blocks on a day, snapped to 10 minutes. Drag across empty time to make one (it is made at the drag and opens in the side panel; closed without a name it goes again, and a plain click on empty time only closes the panel); drag a block to move it (across days in the week view) or its top or bottom edge to resize it; click it, or press Enter on it, to open it in the panel; **double-click it (or press F2) to change its title on the block**; right-click, or press and hold on a touch screen, for **Edit, Duplicate, Skip this day, Delete**. Hovering a block shows a small bar with **Duplicate** and **Delete** (or **Skip on this day** for a shift that repeats).
- **The side panel** (`ShiftPanel`, `.wrSchedPanel`) stands inside the Agenda tile beside the calendar, so the calendar stays visible; Escape, its close icon, or a click on empty time closes it. **Every field is applied as it is set: there is no Save.** For a shift that repeats, **the days the change is for are asked first**, at the top: **This day** (the default; a title and a time are that day's own, the facet and the aim are the pattern's), **This and following** (the pattern is split once, at this day) or **All of them**; how it repeats and its pauses are the pattern's, so they are changed only under the last two. Always shown: the title, the facet (five chips, each with the facet's icon), the day and the time (two pickers of 10-minute times and the length). **Folded**, each with its summary on the right: **Aim and target** (the aim in the facet's unit, and what it is on), **Repeat** (the repeat picker), **Pauses** (a shift that repeats) and **Checklist** (the tasks on the shift, ticked per day, with a task assigned from the Backlog or a new one). The icons at the top are Save as template, Delete (Skip this day, for one day of a pattern) and Close.
- **The next-up bar** (`NextUpBar`, `.wrNextUp`) is the first thing in the Agenda: **Now**, with the shift running, the time left and how many of its tasks are ticked off, or else **Next**, with the shift that starts next and the time until it ("in 2 h", "tomorrow at 9:00 AM"). It has the shift's facet colour as its edge, and pressing it opens the shift.
- **The now line is kept in view.** The day and the week open scrolled to it (one third down the tile), and while it is scrolled out of sight a **Now** icon button (a clock) appears in the head and brings it back.
- **What a block shows**, beyond its title and time: **how many of its tasks are ticked** after the time (`2/5`), the **name of the node** it is working on (italic, when the block is five slots or taller) and a thin **progress bar** of what its facet logged inside its slots against its aim (words, nodes or flags; four slots or taller), with "412 of 800" in its tooltip. The marks for done, missed and skipped are as before.
- **What falls due** is marked on its day, in the week's header, the month's cell, the agenda's day and the year's tooltip (the day is underlined there): a task on its due day, a routine on each day it comes round (one that comes round after an interval, on the last day of its range, and today once it is overdue), as a small outlined "2 due" with the names in its tooltip.
- **When a shift ends** with tasks that are not ticked off, a desktop notification says how many (with the reminder before a shift, once reminders are turned on in the settings).
- **At the Dash** each **project's own colour** (its hue, at the facet colours' saturation and lightness) is a 4px edge on its blocks and agenda rows and a dot beside its tasks and routines; the **account's** shifts, routines and tasks have none, and are their own choice in the project list. One **project choice** ("All projects", the account, or one project) is in the Agenda's head and in the Backlog's toolbar and is **the same choice** in both. A **new shift asks which project** it is for (a choice at the top of its panel, a new task's in the Backlog), the last one used being the first answer. With one project there is no choice and no colour.
- **A narrow tile** (under **480px**) lists the days as a **checklist** instead of the day or week grid (the view that is kept is not changed), and the panel takes the whole tile.
- **Dropping from the Project tab** (a card's grip; `lib/planDrop.ts`): a **routine** on an empty slot makes a **shift that repeats as the routine does**, from that day, with the routine on it (one that comes round after an interval, which a shift cannot, makes a one-off shift); a **process** makes a **shift as long as its steps** (an hour without estimates) whose **checklist is the steps**, and in an ordered group each step **follows the one before it**: its box is dimmed and cannot be ticked, in the shift's checklist and in the Backlog, until that one is; an **exercise** makes a **shift as long as it** (never under 30 minutes) with one task, the exercise, its prompt in the task's notes. Dropped on a **shift**, the same things go on it instead. On a day of the month or year, where there is no time to point at, the shift starts at nine in the morning. A process that needs milestones not yet done is **refused**: a notice over the Agenda (`.wrSchedNotice`, dismissed by its icon) says which, and nothing is made. **Milestones and the starts and ends of phases** are marked on their days in the week header, the month, the year and the checklist, as the goals' deadlines are.
- **Dropping a task** (dragged by its grip in the Backlog): on a **shift** it goes on the shift; on an **empty slot** of a day or week a shift is made for it (its name, facet and node, as long as its estimate, an hour without one, starting where it was dropped); on a **day of the month or year** that day is its **due day**.
- **Repeat picker** (`RepeatPicker`): Does not repeat (a shift only), Every day, Weekdays, Weekly, Custom and After an interval (a routine only), with the weekday chips for Weekdays and Weekly, and "every N days or weeks" for Custom.
- Shifts and routines share how they come round; the checklist items assigned to a shift are ticked per day.
- **Routines** come round daily, weekly, or after an interval of days, weeks or months. An interval can be a **range** (every 7-10 days): inside it the routine is low priority but can be ticked, and past it the routine goes to the top as **Priority**. A routine can be **paused** for a length of time (skipped, not missed). **Rest days** (Settings) pause the routines and skip the shifts, and do not break a streak.
- **Deadlines** sit in a strip under the day's name, and on the month.
- **Settings** is a **gear icon** button ("Schedule settings") at the right of the **console's header** (the title bar of the Schedule tab's tile, not a tile of the console), opening a panel over the console from its top right (Esc closes it). It holds the week's first day, the facet colours, a reminder before each shift (a desktop notification), the project's timeline, **Export shifts (.ics)** and **Print the week**.

## Tokens

The console sits on the level it belongs to, so it uses the **level's tokens** like the other tabs ([level colours](../../level-colours.md)): the Schedule tab itself is on the level's fill like every other console (never the light surface), the console's tiles are one step deeper on `--wr-fill-3`, and a card, a day column, a shift's card behind it, a tile's title bar, a header, a chosen button's text and a menu are on `--wr-fill-4`; text in `--level-ink` (white) and `--level-ink-soft`; lines in `--level-line`, a button's wash in `--level-wash`; a field in `--level-input`, `--level-input-ink` and `--level-input-hint`; `--wr-f-*` for the facets. A chosen button, today's head and a Priority tag are inverted (`--level-ink` with `--wr-fill-4` for the text). The grid's shades are four OKLCH steps of `--cal-src` (the day's leading facet colour, else the accent hue); the lowest is audited at 3:1 on the tile in every hue. Text is 12px or 13px; 11px is for a shift's time only. The facet pairs are in the [contrast audit](../../contrast.md) (group *Schedule*), the rest in *Level panels*.

## The calendar icons

The icons of the calendar's views are **one calendar page** with slight differences: a **single rounded square** with a **line across it 25% of the way down**, dividing a **narrow header** (the top quarter) from the **body** where the days would be (`PAGE` and `PAGE_LINE` in `assets/icons/index.tsx`).

| Icon | Header | Body | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year | filled | open | none |
| Month | open (a little wider, 30%) | filled | none |
| Week | open | open | a filled horizontal stripe across the middle of the body, **edge to edge** |
| Day | open | open | one **small** filled square (3 of the 18 units) in the body |
| Today | none | none | no divider: just the rounded square with **today's day of the month** in it (31 in the drawings) |

Today is the same rounded square without the line. The Review's week and month switch (in the Analytics tile) uses the Week and Month icons.

## Buttons are icons

Every button in the console is an **icon** (`IconBtn`: the icon at **20px, never smaller**, in a **26px square**, its name as the accessible label and the tooltip). **All icon buttons are the same square**, in the title bars and the tiles alike (the `%` and `#` of the shade rule too); an icon is never squeezed under 20px by a flex box, and the grips and icons of the title bars of the console's own tiles and of the Schedule tab's tile are 20px as well: the gear for Settings; plus for a new goal, shift, task or rest days; a calendar page for Today; chevrons for Previous and Next; day, week, month and list icons for the calendar's views and week and month icons for the Review's; a pencil, a trash can, a tick and a cross for edit, remove, save and cancel; play and pause for a routine; an export arrow and a printer for the settings' export and print. Only a **choice that has no picture** stays text: the three answers to "Change which shifts?" (This day, This and following, All of them), the weekday letters, the context menu's items, the facet toggles (the facet's icon in its colour and its name), the priority choices (High, Medium, Low, None), the repeat picker's presets and a shift template's own name.

## Don't

- Don't colour a number: the figure is in the ink on the tile; state is in a bar's length, a chip's glyph and word, not only its hue.
- Don't show a chart without its value in text (a hover, an `aria-label`).
- Don't write a literal white or black for the today ring or the line axis: use `--level-ink`. Don't set the tile's own light background or `ink`: the console is on the level's dark fill, and a button or a field not restyled for it turns pale on pale.
- Don't put a scrollbar on the grid: it scrolls by the wheel and touch along its long side (see [interaction](../../interaction.md)).
