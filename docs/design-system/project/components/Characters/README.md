# Characters (the cast builder)

The **Characters** console is where the book's cast is built. It is a tab of the Writer's **Project** level (after Maps, before History), with the people icon. A character is its **profile in words** and its **portrait**: a card (a head and shoulders on a coloured tile) drawn from a handful of choices (ids of the portrait library), numbers (sliders) and colours. No image is stored; the card is drawn from the choices by code, wherever it is shown.

Code: `writer/characters/` (`CharactersConsole.tsx`, `CharacterList.tsx`, `PortraitSection.tsx`, `PortraitCard.tsx`, `PreviewSelect.tsx`, `CharFields.tsx`, `AppearanceSection.tsx`, `ForReadersSection.tsx`, `CastSection.tsx`, `SceneCast.tsx`, `castStore.ts`, `charFile.ts`, `sheet.tsx`, `ProfileSection.tsx`, `RelationshipsSection.tsx`, `StorySection.tsx`, `story.ts`, `describe.ts`, `useCharacters.ts`, `MannequinPanel.tsx`, `characters.scss`), the art mannequin in `writer/characters/figure/` (`figureState.ts`, `figureEditing.ts`, `figureModelStore.ts`, `Figure.tsx`, `CharacterArt.tsx`, `figureWords.ts`, and the editor: `FigureEditor.tsx`, `FigureCard.tsx`, `FigureSteps.tsx`) and the portrait engine in `writer/characters/portrait/` (`Portrait.tsx`, `library.ts`, `rig.ts`, `scene.ts`, `crops.ts`, `registry.ts`, `defaults.ts`, `age.ts`, `diff.ts`, `colour.ts`, `paths.ts`, `draw/{body,head,features,facial,hair}.ts`). Storage: the project's `characters` section (`project.json`), one entry per character; the API is `/api/projects/{id}/characters`.

## Anatomy

`.charPage` holds a bar (the title, the **sections**, the **Save cluster**) and `.charSplit`: the **cast** (`.charList`, 200px) and the open section. A character opens on **Appearance**; the bar's sections (icon buttons with a label and a title, `aria-pressed`, in a toolbar named "What to work on") are **Appearance**, **Portrait**, **Profile**, **Relationships**, **Story**, **The cast** and **For readers**. Below 860px of its own width the parts stack. The console sits on the tab's surface in the ink colour.

- **Cast** (`CharacterList.tsx`): each character is a **card on the colour of their own tile** (`.charRowItem--card`: the portrait's tile colour is the background, `--cardBg`, and the name and icons are written in an ink that can be read on it, `--cardInk`, computed from the colour by `readableInk`; both are set on the card because they are the character's data). In a **wide** console (the list is a 200px column) the card is a row: a **52px square portrait** on the left, flush with the bottom of the card so the shoulders run off its edge, and the **name right-aligned** beside it (one line, an ellipsis when long). In a **narrow** console (under 860px, where the list sits above the page) the cards are vertical: the **name above a larger (88px) portrait**, as many to a row as fit, in a list that scrolls on its own. Save to a file, copy and delete (which asks first) appear on hover or focus, under the name (wide) or over the bottom of the portrait (narrow). The open character is a ring in the ink (2px) and shows its portrait **as it is being edited**; hovering is a 1px ring (an overlay or the accent bar could be the card's own colour). The plus makes a new one with the default portrait.
  - **The list's width is adjustable** (`listWidth.ts`): a handle (`.charResizer`, `role="separator"`, the shared `SideResizer`) sits in the gap on the list's right edge, a 10px strip with a thin accent line on hover, on focus and while dragging. Drag it, or focus it and press the left and right arrow keys (16px, Shift for 80px); double-click or Home puts it back to the default. The width is **held between 160px and 40% of the console, never over 420px** (200px by default), by the code and again by the CSS so a window that shrinks keeps a stored width, and is **remembered** on this computer (`scriblr.characters.list.width`). The handle is a sibling of the list, not inside it (the list scrolls). Where the list lies above the page (a console under 860px) there is no column and no handle. The Items list keeps its fixed 200px.
- **Portrait** (`.charPortrait`): the **stage** (`.charPortraitStage`, 320px) holds the card as it is drawn, big, with the name and a line "role · age" under it (from the Profile: `Navigator · age 29`; empty when neither is written); beside it **one page of collapsible sections** (`.charAcc`): **Head**, **Hair**, **Face**, **Body**, **Fantasy** and **Card**, each a heading button (`aria-expanded`, with an icon, its name and a chevron) over **subsections** that open and close on their own (a smaller heading button each). **One section is open at a time** (opening one closes the other; pressing the open one closes it). The page is **remembered** on this computer (`scriblr.characters.portrait.panels`: the open section, the subsections that were closed and the sections that are locked) and opens on the Head, every subsection open and the Fantasy locked, when nothing is remembered. It **scrolls on its own** beside the card, which stays where it is (the bar is hidden, as in every list of the Writer); below 860px of the console's width the card sits above and the whole page scrolls. A subsection whose only field has its own name (Age, Ears, Skin, Expression, Name) does not write the label twice. A field that only matters when something is chosen (the colour of a horn, a crown, a hood, an item or a necklace, the hand that holds an item, the second eye's colour) appears once there is something to colour.
  - **Randomise** (`.charRandom`, under the card): a dice icon button ("Randomise the portrait") and, beside it, a **lock** for each section (the section's icon, a small padlock on it when locked, `aria-pressed`; "Lock Hair"). Randomise makes new choices for every section that is not locked, in one undo step, and leaves the locked ones as they are. **The Fantasy is locked until it is unlocked**, so a random character stays a person; unlocked, a roll gives some of the fantasy (at least one element). Ages and hair lengths are drawn so that most characters are adults with hair of ordinary length; a child has no beard.
  - **Head**: *Age* (nine stages from infant to elderly), *Head shape* (the shape, and a head width slider), *Ears*, *Skin* (colour).
  - **Hair**: *Length, placement and ends*, *Texture and style*, *Hairline*, *Colour* and *Facial hair* (see below).
  - **Face**: *Expression*, *Eyes* (shape, colour, size, spacing and tilt sliders), *Brows* (and their colour, or "Same as the hair"), *Nose* (shape, size and width sliders), *Mouth* (shape, lip colour or "Natural", size slider).
  - **Body**: *Build* (build, chest), *Shoulders and neck* (a slider for the width of each: 80 to 125% and 80 to 130% of the build's own), *Top* (fifteen necklines: crew, V, deep V, scoop, wide scoop, boat, square, sweetheart, henley, polo, mock neck, turtleneck, cowl, off the shoulder, strapless, and its colour), *Necklace* (fine chain, choker, beads, pearls, round pendant, gem amulet, locket, medallion, torc, and its metal).
  - **Fantasy**: *Ears* (pointed, elf, long elf, goblin, bat, fins), *Horns and antlers* (small horns, curved, ram, bull, long swept, unicorn, small antlers, stag antlers, brow spikes, and their colour), *Unusual skin* (blue, teal, green, grey, purple, red, gold, white and others; the usual skin colours stay in the Head), *Eyes* (style: glowing, solid colour, two colours (the picture's left eye has the second colour), slit pupils, goat pupils, blank white; unusual colours; and how many: one eye, a third eye on the forehead, four, six, an extra pair on the cheeks or at the temples), *Crown or circlet* (circlet, gem circlet, diadem, tiara, laurel wreath, flower crown, spiked crown, royal crown, and its metal), *Hood* (hood up, deep, pointed, cowl, hood down, and its colour), *Held item* (staff, crooked staff, crystal staff, wand, closed book, open book, scroll, lantern, torch, candle, goblet, flower, quill; the hand that holds it, the picture's left or right; and its colour).
  - **Card**: *Tile* (colour) and *Name*.
- **Hair** is a set of dropdowns: **length** (bald, buzzed, short, chin, shoulder, chest, stomach, hip, knee, body, Rapunzel), **placement** (as you look at the portrait: all front even, all front left, all front right, left front, right front, all behind), **ends** (straight, curve in, curve out), **texture** (1A to 4C: 1 straight, 2 wavy, 3 curly, 4 coily; A fine, B medium, C coarse), **style** (plain, braids, locs, twists, cornrows, Bantu knots, buns, ponytails, pigtails, cuts), **front** (the part or fringe), **hairline** (nine shapes, and its height), **edges**, hair colour, highlight colour, greying (with age by default) and facial hair with its colour. The placement and the ends are a choice only for loose hair that hangs (chin length or longer, not coily; a triangle or a shaved side has only front and behind, and the ends are straight for any other style); where they are not, one note says so. *All front, even* is both sides in front over the shoulders; *all front left* or *right* brings all the hair forward over one shoulder (a wide fall that crosses toward the chest) and tucks the other side behind the ear, which shows; *left front* or *right front* is one side in front and the other behind; *all behind* leaves the face clear. "Left" is the left of the picture.
- **Choices from a list** are `PreviewSelect`s (`.charPick`): a button showing the chosen choice drawn on this character, with its name; pressed, it opens a grid of every choice drawn the same way (the eyes as eyes, the nose as a nose, the hair as the whole head), each beside its name. It is a listbox: arrow keys move over the choices (a row at a time in the grid), Home and End go to the first and last, Enter or Space chooses, Escape closes without choosing and gives the focus back, Tab leaves, typing the start of a name goes to it, and a press outside closes it. The pictures are only drawn while it is open.
- **Hair shows only what fits.** Each style has the lengths it suits and the fronts it allows (a mohawk is not knee-length; bangs need more than a buzz cut), so the style and front dropdowns list only those; changing the length puts a style, a front, a placement or ends that no longer fit back to the first that do (plain first, then the usual placement and straight ends).
- **Colours** are a row of swatches and the browser's picker; a colour that can follow another (brows, lips, facial hair, highlights) has a first swatch that says so. The swatches and the portrait are the author's data, so they are drawn in their own colours. **Sliders** show their value beside them (the tilt of the eyes in degrees).
- Every change is **one undo step**; a slider or a colour moved several times in a row is one step.

## The portrait

A portrait is **flat colour shapes with real adult proportions, no outlines**, front view only, on a **rounded coloured tile** (a different colour per character; the shoulders run off the bottom of the tile). The eyes are halfway down an adult head; the neck is real; the shoulders slope; a child's head is larger and rounder with the eyes lower; an older face has lines, and the hair, brows and beard grey with age unless set by hand.

- **A list of filled paths.** The renderer returns `{d, fill, opacity}` shapes only, in a 200 by 200 tile, never a stroke. They are painted in this order: the tile, the hair behind, the hood when it is down (behind the neck), the body (neck and shoulders and the top), the necklace, the ears (or the fantasy ears), the head, the lines of an older forehead, the brows, the eyes (and the extra ones), the nose, the beard, the mouth, the moustache, the hair in front, then, for a hood that is up, the hood and the hair that falls out from under it, the horns, the crown, and last the hand with what it holds. Shading is one darker tone of the same colour on a shape of its own.
- **The fantasy is drawn the same way** (`draw/fantasy.ts`, lists in `fantasyLibrary.ts`): filled shapes in the author's colours. A **hood** covers the top of the head and lies over the shoulders, open at the face and down the front; it is made large enough for the hair under it, and the hair that hangs (the curtains, the braids, the pigtails) is drawn again over the hood (the `fall` pass of the hair), while the cap, the parting and the fringe stay under it and a bun above the head is not drawn. A **held item** comes with a sleeve and a hand raised at the shoulder on the side that was chosen (a lantern hangs from a hand held higher); horns are made smaller when they would rise out of the tile. An **extra eye** is a small copy of the eye (no crease, bags or lines); one eye is a larger one in the middle with a single brow over it. A glowing eye has a halo and no dark pupil; a solid one fills the whole eye with its colour.
- **The crown is a superellipse**, rounder than an egg. The hair is made from the length, the texture (a wavy, ringleted or coiled edge; tighter textures look shorter and fuller), the style, the front, the hairline and the edges. The fall of hair starts at the hairline at the temple, on the edge of the cap, with no tab at the join, and covers the ears (however far they stick out) wherever it lies in front; the ends of a fall turn in toward the neck or out over their last third. **Curls and coils are not all alike**: the spaces between them, their height and the lean of each vary (from a fixed series, so the same portrait is drawn the same every time), and the two sides are not the same. Hair that hangs is as thick at the crown as it is wide at the length: the cap over the skull, the curtains beside the face and the mass behind the head share one thickness (`hangW` in `draw/hair.ts`, growing with the length and the texture), so a long fall of hair never sits under a buzz-cut cap. Hair past the chest runs off the tile; the exact length is stored and said in the Appearance words.
- **Stored as choices:** ids as text ("none" is an id), colours as `#rrggbb` (a few may be none), the sliders as numbers in their ranges. An id the app does not know is kept and drawn as the default for that part (an unknown age draws an adult), so a portrait from a newer build still draws.
- **Crops:** the card, the head, the top of the head (horns and crowns), the face and one part (ears, eyes, brows, nose, mouth, lower face, the neck for a necklace) are squares of the tile; the dropdowns' pictures, the small heads on a scene and the cast cards use them. Several portraits on a page each have a clip path of their own.
- **The tile's corners are the one rounded shape** in the Writer's otherwise square chrome: they belong to the picture, are drawn in it (radius 16 of 200) and nowhere else.

## The mannequin (a character's figure)

A character may be drawn from a **figure** instead of a portrait: a frozen copy of a published model (designed in Configuration, Characters; see CharacterModels) and the writer's own choices on it. The two live side by side on the character: the figure is drawn when the character has one and its model copy is here, else the portrait (so a figure whose copy is missing, or has not loaded yet, shows the portrait and nothing breaks).

- **One component draws a character** (`CharacterArt`): the cast list's cards, the overview, Compare and Age, the small heads on a scene (Outline cards, Draft chips) and in a story's states, the Portrait section's big card, and a reader's card. A part crop of the portrait (a dropdown's picture) is always the portrait. The figure is drawn by the same engine as the designer's previews, in the model's own shapes, on the card colour of the figure (`tile`); there is no outline and no image.
- **Crops** are the model's frames: the whole figure for a card (the same box for every figure of a model, so cards line up), the smallest frame with Face or Head in its name for a small head (else the top of the figure), and a square tile around either. A frame leaves out what the model says it hides (a face frame has no shirt).
- **The copy is the character's own.** The first use of a published model keeps a frozen copy of it once in the project; every character that uses that model version points at the same copy, and publishing the model again never changes it. Characters carry only their choices, so the cast list stays light. A character file carries its copies and importing keeps them; a copy that cannot be kept (too big, or the project holds forty) leaves that figure out and says so, and the character keeps its portrait.
- **Making one** (Portrait section, the Mannequin panel under the big card): choose a published model and, if it has genres, the genre of the first look, then the icon button *Make a mannequin*; the look is rolled within the genre (a form on every axis, a colour for every slot, a value for each slider the model shows to writers within the span it allows, and now and then an age, a build or an expression). *Roll a new look* does it again within the genre; *Go back to the portrait* puts the figure away (the portrait is kept all the time). Randomise never takes clothes off, never changes the palette or the card colour, and changes an axis only as often as its **chance** says (the basic model's beard and moustache, 15 percent; its hair cuts, never: a hairstyle chooses them), leaving the presets that set such a form to the writer. A character that has a figure (and its copy) is edited on **its figure's own card** (below) instead of this page of sections; one whose copy has not arrived keeps the page, which draws its portrait and says where the mannequin stands. **No model is published yet?** The panel says so and offers *Add the basic model*: the basic adult that comes with Scriblr (a bare body with a face and hair, the nine ages, four builds, thirteen expressions, fourteen hairstyles and fourteen facial hairs) is added and published by the server. A fresh install already has it.
- **Age** shows a character at another age from the model's own presets of the kind Age (a model with none says so); a story state captures the figure's choices; Compare tells how two figures differ in the model's own words (`Age: Adult > Elder`); the Appearance buttons write the empty fields from the mannequin (the build, the colour of skin, hair and eyes, the forms that are not the model's own) when the character has one.

### The figure's editor (phases 6b-2 and 6b-3)

The Portrait section of a character with a figure is the **editor** (`.charFigEdit`, in a container of its own: `.charFigWrap`). The controls sit **under the card**, one step at a time, and **beside it** (the card staying in view, the controls under the toolbar) once the editor is 660px wide. Only the card is clicked to move between steps: there are no lists of groups or objects.

- **The toolbar** (`role="toolbar"`, *View and frame*): *Front view* and *Back view* (icon buttons, `aria-pressed`), a chip for *Whole figure* and one for each of the model's **frames** (the card looks through the frame and takes its shape), and *Randomise*: a *Genre to roll within* dropdown (when the model has genres) and the dice *Roll a new look*; once something is locked, a count (*3 locked*) with *Unlock all*. Undo and redo are the page's own, in the Save cluster, and every change is one step (a slider or a colour moved several times in a row is one).
- **The card** (`FigureCard`): the figure on its tile, drawn by the same code as every other place. The model's frames lie on it as **sheets**: card-wide buttons nested like paper, the smallest in front, **invisible until the pointer or the keyboard is on one** (then a ring and the sheet's name). In the front view the sheets lie behind the figure, in the back view in front of it. A part goes to the **smallest frame that holds the middle of it**; what no frame holds goes on a sheet of the whole figure, and a model with no frames has just that one. Once a sheet is open, a box (faint, strong under the pointer or for the open part) lies over **each part of it that can be changed** (shown, in a layer that is on, with something the writer may change), in the order the parts are drawn so the one in front is in front. Pointing at a part names it and says what can change (*Shirt: Shirt fit, Style, Stripes, Clothing colour, Worn or not*); the same words are its button's label.
- **Step 0, the figure**: one dropdown for each kind of preset the model has that is not a part's own (Pose, Expression, Build, Outfit, Age, Hand...), at the one that is on (*Custom* when the look is none of them); the sliders no part holds; the fields of a variant that is on; the character's own **Palette** and **Card colour**, and the **Mannequin** note (which model and version it is drawn from) with *Use another model* and *Go back to the portrait*.
  - **Palette**: the swatches this character's colours are taken from (the model's starter palette until the first change, then the character's own). Each has a name and *hue* and *saturation* sliders (no typed numbers); changing one changes every colour that uses it. *Add a swatch* (up to ten) and *Delete* (the colours that used it go gray; the last one stays) are icon buttons.
  - **Card colour**: a row of swatches and a picker; the editor never changes it on its own (Randomise leaves the palette and the card colour alone).
  - **Use another model**: a dropdown of every published model, the one in use marked *(in use)* and a newer version of it *(newer version)*, and the button *Use this model*. It copies the model into the project (a copy that is there already is used) and carries over what the figure has chosen that the new model has too: the same ids, inside the span the new model allows. What the new model lacks is left behind without a note and without a question; Undo brings the old model back. The portrait is untouched.
- **Step 1, a sheet**: where you are (*Figure › Head to hands*) with *Back*, then each **group** on the sheet that has something to change (a twin follows its original and has none): its poses and styles as dropdowns (a *None* puts the kind back to rest), its **states** (a switch such as Arm: Down or Raised) as dropdowns, its sliders and its fields, and a **dice for just that group**. Below, the **clothing** and the **hair** on the sheet as lists, the part in front first: a garment has a *worn* switch (a garment that is off has no box on the card, so this is how it is put on again), a hair part is just named; each has *Move in front* and *Move behind* arrows and a button *Fit and colour* (*Shape and colour* for hair) that opens it. An arrow is off, with the reason as its title, when the part is already at that end or the model keeps it in order (*Undershirt is worn under Shirt: the model keeps it there*): a writer cannot put a shirt over a jacket the model says it is worn under. When the sheet has more than one layer, chips *Pick from Body, Clothing, Hair* leave a layer out of what the card points at, to reach what is under it. *Lock all here* (a switch) locks every part of the sheet for Randomise, and is off as soon as one of them is let go.
- **Step 2, a part**: the part's name, a **lock** (icon button, `aria-pressed`; a locked part has a small lock on its box on the card) and a dice for just it; for a twin, a note that it mirrors its original; *Worn* for a garment; the **family** it belongs to as a dropdown of its members (choosing one opens that member); its **styles** (the model's and the character's own, which carry *(mine)*) and one dropdown for each **form axis** (*Head shape*, *Lids*); *Where it falls* for what hangs; its sliders and **fields**, each held to the span the writer may move it in and shown only if the model shows it to writers (a field of a variant or style only while that is on); a switch for each **fill pattern**; and a **colour block** for each slot the part is painted from: the default colours, the character's palette, *brightness* inside the slot's range and *opacity* when the slot has a range. A part of a colour the model **locks** is said so (*The model fixes this colour's saturation and lightness*) with its slider off; a colour with every part locked cannot be changed. A slider that needs a colour chosen first is off until there is one.
- **My styles**: under a part's styles, a name field (*My style 1, 2...* when left empty) and the icon button *Save as my style* keep what is chosen for that part (its forms, sliders and fields, and what is hidden in it) as a style of this character, listed in the same dropdown as the model's. A style that keeps nothing (the part is as the model has it) is refused with a note. While one of them is the style that is on, it can be renamed and deleted from there. A style does not keep colours: the colour slots are shared with the rest of the figure. At most 40 per character.
- **Locks and the order of layers are saved on the figure**: `locks` (object ids; a twin has its original's) and `zoff` (how many places a part has been moved from the model's own order). Randomise, both the whole figure's and a part's, leaves locked parts as they are.
- **Randomise this part only** rolls what that step controls and nothing else: a form on each axis and the member of the family, the colour of the slots only the part is painted from, a value for each slider the writer may move that is listed under it (from its own random span, else the span the writer may move it in), and, now and then, one of its poses or styles that only moves sliders and forms. It stays within the figure's genre, and leaves alone what the figure locks.
- **A style that only holds a colour lock** (it sets nothing else) is kept as *on* on the figure (`on`), and the order a style gave the shapes of its object as `order`, so a style chosen on a figure keeps working; the figure draws the model's colour locks (an object's own, and those of the styles that are on).
- What the view, the frame and where you are are is the editor's own and is not saved; what is chosen is saved on the figure.

- **A card follows the figure's height.** The crops a card looks through (the bust, the face, every frame) are fixed in the model's space, but a model's *Height* slider scales the whole figure about its feet, so a crop is moved and sized with the same scale: a child's bust card is framed on the child's bust (`figureState.ts#followHeight`; the editor's sheets and hotspots use the same box). The whole-figure card keeps the rest box, so cards of one model still line up and a child stands shorter.

## Appearance (the words)

A character is described in **words** as well. The **Appearance** section (`AppearanceSection.tsx`, `describe.ts`) is where a character opens: the **name**, a paragraph ("In a paragraph") and a field for each thing a stranger notices: **height, build, skin, hair, eyes, face, voice, bearing and mannerisms, how they dress and distinguishing marks** (kept in the profile as `appearance`, `height`, `build`, `skin`, `hair`, `eyes`, `face`, `voice`, `bearing`, `style`, `marks`; the empty fields show an example as a placeholder). Two icon buttons write a first draft in words from the **portrait** (colours named as an author would: "brown skin", "chest-length curly black hair, in two braids", "green eyes"): **Write the empty fields from the portrait** (only fields the portrait has words for; what is written stays) and **Write every field again from the portrait** (asks first, with a check and a close). The portrait has nothing to say about a height, a voice or marks, so those stay the author's. Every keystroke goes into the character; typing in one field is one undo step and the draft is one step.

- Every field can be shown to readers, one by one, from **For readers** (they are among its public fields, each with its own reveal chapter); the character sheet PNG prints the appearance (the paragraph, or the pieces joined) under "In a sentence"; the cast overview and the comparison show the words under the portrait and say how the written height, build, skin, hair and eyes differ.

## Profile, relationships and story

The last three sections replace the portrait with a sheet (`.charSheet`, a grid of panels) beside the cast list. Every keystroke goes into the character, and typing in one field is one undo step.

- **Profile**: identity (role, age, occupation, other names, a sentence), personality and psychology (personality, what they want, why, what they fear, their flaw and strength), background, **fields of your own** (a label and a text, up to 30) and **possessions** (a name and a line each). The role and the age are the line under the portrait card.
- **Relationships**: typed links to the other characters (family, friend, ally, rival, enemy, romance, mentor, other) with a label and a description; a family link says who the other is (parent, child, spouse, sibling). Each link keeps **changes over the story** (a new label and note from a chapter or scene on), and the list can be read **as it stands at** a point. A character can see who has a link to them. The **web** draws the whole cast in a ring, a line for each link; the kinds differ by the **dash** of the line, not by colour, with a key. The **family tree** of the open character puts parents above, children below, siblings and spouses beside, from the family links of either character.
- **Story**: **states** (what is true of the character from a chapter or scene until the next state: mood, goal, where, what they carry, notes, story time and, if captured, a copy of their portrait, shown as a small head beside the state with "Capture the portrait now / again" and "Forget the portrait"); a state **carries forward**, and "What is true at" looks one up. **How they look, point to point** lists what changed between states that captured the portrait ("Hair style: Plain > Two-strand twists"); a state that only has an older look is ignored. **Arc** (stages, each placed on a chapter or scene or not yet) and a **life timeline** in story time.
- The chapters and scenes come from the project's outline in book order. A relationship is only with another character that is there; deleting a character removes the links to them.

## The cast, files, the sheet and who is in a scene

- **The cast** (the team icon): an **overview** of every character as a portrait card (the name, and the role and age); **Compare** two characters side by side (two portrait cards, the words, and what differs in how they look and in role, age and occupation); and **Show them at another age**: a picture dropdown over the nine ages draws the open character at each, beside the portrait as it is. The age moves, the hair, brows and beard grey for an older one, and a child has no beard. The result is a preview until **Change to this** (one edit to save) or **Make a copy at this age**.
- **Where they appear**: the Story section lists the chapters and scenes the character is tagged on (kept on the character). In the **Outline**, a scene card shows the small heads and names of the characters tagged on it, with a select to tag another and a x to take one off (saved at once with the character); in the **Draft**, the scene's chips show the same, read only (`.charTags`).
- **Files**: each row of the list has a save-to-file button, and the list one for the whole cast, writing `*.scriblr-characters.json` (the portrait goes with each character; a file from before portraits gets the default one and keeps its old look); the import button reads one and asks, for each character the project has under the same id, whether to **keep both**, **replace the one here** or **leave out**. Links to characters that are not in the file are dropped, and the tags on the outline are left out (they belong to the project).
- **The character sheet**: the image button in the bar saves a **PNG** of the open character: the name and role and the profile **in words**, on white paper. It has no picture (the portrait is for the console, the cards and the book).
- **Links**: the Story section ends with "Linked to": the plot nodes and places this character is linked to, and a picker to add one (see Maps, "Links to other things of the story").
- **For readers** (the export icon): the character's publish switch, the chapter it is revealed at, which fields are public and each field's own chapter, spoiler warnings and a preview as a reader. See Maps, "For readers", which describes the pieces both consoles share; the card a reader sees is the portrait tile, the name and the public fields (`.wvCard`; the role and age are among the fields, so they are only shown when public).

## Saving

As everywhere in the Writer: **autosave is off by default**, so an edit waits for the Save button. The whole character goes in one request. A slider moved many times within a moment is one undo step. Opening another character saves what is waiting first. A failed save rings the Save button and keeps the edit. A character made before portraits opens with the default portrait; its old look stays in `project.json` untouched and is not shown.

## Tokens

Surfaces `surface-side`, `surface-raised`, `ov-sink-1`, `ov-sink-2`, `hairline`; text `ink`, `ink-muted`; `accent` for the chosen choice and the open section; `alert` for a refused save; `hairline` and a shadow for the dropdown's list. Space and type from the scales. No literal colours in the chrome.

## Don't

- Don't put a literal colour in the chrome: only the colours of the character itself (the portrait, its swatches) are data.
- Don't make a button text-only where an icon fits: the sections of the console and of the portrait page are icons with a label and a title.
- Don't store art: store the choices, and draw them.
- Don't copy a model into every character, or follow it when it is published again: a figure points at a frozen copy kept once in the project.
- Don't draw a figure without its copy, or hide a figure that has no copy: draw the portrait.
- Don't list the groups and objects of a figure to pick from: the card is what is clicked (sheets, then parts), and the steps follow.
- Don't offer a slider the model does not show to writers, or one past the span it allows: a field is a slider too, and is held to the same span.
- Don't let a part of a colour the model locks be changed: it is shown, with its slider off, and the figure draws it.
- Don't make a garment that is off disappear from the editor: it keeps its *worn* switch in its sheet's step.
- Don't let a writer move a garment past one the model says it is worn under or over: the arrow is off and its title says why.
- Don't ask before using another model, or list what did not carry over: the copy is the character's, Undo brings the old model back.
- Don't lock a twin on its own: a lock is kept on the part (a twin has its original's), and *Lock all here* is only a shortcut for locking every part of a sheet.
- Don't outline the portrait or make it an image: it is flat filled shapes, drawn by code.
- Don't list a hair style or front the length does not allow.
- Don't let the settings clip: the page of sections scrolls beside the card, and a long section must never be cut off.
- Don't draw curls in a row: no two are alike.
- Don't let a hood cover the hair that hangs, or let a horn, a staff or a flame run out of the tile: they are made to fit it.
- Don't randomise the fantasy without being asked: it is locked until the user unlocks it.
- Don't ask for the colour of something the portrait does not have.
- Don't draw a sheet or a part's box in the ink alone: it lies on the character's own colour, so it is an accent line on an edge of `on-accent` (a ring that shows on any tile).
- Don't write on a character's colour in the theme's ink: the tile can be any colour, so the name and icons of a card use `readableInk` (4.5 to 1 for text, 3 to 1 for icons, tested over the whole colour space), and the card is marked with a ring in that ink, not an overlay or the accent.
