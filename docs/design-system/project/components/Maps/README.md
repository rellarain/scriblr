# Maps (the setting builder)

The **Maps** console is where the book's setting is mapped. It is a tab of the Writer's **Project** level (after Project, before History), with the folded-map icon. A map is one of **nine levels**, from intergalactic down to one room; its land is a **base** (a biome and a height under everything) with **biome areas** drawn over it as shapes, coloured by biome and shaded by height; the grid stays to show the scale. Maps nest (a town inside a continent), and each has layers.

Code: `writer/maps/` (`MapsConsole.tsx`, `MapList.tsx`, `MapCanvas.tsx`, `MapSide.tsx`, `MapFields.tsx`, `useMaps.ts`, `mapModel.ts`, `mapLevels.ts`, `mapUnits.ts`, `mapBiomes.ts`, `mapHeights.ts`, `mapItems.ts`, `mapOutline.ts`, `mapTemplates.ts`, `mapLibrary.ts`, `mapCombine.ts`, `mapOrbits.ts`, `mapFloors.ts`, `mapOpenings.ts`, `FloorBar.tsx`, `mapShapeSeed.ts`, `mapShapeStore.ts`, `mapMarkerArt.ts`, `ShapesPanel.tsx`, `mapThemes.ts`, `mapView.ts`, `mapRender.ts`, `mapDrawItems.ts`, `mapShapes.ts`, `mapDrawShapes.ts`, `mapExport.ts`, `mapFile.ts`, `placesStore.ts`, `PlacePicker.tsx`, `ForReadersPanel.tsx`, `mapVersions.ts`, `mapJourneys.ts`, `mapDrawJourneys.ts`, `JourneysPanel.tsx`, `VersionBar.tsx`, `maps.scss`). Storage: the project's `maps` section (`project.json`), one entry per map, and the project's own biomes (`mapBiomes`); the API is `/api/projects/{id}/maps` and `/api/projects/{id}/map-biomes`; the shape libraries are `/api/map-shapes` (the admin's default shapes) and `/api/my-map-shapes` (My shapes, kept in the app for every project). The admin's biome list and default shapes are set on [Map biomes and shapes](../MapBiomes/README.md).

## Anatomy

`.mapPage` holds a bar (the title, the **tools**, the **Save cluster**) and `.mapSplit`: the **list** (`.mapList`, 220px), the **stage** (`.mapStage`: the canvas and a status line) and the **side** (`.mapSide`, 280px: the chosen thing, Biomes, Shapes, Lines and regions, Doors and windows (buildings), Layers, Journeys, Map, Image, For readers). Below 860px of its own width the parts stack. The console sits on the tab's surface in the ink colour.

- **Tools** (icon buttons, `aria-pressed`): **Select and move** (the pointer), **Draw a biome area** (an island with a hill), **Place a shape** (the stamp), **Draw an orbit** (a star with a path round it; on space maps only), **Put a door or window in a wall** (a door in its swing; on architectural and interior maps only), **Place a label**, **Draw a line**, **Draw a region**, **Measure a distance** (the ruler), **Add a stop to a journey** (the person with a plus) and **Move the view** (the hand); then the **grid** toggle and, on planetary, continental, national and regional maps, the **view switch** (chips **Biomes** | **Nations**, `aria-pressed`; remembered on this computer for each map, not saved in it). There is no brush, fill or dropper: the land is drawn with shapes. The middle or right mouse button pans with any tool; the wheel zooms at the pointer; the view buttons at the canvas corner zoom in and out and **fit** the map. The status line says the biome and the height under the pointer ("Forest, 1,250 m"), the height read from the worked-out heights (so a hill's slope reads lower at its foot); maps of space and buildings show the biome only.
- **List**: each map with the maps inside it under it (indented), its **level**, and a delete that asks first (the maps inside move up). The plus opens the **New map** form: title, **level** (which fills the size of a cell, the unit, the base and the size from the level's defaults, table below), the **unit system** (Metric, Imperial, or Custom for paces, leagues and the like), a size (Small 32×24, Medium 64×48, Large 128×96, Huge 256×192, Largest 512×512, or any width and height up to 512), what one cell is (a number and a unit from the system's list; free text when custom), and the map it is inside.
- **Biomes** (`.mapTerrains`; "Floors" on architectural and interior maps): first the **Base** (the biome under everything, and on land levels its height in metres or feet), then the biomes the map's level offers as swatch buttons ("Draw with Forest", `aria-pressed`; the count of areas using each), the admin's list first and then the project's own ("(this project)"). Choosing one picks the **Draw a biome area** tool. The plus **adds a biome of this project** (a name and a colour, at most 64; saved at once, outside a map's undo, since it belongs to every map of the project); "Change a biome of this project…" opens one to rename, recolour or remove (asks first: maps that use it keep its colour, it is no longer offered). **Curve the points of new areas** makes new areas smooth. On land levels, **New areas** sets what a new area does to the height (the same choices as a chosen area, below), its amount, its profile and its slope, so a run of hills can be drawn one after another. The admin's biomes and colours are set in Configuration > Maps.
- **Layers**: biomes, regions and lines, markers, labels, notes (bottom to top; listed top first, and drawn in that order, so a markers layer above the labels draws over them). Each has **show / hide**, **lock**, a name, **move up / down** and delete (never the biomes layer; deleting a layer takes what is on it, and an undo brings it back). A biome area is drawn on the biomes layer while it is shown and not locked; a marker goes on the top shown, unlocked markers layer, and a label on the top shown, unlocked labels layer; the console says so when there is none. A line or region goes on the top shown, unlocked layer of regions and lines (a map with none puts them on the markers layer).
- **Map**: title, **level** (a select: "Planetary: one planet and its continents"...), width and height (a new size keeps every shape where it is), the **unit system** (a change between metric and imperial asks: **Convert the numbers**, which turns the size of a cell, a day's travel and every height into the new units, or **Keep the numbers**, which only renames the units; Cancel puts it back), the size of a cell and its unit, on land levels the **height range** (the height drawn lightest, and the same depth darkest; a stepper in metres or feet that starts at the level's, with a restore button that goes back to it), and the check boxes for what is drawn over the map: **Scale bar**, **Compass**, **Legend** and, on land levels, **Contour lines** and **Relief**. There is no look any more.
- **Shapes** (`ShapesPanel.tsx`): on land levels, **Templates make** (chips **Biome areas** | **Regions**: what a template is made as; maps of space and buildings make regions); the **Templates** (chips **Circle**, **Ellipse**, **Rectangle**, **Polygon** with a **Sides** stepper of 3 to 12, **Blob**, **Ridge**); the **kinds of shape** the map's level offers (chips **Land forms**, **Space objects**, **Building parts**, **Markers**) and, for the kind, a grid of square buttons, each the shape's own outline in the ink colour ("Place Castle", `aria-pressed` on the one that is chosen); then **My shapes**, grouped by the project each came from, each with a delete that asks first (it leaves My shapes, for every project). Picking any of them picks the **Place a shape** tool.
- **Chosen thing** (top of the side, only while something is chosen): a **biome area** has its **biome** (a select of the level's biomes; *None* keeps the biome under it and only changes the height), **what it does to the height** (*Keeps the height*, *Raises the land*, *Lowers the land*, *Sets the height*) and the amount in metres or feet, its **profile** (chips **Plateau**, **Hill**, **Peak**, **Basin**, `aria-pressed`) and its **slope** in cells (a stepper, 0 to 64 by halves; none for a peak), **Bring to front** and **Send to back** (later areas lie over earlier ones), **Curve all points** / **Straighten all points**, its place fields and a delete; a **region** has its line and fill fields and **Political area** (a select: *No: an ordinary region*, *A nation*, *A state or province*, *A municipality*); a **marker** has its width in cells (0.25 to 16), its **colour** (a button takes it back to the ink), its place fields and a delete; biome areas, regions, lines and markers also have the **shape tools** (below); a **label** has its **text** (it takes the cursor when it has just been placed), its height in cells and a delete. Drag it with the Select tool, or move it with the arrow keys (a cell; Shift a quarter), Delete removes it, Escape lets go.
- **Lines and regions** (the pen): a row of chips for the kinds of **line** and one for the kinds of **region** the map's level offers (land: River, Road, Trail, Border, Wall, Fence and Nation, State or province, Municipality, Territory, Water, Forest, Danger; space: Border, **Route** and Danger, **Nebula**, Territory); a chip sets the pen and picks the Line or Region tool. Below, the fields of the pen: a line's **style** (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Double road, Wall, Fence), **width** in cells and **colour** (a button takes it back to the ink of the map), for a region its **fill** colour, how **solid** it is (percent) and its **pattern** (Solid, Hatched, Cross-hatched, Dotted, **Glow**: a soft light inside its edge), and **Curve the points** (new points are smooth, or corners).
- **Chosen line or region** (top of the side): the same fields for the one that is chosen, **Curve all points** and **Straighten all points**, and a delete.
- **Shape tools** (in the chosen biome area, region, line or marker): **Rough edge** (a stepper, 0 to 100 by fives: how rough its edge is drawn), **Combine with another shape** (chips **Merge with…**, **Cut out…**, **Split along…**, `aria-pressed`: pick the other shape on the map, Escape stops; a refusal says why, such as "The two shapes do not overlap.") and **Save to My shapes** (a name, a kind, and a check button: the shape is kept in My shapes for every project, with this project's title, and offered on the levels of this map's group).

## Levels

| Level | Holds | Drawn as | Base | Cell, metric / imperial | Height range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intergalactic | star systems | space | space | 1 ly | none |
| Galactic | one star system | space | space | 0.1 AU | none |
| Planetary | continents | biomes | saltwater, −3,000 m | 100 km / 60 mi | ±8,000 m |
| Continental | nations | biomes | saltwater, −200 m | 25 km / 15 mi | ±6,000 m |
| National | states, provinces | biomes | plain, 0 | 10 km / 6 mi | ±4,000 m |
| Regional | biomes, municipalities | biomes | plain, 0 | 2 km / 1 mi | ±2,000 m |
| Local | buildings | biomes | plain, 0 | 10 m / 30 ft | ±300 m |
| Architectural | floors, rooms | floor materials | outside | 1 m / 3 ft | none |
| Interior | one room | floor materials | wood | 0.5 m / 1 ft | none |

A place's map is the **next level down** by default; any lower level can be picked beside the folder-plus button. Maps from before levels were given one: world → planetary, town → local, interior → interior. (`mapLevels.ts`; the backend's `MAP_LEVEL_DEFAULTS` holds the same table, and a test keeps them equal.)

## The canvas

The **base** fills the map in its biome's colour; then the **biome areas** of the biomes layer, in their order (later on top), each a filled outline with crisp edges at any zoom; then the layers above in their order; the grid shows once a cell is 6 pixels or more. **Height** is shading: a biome's colour is how it looks at sea level, and a higher area is drawn **lighter** (a white wash), a lower one **darker** (a black wash), at most 55% at the map's height range (the level's, table above). The height an area gives the land: *Sets* is its own height; *Raises* and *Lowers* add or take away from what is under it; *Keeps* is what is under it. **Land never sits under the sea**: a land biome over the sea base starts from sea level, so an island is at 0 and a forest on a mountain keeps the mountain's height (green, but lighter). An area with no biome only changes the height of what is under it (a hill under a forest).

Heights are worked out **point by point** (`mapHeights.ts`, pure and tested in node): a grid of samples (up to four a cell, about a million at most) starts at the base's height, and each biome area, in its order, changes the samples inside its outline by its **profile**: a **plateau** is flat, with a ramp as wide as its slope (none: a step at its edge); a **hill** rises smoothly over its slope (0: to its middle); a **peak** rises straight to its middle; a **basin** is steep at its edge and flat in the middle (a bowl when it lowers the land). How far in a sample is comes from a distance transform of the outline. The heights give a **shading image** drawn over the land, stretched and smoothed (white where higher than sea level, black where lower, as strong as the height up to 55% at the range); **Relief** adds a hillshade to it, lighter on slopes that face the upper left and darker on those that face away; **Contour lines** are drawn in the ink at low alpha every round step (1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, about a quarter of the range), every fifth and sea level a little stronger. The heights, the image and the contours are cached for the map until its land, base or range changes, so hovering stays cheap. The **legend** ends with a **height scale**: a ramp from the darkest depth to the lightest height with three labels (the depth, 0 and the height with its unit).

**Political areas** are regions marked as a nation, a state or province, or a municipality (the region presets **Nation**, **State or province** and **Municipality** make them, with a solid, dashed or dotted border). In the **Biomes** view they are a border and a name over the land, with no fill, and they are picked by their border, so the land inside stays clickable. In the **Nations** view the land is a plain pale paper and the water a pale blue, unshaded, and each political area is filled in its own colour (its fill, else a colour from its name): nations strongest, states and municipalities lighter. A biome the map cannot find is drawn in neutral gray and named "Unknown biome". **Space** levels are drawn light on the dark backdrop (`mapThemes.ts`: the land style and the space style, ink, halo, plate and typeface); everything drawn over the land (symbols, text, grid, plates) is in those colours. The biome colours and the two styles are the map's art, set in data and code, and the only colours that are not tokens. A **named area** is a place, and its name is written at its middle over a halo when the area is at least 40 pixels across. **Markers** (a castle, a tree, a door) are region shapes with the line style **halo**: filled in their own colour or the ink over a ring in the halo colour, a dot when under 4 pixels across, and a named marker has its name under it; **labels** are drawn over the same kind of halo, and left out when they would be under 6 pixels tall. Labels and markers placed with a click are centred on the middle of a cell (Alt while placing or dragging: free), so they keep their place when the view zooms. The **scale bar** (a round length, 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, of about 110 pixels), the **compass** (north is up) and the **legend** (the biomes that are used, in the list's order, and the kinds of marker on the map, each drawn small) sit in corners of the map on a plate, and are left out when the map is too small on the canvas to hold them. The frame round the map takes the **ink colour** of the page. **Lines and regions** (`mapShapes.ts`, `mapDrawShapes.ts`) are lists of points in cells; a region closes on its first point. A corner is a straight piece; a **smooth** point is a curve through it, a cubic Bezier whose handle is the one its two neighbours make (so a smooth line is a good curve untouched), or one that was dragged. The six line styles are strokes of the one path: dashed and dotted by dash pattern, a **double road** as two edges with the road colour between them, a **wall** as a solid line with crenels, a **fence** as a thin rail with cross ticks. A region is filled at its opacity, with hatching, cross-hatching or dots clipped to its outline, over its outline in a line style. Drawing: click to add points (they **snap to grid corners and cell centres**, Alt: no snapping), double-click or Enter finishes, Escape cancels, Backspace takes the last point back; a line needs two points, a region or a biome area three (a biome area being drawn shows its fill in the biome's colour under a dashed outline). The chosen shape shows its points as squares and its handles as circles (a marker's only once it is 160 pixels or more across: its points lie close, so at a smaller size a drag moves the whole marker; zoom in to reshape one); drag any, or the body (half a cell at a time); double-click a point to curve or straighten it, double-click the line to put a point in; with a point chosen Delete takes it away (never below the fewest). A drag or a change is **one undo step**; the Save cluster's Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) work per map and are kept while the project is open. The canvas is `role="img"` with the map's name and size.

## Shapes: templates, libraries, combining, rough edges and holes

- **Placing** (the Place a shape tool): **drag a box** and the shape fills it (a library shape keeps its proportions, centred in the box; Shift makes the box a square; it is previewed while dragging), or **click** to place it at its own size (a template: four cells; a library shape: the size it was saved at), at the middle of the cell (Alt: where the pointer is). The new shape is chosen, so it can be edited point by point like any other; Escape drops a box being dragged.
- **Templates** (`mapTemplates.ts`, pure): a circle (eight smooth points in the box's smaller side), an ellipse, a rectangle (four corners), a polygon of 3 to 12 corners, a **blob** (nine smooth points, seeded, so no two are alike) and a **ridge** (a crest of peaks along the box's longer side over a smooth foot, coming to a point at each end). As a biome area a template takes the chosen biome and what new areas do to the height; as a region, the region pen.
- **Libraries** (`mapLibrary.ts`, `mapShapeStore.ts`): a library shape is a biome area, region or line kept in a box of 1 by 1 with its own size in cells, its biome, height settings (in metres; feet on an imperial map once placed), roughness and holes, in one of four kinds, offered on the levels it names (none: every level). The **default shapes** are the admin's (Configuration > Maps > Shapes, published as a whole); a new install starts with the starter set: land forms (island, continent, archipelago, lake, mountain range, ridge, valley, delta, plateau), space objects (star, planet, ring, asteroid belt, nebula), building parts (rectangular, L-shaped and round rooms, tower, stair, table, bed) and the **markers** (every old symbol, from its SVG path, plus "Place"). **My shapes** are a writer's own, kept in the app for every project and grouped by the project each came from. A placed shape keeps the library shape's id as its `kind` (so the legend can name a marker) and is a plain shape of the map from then on: a later change to the library never changes it.
- **Combining** (`mapCombine.ts`, polygon boolean operations by the `polygon-clipping` library on the drawn outlines, then simplified and given smooth points again if most of the old ones were): **Merge** joins two biome areas, or two regions (not one of each), into the chosen one, and the other goes; two that do not touch become one shape of two pieces. **Cut out** takes any closed shape out of the chosen one: inside it, it leaves a hole; over the edge, a bite; the cutter stays. **Split along** a line drawn right across the chosen shape makes a shape of each piece (the first keeps the id, name and place; the line stays). Each is one undo step; the result loses its rough edge (its outline is new).
- **Rough edges** (`mapOutline.ts`, pure): a shape's `rough` (0 to 100) and `seed` wobble its outline by a few octaves of seeded noise along the edge's normal, sized to the shape, at draw time only (the points stay as they are). The fill, the heights and the picking all use the same wobbled outline, so what is drawn is what is clicked and what is raised; a line keeps its ends where they are.
- **Holes**: a biome area or region can have more rings than its outline (`holes`), filled even-odd: a ring inside it is a hole, a ring apart from it is another piece. Holes come from Cut out, a merge of shapes apart, and markers (a castle's gate). A click in a hole picks what is under it, and a hole gives the land no height.

## Space

Intergalactic and galactic maps are drawn **light on dark**: the base is **Space** (dark), with a backdrop of faint **stars** (seeded by the map, so the same each time; about one every two cells, a few brighter), and everything over it in the space style's light ink.

- **Kinds of star and planet** are entries of the biome list (groups *Kind of star* and *Kind of planet*, set on Configuration > Maps like the biomes): stars Yellow star, Red dwarf, Blue giant, White dwarf, Neutron star and Black hole (intergalactic and galactic); planets Rocky planet, Ocean world, Gas giant, Ice world, Lava world, Desert world and Garden world (galactic). A star, a planet or a moon is a **biome area** whose biome is its kind (*Kind* in its panel, which has no height on space maps), so it takes the kind's colour; a **star glows** in its colour (a dark one, a black hole, in a warm light).
- **Space objects** (the Shapes panel): Star, Planet, Moon, Star system (a star in a ring, for intergalactic maps), Station, Comet, Asteroid, Ring, Asteroid belt and Nebula. A named star system opens a galactic map, a named planet a planetary one (the next level down).
- **Orbits** (`mapOrbits.ts`, pure; the **Draw an orbit** tool): press on a star and drag out (a click: 4 cells round; the radius snaps to half cells, Alt: as dragged); pressed on a body, the orbit goes round its middle. An orbit is a shape of its own type: an ellipse (a centre, a radius across and down, a turn), drawn as a dashed path, never filled, picked by its path. **A planet dropped near the path** (its middle within 0.6 cell, or half its size; and smaller than the orbit) **goes round on it**: its middle is put on the path and the orbit and angle are kept on it. Dragged, it **keeps to the orbit** (to the point nearest the pointer); a new orbit catches the bodies already on its path. **Chosen orbit**: Across, Down (cells, 0.5 to 512) and Turn (degrees, -180 to 180) steppers, its line style, width and colour, and how many bodies go round on it; moving, growing or turning it takes them along, each at its angle; deleting it lets them go where they are. A body on an orbit has **Leave the orbit** in its panel.
- **Regions and lines** in space: nebulae and territories are regions with the **Glow** pattern; routes are dashed lines.

## Buildings

Architectural maps (one building) and interior maps (one room) are drawn in **floor materials** (wood, stone, tile, marble, carpet, dirt, paving, grass, outside; the Biomes panel is called Floors there), with no height shading.

- **Floors** (`mapFloors.ts`, `FloorBar.tsx`): a floor is a map inside a building's map, with its **place** among the floors (-3 to 20: 0 is the ground floor, below it the basements) and an optional name ("Ground floor", "Floor 2", "Basement 1" when it has none). The building map and each of its floors show a **floor bar** above the canvas (`role="group"`, "Floors of The Lantern Inn"): the building, then its floors lowest first, as chips (`aria-pressed` on the open one; the open floor's name as typed), and **Add a floor** (the plus): a map inside the building, the same size and scale, one floor above the highest (the ground floor for the first), which opens. A floor's Map panel has its **Floor** (a stepper) and its **Name**; the list names a floor by its place. A **room** (a named region or area on a floor) opens an interior map, the next level down.
- **Walls on the cell edges**: on a building a line drawn with the **Wall** pen snaps to grid corners only (not cell middles), its points snap to corners when dragged, and the wall moves by whole cells, so walls run along the grid lines (Alt: free). On architectural and interior maps a wall is drawn as a **plain bar** (square ends, mitred corners), not with the crenels of a town's or a castle's wall.
- **Doors, windows and arches** (`mapOpenings.ts`, pure; the **Doors and windows** panel and the door tool): pick **Door**, **Window** or **Arch** and a **width** (cells, 0.25 to 8), then click on a wall: the opening goes on the piece of the wall nearest the click (from one point to the next), at that place along it, kept whole inside it. The wall is drawn with a **gap** for each opening: a door as its leaf and the arc it swings through (dashed), a window as a thin double line across the gap, an arch as its two jambs. The chosen wall lists its openings (*Door 1*, *Window 2*...): kind, width, where it is along its piece (percent), **Other side** for a door's swing, and a delete. An opening stays on its piece when the wall is moved or a point dragged, and moves to the right piece when a point is put in or taken away. A click with the door tool away from a wall says "Click on a wall...".
- **Furniture and fittings** are building parts in the Shapes panel: rectangular, L-shaped and round rooms, tower, stair, table, bed, sofa, desk, wardrobe, counter, bath and rug, and the interior markers (door, stairs, table, chair, bed, chest, fireplace, pillar).

## Symbols from before markers

Maps once had **symbols**, stamped pictures of a fixed set. When a project is first opened by this build (project version 8, map format 3), each map's symbols become **markers** once (backend `storage/map_markers.py`, for edition maps too and for an old map file when it is imported): each is the starter marker of its kind (an unknown kind: "Place"), in the square the symbol filled, with the same id, layer, colour, name and maps (so scenes, links and reveals keep their place). Symbols layers are now named "Markers". The old symbols stay in the file, unused (`legacySymbols`), and a `.v7.bak` copy of the project is made.

## Painted maps from before levels

Maps were once painted cell by cell. When a project is first opened by this build, every painted map (and every map frozen in a reader edition) is **traced once** into biome areas (backend `storage/map_trace.py`): each connected area of one terrain becomes an outline (grown a little so neighbours overlap, simplified, and smoothed on land levels; local and interior maps keep square corners), the terrain with the most cells becomes the base, tiny specks are merged, and the areas are drawn largest first so a lake lands on its forest. Hills and mountains become raised land; terrains of the author's own become biomes of the project. The old cells stay in the file, unused (a `.v6.bak` copy of the project is made too). A map file from before (version 1 or 2) is traced the same way when it is imported.

## Places, nesting, measuring and files

- **Places**: a marker, a region or a biome area that has a **name** is a **place** (key `mapId/shapeId`; ids are never shared between shapes). The chosen thing's panel has a *Place name* field and, once it is named, **Maps from here** (the maps that open from the place: open one, stop opening it, make a new one inside this map for the place with the folder-plus button, at the level chosen beside it (the next one down by default), or link one that exists) and **Scenes here** (the scenes whose Location is the place). **Double-click** a place with the Select tool to open its first map; the **way up** (`.mapPath`: an up button and the crumbs of the maps above) sits above the canvas. A marker's name is drawn under it; an area's inside it.
- **Scene Location** (Outline and Draft) is a dropdown of the places of the project's maps, shown with their path ("Known World > Port Veyra"); old text is kept until it is converted. The **Old locations** panel (shown while a scene still has text typed before places existed) makes places of the checked texts on the open map and points their scenes at them; "Drop the old texts" asks first and clears the rest.
- **Inside**: the Map panel's *Inside* field moves a map into another (never into itself or a map within it). **A day's travel** is how far a traveller goes in a day, in the map's unit.
- **Measure** (the ruler tool): click to add points to a dashed path, Backspace takes one back, Escape clears; the length is said on a plate at the end and in the status line, with the days it takes when a day's travel is set.
- **Image**: the *Image* panel saves the map as a **PNG** at 1x, 2x or 4x (a cell is 16, 32 or 64 pixels, held to 8192 on the longest side) with the layers that are checked; the grid and the editing marks are left out.
- **Files**: the export button on a map row writes the map **and the maps inside it, with their versions over the story and their journeys,** as `*.scriblr-map.json` (file version 4, with the project's own biomes the maps use, which an import adds to the project's; version 3 files have symbols, which become markers on import; version 2 files have no biomes and painted cells, version 1 files no versions either: both are traced on import); the import button on the list reads one and asks, for each map the project already has under the same id, whether to **keep both**, **replace the one here** or **leave it out**. Links to maps that are not in the file are dropped. A version comes with its map and is made again as a version of the new map (so it is not a row of its own in the import form); a journey of a character the project does not have is left out.
- The **Characters** and **Settings** built-in plot presets no longer appear in the Plot's preset list.

## Versions over the story, and journeys

- **Versions** (`mapVersions.ts`, `VersionBar.tsx`, the copy button of a row in the list): a map can change in the story (a town burns, a bridge falls). **Make a version of** a map copies it as it is now, with a title and the chapter or scene it is **true from**; the version is listed under its map (with "from Chapter 2"), not in the tree of maps inside maps, and its markers and areas are not places (the first map's are, so scenes and links keep one place). A map that has versions, or is one, shows a **bar** above the canvas: the map and its versions as chips, and a picker "The map at…" that opens the version that is true at a chapter or scene (the last whose point is at or before it; an unplaced version is never chosen). A version has a "True from" field instead of "Inside". Deleting a map deletes its versions. Backend: `versionOf`, `appliesFrom`, `POST /maps/{id}/versions`.
- **In the Reader**: a version is published like any map, with its own reveal chapter; the Maps console shows the latest revealed version first with chips for the others. Journeys are never sent to readers.
- **Journeys** (`mapJourneys.ts`, `mapDrawJourneys.ts`, `JourneysPanel.tsx`, the footprint tool): "Follow a character…" gives a character a journey on this map (one each, in a colour of the map's own palette that can be changed); with the **Journey tool**, each click puts a **stop** at the middle of a cell, from the point of the story chosen under "New stops are from"; every stop has its own point, a note, and a button to take it away; with the Journey tool and the journey chosen, a **stop can be dragged** to another place (it snaps to half a cell, Alt moves it freely; one undo step), and a click away from a stop makes a new one. The stops stay in the order of the story (a stop with no point goes last). The canvas draws each path as a line with arrows and a dot at each stop (the chosen journey's dots are numbered), and **"Where everyone is at"** puts a marker with each character's initial at their last stop at or before the chosen point. "Show the paths" hides the lines. Removing a journey asks first.
- Colours of a journey are data (the map's art), the panel's chrome is tokens.

## Links to other things of the story

A plot node, a character and a place can be linked to each other (not to their own kind: plot nodes have their own links, characters their relationships). The pieces are in `writer/links/` (`WorldLinks.tsx`, `linkStore.ts`, `worldNav.ts`, `links.scss`); a link is one record in the project (`worldLinks`, backend `world_links.py`), so it reads the same from both ends.

- **Chips** (`.wlList`): each link is a row with the kind ("Plot", "Character", "Place") and the other end's name (a plot node by its place in the plot, a place by the maps it is inside); the row opens the other end, the x beside it takes the link away from both ends.
- **Picker** (`.wlSelect`, "Link to…"): grouped by kind (Plot nodes, Characters, Places), without what is linked already and without the thing's own kind.
- **Where**: in the Characters console under Story ("Linked to"), in the Maps console under the chosen named place, and under the plot node's Linked list ("Characters and places"). A link to something that is deleted (a character, a place that lost its name) is simply not shown.
- **Opening**: a plot node opens in the Plot; a character or a place opens in its own console when that console is on screen (else when its tab is next opened): the Maps console opens the map and chooses the place.

## For readers

What readers see of the setting and the cast is the author's choice, and it is only ever what the author has published. The shared pieces live in `writer/readers/` (`ReaderControls.tsx`, `WorldViews.tsx`, `readers.scss`); the Reader's two consoles in `reader/WorldConsoles.tsx`.

- **Publish switch** (a checkbox, "Show this map to readers" or "Show Ada to readers"): nothing is shown until it is ticked, and nothing leaves the project until the **book is published**: publishing a book copies the published maps and the public side of the published characters into the new edition, as it copies the chapters, so a later change waits for the next edition. (Backend: `ReaderShare` on the map and the character, `EditionMap` and `EditionCharacter` on `BookEdition`, `world_publishing.py`.)
- **Reveal at a chapter** (`.rvSelect`, "From the start" or "Chapter n: title"): the chapters are the ones of the published edition, so the numbers are the ones readers see. A chapter the edition has not got yet is kept and marked "(not published yet)". When the project has several books, a select names the book whose chapters count.
- **Biomes in editions**: an edition freezes the colours of the biomes its maps use (`BookEdition.biomes`), so an admin's later change to a colour waits for the next edition, as the chapters do.
- **Parts**: a map's marker, label, line, region or area can be revealed later than the map (choose it, then its own "shown from"); a character's fields are ticked one by one (the look is always shown, the rest of the profile never is), each with its own chapter; relationships show only between published characters.
- **Spoiler warnings** (`.rvWarn`, a list named "Spoiler warnings"): a scene that uses a place of a map, or a character, in a chapter before the chapter where it is revealed. Fetched again after each save.
- **Preview as a reader** ("A reader who has reached"): the saved map or character as a reader who has reached a chapter sees it (the preview is of what is saved, and says so).
- **In the Reader**: the Book page gets two consoles, **Maps** (the maps, the ones inside another listed under it, the named places: markers and areas) and **Cast** (a card for each character: close-up, name, public fields, links). A place that has a map of its own, when that map is one the reader can see, is a link that opens it; a relationship on a card jumps to the other character's card (marked with the accent while it is the one jumped to), and a character who is not shown yet is only named. What is still to be revealed is only counted ("2 more maps to discover as you read"), never named. The chapter a reader has reached is where they last stopped (or the whole book once finished).
- Checkboxes use the browser's own, sized 16px (`appearance: auto`: the global input reset hides them otherwise), in the accent colour.

## Saving

As everywhere in the Writer: **autosave is off by default**, so an edit waits for the Save button (or the autosave wait, or leaving the page or the map). The whole map goes in one request. Opening another map saves what is waiting first. A failed save rings the Save button and keeps the edit.

## Tokens

Surfaces `surface-side`, `surface-raised`, `ov-sink-1`, `ov-sink-2`, `hairline`; text `ink`, `ink-muted`; `accent` for the chosen row, tool and biome; `alert` for a refused save and the confirm bars. Space and type from the scales. No literal colours in the chrome.

## Don't

- Don't put a literal colour in the chrome: only a biome's own colour is data.
- Don't bring back a brush: the land is shapes (biome areas over a base); the grid is for scale.
- Don't draw a door as a shape on a wall: put an opening in the wall, so the wall shows the gap and the door stays on it.
- Don't move a planet by hand round its star: put it on an orbit, and change the orbit.
- Don't stamp a picture: a castle or a tree is a marker, a shape of the library, so it can be recoloured, resized, named, reshaped, merged or cut like any other.
- Don't move a rough edge's points to make it rough: set its roughness, and the points stay easy to edit.
- Don't give a map a look: biomes colour the land, and height is lighter or darker.
- Don't paint a mountain in a gray of its own to show its height: give the area a height and a profile, and let the shading and the contours show it.
- Don't fill a nation in the Biomes view: its border and name are enough there, and the Nations view fills it.
- Don't make a button text-only: the tools are icons with a label and a title.
- Don't draw with the pointer events of the page: the canvas owns them (touch-action none).
