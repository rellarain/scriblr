# Maps (the setting builder)

The **Maps** console is where the book's setting is mapped. It is a tab of the Writer's **Project** level (after Project, before History), with the folded-map icon. A map is a grid of terrain cells painted with the pointer; maps nest (a town inside a world map), and each has layers.

Code: `writer/maps/` (`MapsConsole.tsx`, `MapList.tsx`, `MapCanvas.tsx`, `MapSide.tsx`, `MapFields.tsx`, `useMaps.ts`, `mapModel.ts`, `mapItems.ts`, `mapSymbols.ts`, `mapThemes.ts`, `mapView.ts`, `mapRender.ts`, `mapDrawItems.ts`, `mapShapes.ts`, `mapDrawShapes.ts`, `mapExport.ts`, `mapFile.ts`, `placesStore.ts`, `PlacePicker.tsx`, `ForReadersPanel.tsx`, `maps.scss`). Storage: the project's `maps` section (`project.json`), one entry per map; the API is `/api/projects/{id}/maps`.

## Anatomy

`.mapPage` holds a bar (the title, the **tools**, the **Save cluster**) and `.mapSplit`: the **list** (`.mapList`, 220px), the **stage** (`.mapStage`: the canvas and a status line) and the **side** (`.mapSide`, 280px: the chosen thing, Terrain, Symbols, Lines and regions, Layers, Map, Image, For readers). Below 860px of its own width the parts stack. The console sits on the tab's surface in the ink colour.

- **Tools** (icon buttons, `aria-pressed`): **Select and move** (the pointer), **Paint** (brush), **Fill an area**, **Pick up a terrain** (the dropper), **Stamp a symbol**, **Place a label**, **Draw a line**, **Draw a region**, **Measure a distance** (the ruler) and **Move the view** (the hand); then the brush width (1, 2 or 3 cells) and the **grid** toggle. The middle or right mouse button pans with any tool; the wheel zooms at the pointer; the view buttons at the canvas corner zoom in and out and **fit** the map.
- **List**: each map with the maps inside it under it (indented), its size, and a delete that asks first (the maps inside move up). The plus opens the **New map** form: title, kind (world or region, town or city, interior), a size (Small 32×24, Medium 64×48, Large 128×96, Huge 256×192, Largest 512×512, or any width and height up to 512), what one cell is (a number and a unit: miles, paces, feet ...), and the map it is inside.
- **Terrain**: the palette of the open map (each kind starts with its own set; a map keeps its own copy). A row is a swatch, the name and the share of the map it covers. The chosen terrain is what the brush and the fill paint; below it, its colour, name and a remove (it asks when cells use it, and says they become the first terrain). The plus adds a terrain of your own.
- **Layers**: terrain, regions and lines, symbols, labels, notes (bottom to top; listed top first, and drawn in that order, so a symbols layer above the labels draws over them). Each has **show / hide**, **lock**, a name, **move up / down** and delete (never the terrain layer; deleting a layer takes the symbols and labels on it, and an undo brings them back). Hidden or locked terrain cannot be painted, and a symbol or label goes on the top layer of its kind that is shown and not locked; the console says so when there is none. A line or region goes on the top shown, unlocked layer of regions and lines.
- **Map**: title, kind, width and height (a new size keeps the painted cells from the top left), the size of a cell and its unit, the **look** (Parchment, Modern, Night or Blueprint) and three check boxes for what is drawn over the map: **Scale bar**, **Compass** and **Legend**.
- **Symbols** (`.mapSymbols`): four sets as chips (**Settlements**, **Nature**, **Interior**, **Landmarks and travel**) and, for the set, a grid of square buttons, each the symbol's own silhouette in the ink colour (`aria-pressed` on the one that is chosen). Picking one picks the Stamp tool. "New symbols are N cells wide" sets the size a stamp is made at.
- **Chosen thing** (top of the side, only while something is chosen): a **symbol** has its width in cells, its **colour** (a button takes it back to the colour of the look) and a delete; a **label** has its **text** (it takes the cursor when it has just been placed), its height in cells and a delete. Drag it with the Select tool, or move it with the arrow keys (a cell; Shift a quarter), Delete removes it, Escape lets go.
- **Lines and regions** (the pen): a row of chips for the kinds of **line** (River, Road, Trail, Border, Wall, Fence) and one for the kinds of **region** (Territory, Water, Forest, Danger); a chip sets the pen and picks the Line or Region tool. Below, the fields of the pen: a line's **style** (Solid, Dashed, Dotted, Double road, Wall, Fence), **width** in cells and **colour** (a button takes it back to the ink of the look), for a region its **fill** colour, how **solid** it is (percent) and its **pattern** (Solid, Hatched, Cross-hatched, Dotted), and **Curve the points** (new points are smooth, or corners).
- **Chosen line or region** (top of the side): the same fields for the one that is chosen, **Curve all points** and **Straighten all points**, and a delete.

## The canvas

A bitmap of one pixel per cell is scaled up without smoothing, so each cell is a crisp square, then the layers above it in their order; the grid shows once a cell is 6 pixels or more. **Symbols** are one SVG path each in a 24 by 24 box (`mapSymbols.ts`), drawn in their own colour or the look's, over a halo of the look's paper colour; **labels** are drawn over the same kind of halo, and left out when they would be under 6 pixels tall. Things are placed in cells (a symbol's or label's centre), snapped to the middle of a cell (Alt while placing or dragging: free), so they keep their place when the view zooms. The **scale bar** (a round length, 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, of about 110 pixels), the **compass** (north is up) and the **legend** (the terrains that are used and the kinds of symbol on the map) sit in corners of the map on a plate, and are left out when the map is too small on the canvas to hold them. The frame round the map takes the **ink colour** of the page; the colours of the terrain are the author's, and everything drawn over it (symbols, text, grid, plates) is in the colours of the map's **look** (`mapThemes.ts`: ink, symbol, halo, plate and typeface per look; Parchment fades the terrain towards old paper, Night darkens it, Blueprint draws it in shades of one blue, Modern shows it as it is). These are the map's art, set in code, and the only colours that are not tokens. **Lines and regions** (`mapShapes.ts`, `mapDrawShapes.ts`) are lists of points in cells; a region closes on its first point. A corner is a straight piece; a **smooth** point is a curve through it, a cubic Bezier whose handle is the one its two neighbours make (so a smooth line is a good curve untouched), or one that was dragged. The six line styles are strokes of the one path: dashed and dotted by dash pattern, a **double road** as two edges with the road colour between them, a **wall** as a solid line with crenels, a **fence** as a thin rail with cross ticks. A region is filled at its opacity, with hatching, cross-hatching or dots clipped to its outline, over its outline in a line style. Drawing: click to add points (they **snap to grid corners and cell centres**, Alt: no snapping), double-click or Enter finishes, Escape cancels, Backspace takes the last point back; a line needs two points and a region three. The chosen shape shows its points as squares and its handles as circles; drag any, or the body (half a cell at a time); double-click a point to curve or straighten it, double-click the line to put a point in; with a point chosen Delete takes it away (never below the fewest). A stroke is **one undo step**; the Save cluster's Undo and Redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z) work per map and are kept while the project is open. The canvas is `role="img"` with the map's name and size; painting is by pointer.

## Places, nesting, measuring and files

- **Places**: a symbol that has a **name** is a **place**. The chosen-symbol panel has a *Place name* field and, once it is named, **Maps from here** (the maps that open from the place: open one, stop opening it, make a new one inside this map for the place with the folder-plus button, or link one that exists) and **Scenes here** (the scenes whose Location is the place). **Double-click** a place with the Select tool to open its first map; the **way up** (`.mapPath`: an up button and the crumbs of the maps above) sits above the canvas. The name is drawn under the symbol.
- **Scene Location** (Outline and Draft) is a dropdown of the places of the project's maps, shown with their path ("Known World > Port Veyra"); old text is kept until it is converted. The **Old locations** panel (shown while a scene still has text typed before places existed) makes places of the checked texts on the open map and points their scenes at them; "Drop the old texts" asks first and clears the rest.
- **Inside**: the Map panel's *Inside* field moves a map into another (never into itself or a map within it). **A day's travel** is how far a traveller goes in a day, in the map's unit.
- **Measure** (the ruler tool): click to add points to a dashed path, Backspace takes one back, Escape clears; the length is said on a plate at the end and in the status line, with the days it takes when a day's travel is set.
- **Image**: the *Image* panel saves the map as a **PNG** at 1x, 2x or 4x (a cell is 16, 32 or 64 pixels, held to 8192 on the longest side) with the layers that are checked; the grid and the editing marks are left out.
- **Files**: the export button on a map row writes the map **and the maps inside it** as `*.scriblr-map.json`; the import button on the list reads one and asks, for each map the project already has under the same id, whether to **keep both**, **replace the one here** or **leave it out**. Links to maps that are not in the file are dropped.
- The **Characters** and **Settings** built-in plot presets no longer appear in the Plot's preset list.

## Links to other things of the story

A plot node, a character and a place can be linked to each other (not to their own kind: plot nodes have their own links, characters their relationships). The pieces are in `writer/links/` (`WorldLinks.tsx`, `linkStore.ts`, `worldNav.ts`, `links.scss`); a link is one record in the project (`worldLinks`, backend `world_links.py`), so it reads the same from both ends.

- **Chips** (`.wlList`): each link is a row with the kind ("Plot", "Character", "Place") and the other end's name (a plot node by its place in the plot, a place by the maps it is inside); the row opens the other end, the x beside it takes the link away from both ends.
- **Picker** (`.wlSelect`, "Link to…"): grouped by kind (Plot nodes, Characters, Places), without what is linked already and without the thing's own kind.
- **Where**: in the Characters console under Story ("Linked to"), in the Maps console under the chosen named symbol, and under the plot node's Linked list ("Characters and places"). A link to something that is deleted (a character, a place that lost its name) is simply not shown.
- **Opening**: a plot node opens in the Plot; a character or a place opens in its own console when that console is on screen (else when its tab is next opened): the Maps console opens the map and chooses the place.

## For readers

What readers see of the setting and the cast is the author's choice, and it is only ever what the author has published. The shared pieces live in `writer/readers/` (`ReaderControls.tsx`, `WorldViews.tsx`, `readers.scss`); the Reader's two consoles in `reader/WorldConsoles.tsx`.

- **Publish switch** (a checkbox, "Show this map to readers" or "Show Ada to readers"): nothing is shown until it is ticked, and nothing leaves the project until the **book is published**: publishing a book copies the published maps and the public side of the published characters into the new edition, as it copies the chapters, so a later change waits for the next edition. (Backend: `ReaderShare` on the map and the character, `EditionMap` and `EditionCharacter` on `BookEdition`, `world_publishing.py`.)
- **Reveal at a chapter** (`.rvSelect`, "From the start" or "Chapter n: title"): the chapters are the ones of the published edition, so the numbers are the ones readers see. A chapter the edition has not got yet is kept and marked "(not published yet)". When the project has several books, a select names the book whose chapters count.
- **Parts**: a map's symbol, label or line can be revealed later than the map (choose it, then its own "shown from"); a character's fields are ticked one by one (the look is always shown, the rest of the profile never is), each with its own chapter; relationships show only between published characters.
- **Spoiler warnings** (`.rvWarn`, a list named "Spoiler warnings"): a scene that uses a place of a map, or a character, in a chapter before the chapter where it is revealed. Fetched again after each save.
- **Preview as a reader** ("A reader who has reached"): the saved map or character as a reader who has reached a chapter sees it (the preview is of what is saved, and says so).
- **In the Reader**: the Book page gets two consoles, **Maps** (the maps, the ones inside another listed under it, the named places) and **Cast** (a card for each character: close-up, name, public fields, links). What is still to be revealed is only counted ("2 more maps to discover as you read"), never named. The chapter a reader has reached is where they last stopped (or the whole book once finished).
- Checkboxes use the browser's own, sized 16px (`appearance: auto`: the global input reset hides them otherwise), in the accent colour.

## Saving

As everywhere in the Writer: **autosave is off by default**, so an edit waits for the Save button (or the autosave wait, or leaving the page or the map). The whole map goes in one request. Opening another map saves what is waiting first. A failed save rings the Save button and keeps the edit.

## Tokens

Surfaces `surface-side`, `surface-raised`, `ov-sink-1`, `ov-sink-2`, `hairline`; text `ink`, `ink-muted`; `accent` for the chosen row, tool and terrain; `alert` for a refused save. Space and type from the scales. No literal colours in the chrome.

## Don't

- Don't put a literal colour in the chrome: only a terrain's own colour is data.
- Don't make a button text-only: the tools are icons with a label and a title.
- Don't paint with the pointer events of the page: the canvas owns them (touch-action none).
