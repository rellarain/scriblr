# Level panels, headers and tabs

Four stacked levels make the Writer: **Dash**, **Project**, **Outline** (the open book) and **Draft** (the open chapter). Each is a `LevelPanel`: a flat fill of its colour, a header strip and a body. One level is the focus (Max); the one above is Mid, the rest Min; the ones below are not shown.

Code: `writer/levels/LevelPanel.tsx`, `WriterLevels.tsx`, `LevelTabs.tsx`, `levelSizes.ts`; styles `.wrLevel*` in `writer/styles/_levels.scss` and `.wrTab*` in `_level-tabs.scss`.

## Anatomy

- **Panel** `.wrLevel` (`--dash`, `--project`, `--outline`, `--draft`; `--min`, `--mid`, `--max`). Its fill is `--wr-level-fill`: the level's hue at the accent's saturation less a little, a step deeper, held dark enough for white text ([level-colours](../../level-colours.md)). Its text is `--on-accent`, always white. The Dash is the app theme's own colour; the Draft is paper.
- **Header** `.wrLevelHeader`: min height 40, padding 8 / 22. The **title** (`.wrLevelTitle`, 14px bold, all caps, letter spacing 1.5px, line height 1.7) is a button that brings the level to focus; the title and the size toggle share one line (`.wrLevelTitleRow`), the toggle at its right end; the **tabs** (`.wrTabBar`, 30 x 30 `.wrTabBtn`, icons 18) and **quick actions** (`.wrQuickActions`: Search, Save; no header New button) sit at its right; at Mid a **chevron** (`.wrLevelToggle`) folds it to Min. In a narrow header the tabs and quick actions wrap beneath the title line.
- **Body** `.wrLevelBody`: `level-fill-1`, padding 8 / 12 / 12. At Min a level shows only its header (and a short body, e.g. book spines).

## Tabs

At Mid one tab shows at a time; at Max each tab opens or closes a tile on the split grid ([SplitTiles](../SplitTiles/README.md)). The tab strip ends with **Settings** and **Help**. A pressed tab (`.wrTabBtn--on`) is a darker wash of the header, `aria-pressed`. Tab bodies on a light **surface** (editors) use `--surface-side` with the ink, not the level's white.

## Tokens

`level-fill-<hue>`, `level-fill-1..4`, `level-ink`, `level-wash`, `ink`, `surface-side` (an editor tab). Header buttons are white at 16% over the fill.

## You provide

`level`, `size`, `title`, `hue` (the level's colour; none = the theme's), `onPromote`, `onSetSize`; `headerExtras` for the tabs; `minBody` for what a Min level shows; `headerless` where the body brings its own header (the Outline's book editor, the Draft's chapter tile). The tab list comes from `useTabbedLevel`.

## Don't

- Don't give a level a radius, a gradient or a shadow.
- Don't set text colour on a level except `inherit`: white is set once, by the panel.
- Don't nest more than the steps `level-fill-1..4` provide.

## Publishing a book, and what readers did

The **Book level** (Outline) has a **Publish** tab, between Book details and Settings (at Mid a tab; at Max a section of the book's cover). It puts the book in the Reader's library:

- **Publish the book**: a button that asks first ("Publish edition 2?"). An edition is a frozen copy of the chapters published so far with their published footnotes and flagged passages. Publishing again makes the next edition; **Unpublish** takes the book off sale and loan and keeps copies readers own and loans already made.
- **Details in the library**: type, buy price, blurb, genres, tags, tropes, an optional cover colour of its own, and the content flags (a name, the details a reader sees and the class it has by default: hidden, marked, highlight or spoiler). Saved with Save details; a new edition takes the saved details.
- **Invite readers**: a level for this reader account and for each seeded reader (alpha, delta or beta); the level set for this account is the level the Reader shows the book at.
- **Readers**: loans, sales, the author's share (a loan's first credit pays $3.00 and each more $1.00; a sale 70% of the price), the rating and reviews, and the readers' anonymous reactions by chapter (only from readers who share them). Money is simulated.

The **Dash** has a **Readers** tab listing every published book with its loans, sales, earnings and rating.

## Writing analytics

The **Project level's Analytics tab** and the **Dash's Analytics tab** carry the Reader's analytics, for writing. The Project's tab first shows each book's words against its own goal; both then show (`WritingAnalytics`):

- **This week**: words this week, words a writing day, the streak (days in a row with any writing, edits or revisions; a quiet today does not break it) and the best streak of the year.
- **Writing, last 12 months** `.wrCal`: the Reader's calendar, a 14px square for each day of 53 weeks, a week to a column, Sunday first, shaded in four steps of the accent by the day's share of the busiest day. Words, Edits and Revisions switch what it counts; a hover says "1,200 words on Oct 11". A day's words are the net change in the drafts that day, so a day that cut more than it wrote is a quiet one. Days are UTC, as the backend records them.
- **Words per day this week**: a bar for each of the last seven days.
- **Goals**: words a day and words a week, kept on this machine, each with a meter against today and this week.

At the Project level it is over that project; on the Dash, over every project. The calendar is the same component as the Reader's (`components/ActivityCalendar.tsx`, `lib/activityCalendar.ts`); the Writer draws it with the `wr` classes.

## The side column

Beside the focused level, the others sit in a **side column** (`.wrLevelsSide`): 280 to 460px wide (32% of the Writer) by default.

- **Resize**: a 10px handle (`.wrSideResizer`, `role="separator"`) on its right edge; a thin accent line shows on hover and while dragging. The width is held between **200px and 60%** of the Writer, shown live and kept on release; a double click puts it back to the default. At the Draft the column still lies over the page's left side, at the chosen width.
- **Hide**: the **sidebar bar** in the app's header (`SidebarToggle`: a plain 6px vertical bar, no icon, left of the user card with 5px either side of it; lit in the accent colour = shown) hides it, and the focused level takes the whole width. It is disabled at the Dash, which has no side column.
- **Narrow windows**: until the button is pressed, the column is hidden by itself while the window is narrower than 1000px, and shown again when it grows.
- Both are kept on this machine (`scriblr.writer.sidebar`: `{ width, hidden }`; `null` = the default, or automatic).

## The Plot tool

The Project level's Plot tab (`PlotView`) follows **its own width**, not the window's (`useElementWidth`, the `data-size` of `.wrPlotFrame`):

| Width | Layout |
|---|---|
| wide, 1,040px and up | the contents column (250px, or the width it was dragged to) beside the editor; a plotline's fields beside its outline panel |
| medium, 720 to 1,039px | contents beside the editor, but a plotline's chapters panel stands under its fields |
| compact, under 720px | **one column**: the contents are a bar on top (`.wrPlotBar`: where you are, as a trail, or "Comparing 3 plotlines") that folds down over the editor |

- **Rows** (`.wrNavRow`): a **category, a subcategory and a cluster are flat fills of their own hue with white text** (`--wr-cat-fill`, `--wr-sub-fill`, `--wr-cluster-fill`: the level fill, a category's at the quietest saturation tier, a subcategory's the middle, a cluster's the loudest; a hovered or selected one is a little darker), and a **plotline is its category's own lighter colour** with the zone's ink and its stripes on the left. A row's **name is always bold (700, the field size, 13px, with 2px of margin around it) and its description regular (400, the UI size, 12px)**, whichever row is open (the open one is the darker row). A **category's and a subcategory's name is in capitals** (`text-transform`, with a little letter spacing); a cluster's, a plotline's and every description keep the case they were typed in. With the fifth circle lit, a node's **keywords** stand under its name (and its description) as **read-only chips** (`.wrNavChip` in `.wrNavChips`, bold at the small size with 4px of padding on every side, on the sink overlay, their text the row's own ink **fainter than the name and the description**, at 72%, near the least that reads at 4.5:1; wrapped over as many lines as they need; plain items of a list, nothing in one can be pressed, typed in or removed; audited as "plot-nav-chip"). The **title field in a node's editor** (`.wrTitleField`) matches its row: bold, and in capitals for a category and a subcategory with the same spacing, but **larger**, at the title size plus 1px (16px, the row's is 13px); the text is stored as typed, as for the row.
- **Width**: a **handle on the contents' right edge** (`.wrSideResizer`, the same 10px strip over the gap as the side column's, `role="separator"`, "Contents width") sets the column's width: the cursor turns to a resize cursor and a thin accent line shows on hover and while dragging. The width follows the pointer live and is **held between 200px and 45% of the Plot tool**, so the editor keeps its room (`plotNavWidth.ts`; the same limits again in CSS, `--wr-nav-basis`, so a narrower window holds a kept width too). It is **kept on this machine** (`scriblr.writer.plotNavWidth`) and 250px until it is dragged. The compact layout has a bar instead of a column, so it has no handle.
- **Order**: in the contents a category lists its own plotlines first, then its subcategories, and a subcategory its plotlines, then its clusters (each in title order).
- **Inherited fields**: a plotline's `From category / subcategory / cluster` groups put the parent's title first and the kind ("Category", "Subcategory", "Cluster") at the far right of the head, in the muted ink.
- **Placing a value** (`PlotValueCard`, `PlotOutlinePanel`, `plotPlacement.ts`): a value stays in its field however often it is placed; its card keeps its category colours and shows a counter (**x2**) once it is placed, with a lock in place of the delete button (it can be deleted only when it is placed nowhere). Its grip always drags it: onto a chapter of the **Outline** panel for one more placement (on a chapter it is already on, nothing happens). The panel lists **one box for each placement** of this plotline's values in its chapter, in the book's colours (an awareness shade on a moment): an **x** on a placement on the chapter itself takes that placement away, the **awareness eye** of a placement on a moment cycles that placement alone, and a lock marks one on an act or scene (take it away in the Outline). A field's head reads "unplaced / total" and, once any value is placed, the sum of their placements (**x5**); the **Show assigned** switch hides the values placed at least once.
- **Outline panel circles** (`PlotOutlinePanel.tsx`, `LevelDots.tsx`): the panel's head holds the label ("Outline") and, at the far right, the same four circles as the Plot contents (the same `.wrNavDot` styles): three that **fold a whole level** (series, books, arcs, left to right; lit while any card of the level is open, a click closes or opens them all, dimmed when no card of the level has anything under it; chapters have nothing to fold) and a fourth, **filled in the alert hue** while it is lit, that shows the **whole synopsis** of every series, book, arc and chapter card under its name (`.wrPlotCardDesc`, the small size, the card's own ink, wrapped, line breaks kept; a folded card keeps its synopsis; a blank one shows nothing). The fourth circle is dimmed while no card has a synopsis, and its choice is **its own setting**, kept on this machine (`scriblr.writer.plotOutlineDescriptions`), apart from the contents' circle. The circles take the place of the hint, which is now the label's tooltip ("Drop a plotpoint on a chapter", or "Where each plotpoint sits" in the read-only compare view, which has the circles too).
- **Fieldsets** (`FieldBlocks.tsx`, `_plot-fields.scss`): a category, subcategory, cluster or plotline can group some of its fields in named sets. Beside **+ Field** in a node's own group stands **+ Fieldset**, which adds an unnamed set whose name takes focus (left blank and empty it is dropped). A set (`.wrFieldSet`, `role="group"`, a sunk box inside the group) has a head of **chevron** (folds it; open at first, and the fold is **remembered per set**, so a set folded on its category stays folded on the plotlines under it), **grip** (drag to reorder the sets), its **name** (`.wrFieldSetName`, small bold capitals, 30 characters), a count, **+ Field** (adds a field to that set) and a trash that asks "Its N fields stay": deleting a set keeps its fields, loose. A node lists its **loose fields first, then its sets, then the plotline's Scrap field**. Drag a field by its grip, and **the rows slide out of the way**: over its own set (or its node's loose fields) the field leaves its place (hidden; the pointer carries its picture) and the rows it passes slide up or down, 0.16s, to open a gap where it will land, in front of the row the pointer is in the upper half of, or last below the middle of the last row; let go and it is there. Over **another** group nothing slides (it would grow the group under the pointer): a line in the accent colour shows where the field would go, in front of a row or after the last (an empty set is outlined), and dropping on the set's own head puts it last in the set. While a field in a set is dragged, the **"Drop here to take it out of its set"** spot (below the sets) makes it loose, last among the loose fields. The sliding is a transform only, and starts only once the drag is under way (the spot opens **below** the sets for the same reason: a change in the very event the drag starts that moves the dragged row makes the browser cancel the drag); a row's place is its layout place, so a row that has slid under the pointer does not change where the field would go; a field, a set and the spot also accept the drag as it enters, so one let go on arrival still drops. Drag a set onto another to go before it, or onto "Drop here to move it last". A set passes down to the plotlines like its fields: read-only there (a name, a count, no grip, no trash), under the origin's own group. A plotline makes its own sets for its own fields, never putting them into an inherited set.
- **Choice fields** (`ChoiceFields.tsx`, `plotKinds.ts`, `plotAnswers.ts`): a field is the usual list of plotpoints, a **multi-select**, a **dropdown** or a **short text**. The node that defines a field picks its kind from the select after the name (disabled once the field has any value: *A field keeps its kind once it has values.*) and, for a multi-select or dropdown, its **options** under the head. A **dropdown's** options are chips (`.wrOptions`: a grip, the label edited in place, 30 characters, an x; "Add option…" at the end, at most 20; **drag the grip and the chips slide out of the way** (the chips flow and wrap, so each takes the place the wrapped new order gives it, 0.16s, which may be on another line; the dragged chip is hidden while the pointer is over the options, and carries its own picture; the gap is in front of the chip the pointer is in the left half of, or last past the end), or Alt + Left or Right Arrow reorders; removing one that answers use asks, "Remove Fantasy? 2 answers use it and 2 places go."). A **multi-select's** options and its own answers are **one column** (`.wrOptionRows`, one `.wrOptionRow` each, never a second list of the same options): a grip that reorders (the rows it passes slide a row's height, 0.16s, a gap opens where it will land; Alt + Up, Down, Left or Right Arrow also move it), a tick box, the label edited in place, an x that removes the option (asking first as above). Ticking a row makes the answer: the row then wears the answer card's colours, shows its counter (**x2**) once placed and, where an answer can be dragged onto a chapter (a plotline's own field, not the category, subcategory and cluster editors), a second grip ("Drag plotpoint") after the label; unticking a placed one asks first. A plotline shows every choice field above it (and its own) with the kind named at the right of its head and **its own answer**: for the node that defines the field the answer is above it, in **a card of its own** (`.wrAnswerFrom`, a light lift of the group around it): the node's **title at the top left** and its **kind at the top right** ("Romance", "Category": the group heads' small bold capitals; the kind in the ink at 80%, fainter than the title and audited as 'plot-answer-kind'), then its answers below, not changed here (no tick box, and no lock: hovering says "Answered on Romance. Change it there.") but dragged onto a chapter by its grip and counting its places like the plotline's own, and listed in the plotline's outline with them, then "This plotline" and the controls. A short text the defining node has not answered says **"No answer"** in that card; **a multi-select or a dropdown it has not answered has no card at all** (and no "This plotline" caption, which only tells the plotline's own answer from the card's): the plotline's own tick boxes or select stand alone. An answer is a plotpoint: it wears the category's colours like a value card but is one line high (no room above for a field name), has a grip that drags it onto a chapter, and a counter (**x2**) once it is placed. *Multi-select* (where the options are not edited, a plotline under the category): a tick box for each option in a column, **without the options the defining node has answered** (they are shown above; one the plotline already answered stays so that it can be unticked; when none is left, "Every option is answered above.") (drawn by hand, `.wrAnswerBox`: a ring in the ink, filled with the ink and the tick in the card's own colour once ticked); a ticked one is a card. *Dropdown*: a select ("None" and the options) while there is no answer; once an option is picked the select is **replaced by the answer as a chip** with its grip, and an **x that takes the answer away and brings the select back**; once the answer is placed its **count (x2) stands where the x was**, so it is changed only after its places are taken away. *Short text*: one input (50 characters) that is the card once there is text, with its grip, counter and a trash that clears it. Unticking a multi-select answer or clearing a short text that is placed asks first ("Untick Fantasy? Its 2 places go too.", Confirm and Cancel), because the places go with it; a text left blank is taken away unless it is placed. The head counts the answers instead of "unplaced / total", and a choice field takes no value written by hand (no **+ Value**, and the keyboard makes none).
- **Folding a level**: three 10px circles at the top right of the contents (`.wrNavDot`) fold a whole level at once: categories, subcategories, clusters, left to right. A circle is **filled in the accent while any node of its level is open** (and its click closes them all), an outline when they are all closed (its click opens them all), and dimmed when no node of that level has anything under it. A **fourth circle** stands apart from them (`.wrNavDot--desc`, in `.wrNavTools`): an outline when the descriptions are hidden and **filled in the alert hue** when they are shown (see Descriptions); it is dimmed when no row has a description. Lit, it has a ring in the ink around it, so it stands out on a level's dark fill, where the alert hue alone is about 2:1. A **fifth circle** after it (`.wrNavDot--keys`) shows and hides the **keywords** the same way, **filled in the accent's lighter shade** with the same ring while they are shown, and dimmed when no row has a keyword.
- **Adding**: the head of the contents (`.wrPlotNavHead`) has **+ Category** (an empty category, opened at once) and **+ Preset** (`.wrPlotNavAddRow`) side by side at the left, in capitals and with 4px rounded corners like the rows, and the circles at the right; there is no title. When they don't fit on one line (they don't at the default 250px) **the buttons drop to a second line under the circles**; a wider panel keeps all on one. + Preset opens a menu of the published plot presets, under the head and as wide as the panel, a name with its description on each row; choosing one adds a whole category with its subcategories, clusters, fields, keywords and descriptions, as a plain copy, in one undo step, and opens it. See [Plot presets](../PlotPresets/README.md).
- **Descriptions**: hidden until the fourth circle is lit. Then a category, subcategory, cluster or plotline row shows its **whole description** under its name (`.wrNavDesc`, the UI size (12px) on a 16px line height, the row's own ink, wrapped over as many lines as it needs, line breaks kept; a plotline's checkbox and name stay level with the first line, inside its stripes). A row with no description (or only blanks) stays a single line. The choice is one setting for the contents, kept on this machine (`scriblr.writer.plotNavDescriptions`).
- **Keywords**: hidden until the fifth circle is lit. Then every row whose node has keywords shows them as chips, in the node's order and as typed, **under the description** when that is shown and **directly under the name** when it is not (the row is then a stack: its checkbox, chevron and count stand level with the name). A row with no keyword stays a single line. The choice is its own setting, kept on this machine (`scriblr.writer.plotNavKeywords`), apart from the descriptions'.
- **Ticking**: every row of the contents has a checkbox (`.wrNavCheck`, drawn by hand: a box, a tick, or a bar for part-ticked). A plotline's ticks that plotline; a category, subcategory or cluster ticks every plotline under it. "Compare (n)" and "Clear" show once any are ticked. The ticks are kept while browsing and reset with the project.
- **Compare** (`PlotCompare`): two or more ticked plotlines open it in place of the editor. Chips (one per plotline, in its plot colours and its stacked stripes) hide or show its cards, and an x unticks it. The plotpoints stand in **one list**, read-only: each card is a plotpoint tile with its trail, plotline and field, title, description, its counter (x2) and where it is placed (its first place in story order, "2 · Storm", with "and 1 more" when there are others, or "Not placed yet"); a click opens that plotline's editor. **Group by plotline** gives each plotline a header: side by side as columns from 860px, otherwise as sections that fold. The chapters panel, with every shown plotline's chips (each marked with its plotline's stripe), is beside the list when wide and a fold-out under it otherwise.
- **Parent chips** (`PlotParents`, `.wrParents`): above the title of a subcategory, cluster or plotline editor (and under each plotline's header in Compare's groups) stand its parents, top-down, as read-only chips, each in its own plot colour with the stacked 4px stripes of its place in the tree at its left (a cluster's chip: category, subcategory, cluster; the tile ink on a category, white on a subcategory's or cluster's fill: the audited pairs). A chip shows only the parent's title (14px bold capitals, 1.5px letter spacing, no kind label; the description, 14px, is its hover text and what the click opens). A click opens the chip to the whole description; its title then links to that parent's editor (in Compare it also leaves the comparison). A parent with no description is only its title, and the title links. Which chips are open is **remembered per level** while the tool is open: open the category chip and the subcategory chip on one plotline and the category and subcategory chips are open on every plotline, cluster or subcategory editor you go to next (whichever parents they have), and in Compare's group headers; closing a level's chip closes it everywhere. Chips wrap onto more lines on a narrow tile.
- **The contents in the compact layout** (`.wrPlotBarDrawer`) stand on the level's own fill (`wr-fill-2`) over the light tile, so they are read in the level ink: white (`on-accent`) for the label, the circles, "n selected" and "Clear", and the two add buttons are the level wash with that ink (the audited level pairs: white on `fill-2`, the header button wash). In the wide and medium layouts the contents are on the tile's light surface in the dark ink.
- **Order**: the toggle (`PlotOrderToggle`, shared with the Draft's chapter list and the Plotpoints tab) is two icon buttons: a **clock** for by time, an **open book** (`book-open`) for in story. Plotpoints placed nowhere come first; a placed one stands at its earliest placement.
