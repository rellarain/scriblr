# Character models

Character models are the shapes characters will be drawn from: the body, the clothes and the hair, arranged in **groups** of **objects** made of **shapes**. They are **designed in the Admin console called Designer** (a console of its own, beside Configuration) and saved as drafts and published one by one, the way the Plot presets are. The designer draws shapes, arranges them, and (since the second phase) **rigs** them: groups nest and turn about pivots, are fixed to a shape of their parent, and are driven by **sliders**. It also **pairs** left and right (a twin that mirrors another shape, object or group), **links points** (clothes that stay on the body, and loosen with a slider) and shows the **back view** with outlines of their own. Since the third phase an object can have **forms** (alternate outlines along axes such as the shape of an eye), the model keeps named **presets** (poses, expressions, ages, builds, outfits), objects and groups can be **switched off** with an eye to look at the rest, and the figure can be **posed by dragging**. Since the fourth step of that phase an object can be the pattern of a **class** (a family of eyes, or the two hands) whose members follow it, and a **style** keeps a named look of an object or a group. Since phase 4 a shape is painted from a **colour slot** (Skin, Hair, Eye...) whose default colours and ranges the model holds, and the designer works on a gray **test character** that can be coloured by hand or **rolled**. Since phase D3 a variant bundles choices over the same shapes, a group has **states**, and an author can make **fields**: sliders that change one property of one shape, point, edge or object. It stores nothing about any character.

## The page

The Designer is an **Admin console of its own**, beside Configuration (a tile with the dress-mannequin icon, `MannequinIcon`, and the title *Designer*; the region is named *Designer*). **The canvas fills the whole console** and **four toolbars** lie over its edges: the **tree** (left), the **editor** (right), the **view controls** (the footer, flush with the console's bottom edge: the canvas stage grows to the console's height, with a floor of its own for a host that has none) and the **console tools** (the console's title bar). The page keeps the Admin's accent. The console is `.dzConsole`; the work area `.dzStage` has a height of its own (the page scrolls when the window is shorter), the canvas is positioned over all of it, and the view is fitted and centred in what the toolbars leave uncovered (`insets`), so a figure is never hidden behind one.

- **Console tools** (`.dzConsoleBar`, `role="toolbar"` named *Console tools*; in the title bar of the Designer's tile, or at the top where the console has no title bar of its own, as in the design-system card): **Undo**, **Redo** (always there, disabled when there is nothing to undo or redo), **Save** (the Admin's Save cluster, with the autosave toggle it reveals on hover) and **Publish** (disabled, with the reason in its title, while the Checks have something that stops it). Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+S work in the console.
- **The side toolbars** (`.dzSide`, `aside` regions named *Tree tools* and *Editor tools*): a title bar with a **fold** button, a body that scrolls, and an edge to **drag wider or narrower** (`role="separator"`, `aria-valuenow`; focus it and use the arrow keys, Shift for more). Folded, a toolbar is a slim rail with one button to open it again. Neither takes more than 46% of the console. Whether each is open, and how wide, is **remembered on this computer** with the other settings.
- **View toolbar** (`.dzViewBar`, `role="toolbar"` named *View controls*): on the left, **the layers of the canvas** as a row of icon toggles (`aria-pressed`, pressed while the layer is shown): **weights and curve handles**, **shape points**, **shape outlines**, **pivots, anchors and links**, **alignment guides**, **mirror line**, **pattern ghost and frames**, **the figure** and **gridlines**. **Click** a toggle to hide or show its layer; **drag** it sideways (or press Alt + Left / Right on it) to move it: **the leftmost layer is drawn on top**. The painted figure is one of the layers, so the gridlines can lie under it (to begin with) or over it, and it cannot be hidden. Which layers are shown and in what order is **remembered on this computer**. Then **Front view** and **Back view**. On the right: **Fit to screen** (a toggle; everything that is drawn, with little blank space; pressed again with the view left alone it goes back to fitting the selection), the **zoom** (`input type="range"`, a logarithmic scale from 25% to 800% with its percent beside it; **scrolling down over it zooms in and scrolling up zooms out**, the way round that was asked for, and the arrow keys move it), and **Previews and checks**, a button that opens a **drawer** (`.dzDrawer`, `role="region"`) above the footer with the previews and the checks side by side (stacked when narrow). The button shows how many checks stop Publish.
- **Editor toolbar**: the model's strip first (`.dzModel`: its name, its **status badge** (`.cmBadge`: *Published v3*, *v3 · unpublished changes*, *Unpublished (was v2)*, *Not published*; a tick, a filled dot, a hollow dot, a grey dot), **Unpublish**, **Discard changes**, **Versions** (the published version and the ten before it, each with *Bring into draft*), **Duplicate**, **Export** and **Delete**, which asks in place first); then the **Designer tools** (`.dzTools`, `role="toolbar"`: point editor, move shape, **Weights**, **Pose mode** (the hand; see below), **mirror editing**, **snap** (points and handles land on half units, the mirror line and the alignment guides), **snap to grid**, new shape from a template and the side things fall to), the pose bar while Pose mode is on, the **bar of what is open** (below) and then the **Inspector** (below). There are **no typed number fields**: points are moved by dragging and by the arrow keys.
- **Tree toolbar** (`TreeBar.tsx`, `nav` named *Model tree*): a **search box** (*Search the model*) over **nested cards**. The open **model** is a card with its status badge, a **Show all the models** button (`All models (n)`), and inside it, the cards of the levels below. *All models* lists the cards of every model instead (each with its name and status badge, the open one marked, reordered by their grip or Alt + Arrow, **New model**, **Import models from a file** and **Export all the models to a file** beside the heading); pressing a model's card opens it.
- **The tree is nested cards** (`.dzCard`, `role="group"` named *Group Head*, *Object Eye*, *Shape Iris*...). From the outside in the levels are **model, group, object, shape**, and a card carries the states of its own level: the model its **variants**, a group its **poses** and its **states**, an object its **styles** and its **expressions**. At each level **the cards are listed side by side only while none of them is open**; pressing one **opens it, and it stands alone with its children under it**. Pressing the open card that is deepest steps back up to what holds it (a shape to its object, an object to its group, a group to the model); pressing an open card above the deepest makes it the deepest. The search lists a card of any level (with where it lies) and a click on one opens the path down to it, and selecting a shape on the canvas opens its path in the tree. Cards are **listed topmost first**, which is the draw order backwards (the stored order is reversed for display): the first group, object or shape listed is the one in front. A card's **grip** (`.cmGrip`; drag, or Alt + Up / Down) slides it between its siblings.
- **The button at the top right of a card** does one of three things and **a right-click moves it on to the next**: **hide or show** (an eye, `aria-pressed`; only for the open model, a group and an object listed beside its siblings), **add one beside it** (a plus) and **copy it** (a twin for a group, an object or a shape: a mirror image that follows it; a duplicate for a model, a variant, a pose, a style or an expression). The button's `aria-label` says what it does now (*Show Head*, *New group next to Head*, *Make a twin of Head*) and its title says what a right-click will do next.
- **Variants, poses and styles** are presets the card applies: a variant is a preset of the model (kinds such as Age, Build and Outfit are shown on the card), a pose one scoped to a group, a style one scoped to an object. Pressing a card **applies** it; the one that is on is shown alone, and pressing it again turns it off. The plus beside the heading keeps the look now as a new one. **Expressions** are the forms of an object's axes: each axis shows the form it is on, pressing it lists the other forms of the axis, and pressing one chooses it.
- Marks on a card (`.cmTwin`): a twin (the swap icon), a pattern or a member of a class, a shading shape, a helper shape, a shape that follows the pattern. Counts beside a heading turn bold with a bar under them above the warning limit. A card dimmed with a struck-through eye is switched off (it, or what holds it): see *Show and hide* below.
- **The bar of what is open** (`NodeBar.tsx`, a group named *The group that is open*, and so for the model, an object and a shape), at the top of the editor: the kind and name of what is open, **Copy** (a shape, not one that follows a pattern), **Delete** (a shape, an object with its shapes or a group with everything in it, not the model, and not a shape that follows a pattern) and, under them, **Randomize, lock and reset**: **Roll the look** (a new look for the test character), **Lock** (a group or an object: the roll leaves it as it is; `aria-pressed`) and **n off rest** (*Reset test values*), shown while the test values are away from rest.
- **Canvas** (`.cmCanvas`, an SVG): the shapes in the test colours with **no outline of their own** (an outline is canvas chrome and is drawn only round the selected shape, as a ring; the rest is dimmed while something is selected), a **grid** (`.cmGridLine`: a faint line every 2 units at the normal zoom, every 1 and then every 0.5 as the view closes in, and wider apart (5, 10, 20, 50 ...) when it is zoomed so far out that the lines would crowd; every tenth line, or every fiftieth once the lines are ten apart, is a little stronger; there is no grid size to set and no label), the **mirror line** at the model's own axis, and, for the selected shape in the point editor, its **points** and the **curve handles** of the selected point. The shapes are drawn **where the rig puts them**: a shape in a turned group is drawn turned, its points and handles drawn through the group's matrix and a drag undone through it, so a shape can be edited with a slider anywhere on its range. A shape whose points a slider moves, with that slider away from rest, can only be looked at (a note says which, and *n off rest* puts the sliders back). The selected group shows its **pivot** (`.cmPivot`: a cross in a ring, drag it or focus it and press the arrow keys; Shift for bigger steps; for a group fixed to a shape the arrow keys step the anchor: to the next or previous point, along the edge, or across and down the shape) and, if it is fixed to a shape, the **anchor** (`.cmAnchor`, a diamond). A **twin** is drawn with a dashed outline and its points dimmed: it is shown, not edited. A **linked** point (`.cmPoint--linked`) has a heavier ring in the accent and is not dragged: the arrow keys adjust its link instead (left and right slide it along the edge or change a follow's offset across, up and down change how far out it sits or the offset down; Shift: five times as much). Arrow keys move the selected point (Shift: bigger steps; in move mode the whole shape), Delete removes the point, Escape unselects.
- **What the pointer does on the canvas.** *Left button.* **A click on the empty grid unselects everything** and shows the end result (not while a tool is waiting for a click of its own: picking, Pose mode, weights, frames). A click on the body of a shape selects it. **Hover an outline** (within 6 pixels, on any shape): the outline **widens**, **all its points appear as small handles** and a **slightly larger handle** sits on the nearest place of the edge; on the selected shape, **a click on that large handle adds a point there** (and the press carries on as a drag of the new point; with mirror editing on, the twin's point is added too), and on any other shape the click selects it. Double-clicking an edge still adds a point. **Hover a point** of the selected shape: it grows a little. **A click on it (a press that moves less than 3 pixels) steps its handles: none (sharp), both (curve), the incoming handle only (curve, then sharp: the left one), the outgoing handle only (sharp, then curve: the right one), and round again**, one undo step each; a press that moves is a drag and leaves the handles alone. **Hover the crossing of two outlines** (the selected shape's with another shape's): a lock marker appears. **A click** inserts a point of the selected shape there (a straight edge stays straight) and **locks it onto the other shape's edge** (the lock clothes use: it follows the edge, whatever turns it); **a second click welds** the two shapes (the other shape gets a point there too, and the two are one); **a third click unlocks** (both points stay where they are). The marker is a ring (hollow, ringed with a dot, filled) that says how far the lock has got. A lock is made in the model's own space, so it waits for the sliders at rest, the front view, the base outline (no form being edited) and a shape that is edited, and it is refused with the reason for a twin or a shape at its most points. A crossing is found before a point lying on it, so the point that holds a lock is stepped through the lock, not through its handles. *Right button*: a click on the grid unselects everything; a click on a shape moves through point editor, move shape and unselected; a drag pans.
- **Alignment guides.** While a point, a shape moved whole or a pivot is dragged, thin dashed lines (`.cmGuide`) show where it **lines up** (within 3 pixels) with another shape's point, the edges or the middle of another shape's box, the mirror line or the side of a frame; with *Snap* on it also lands on that line. The *Alignment guides* layer switches both the lines and the pull off.
- **The view** is fitted when Fit to screen is pressed, when the front or back view is switched, or when something is selected that cannot be seen in the view (then to the selection, with room for its handles), and then held. **A selection that is in view leaves the view where it is**, whether it was made by a click, by the right button moving a shape on through point editor, move shape and unselected, or from the tree, and so does selecting nothing: **the grid never moves because something is being dragged**, however far a shape or a point goes. A drag that is **held at an edge or a corner** of the canvas (within 28 px of it, for a moment) pans the view that way, faster the nearer the edge, and the thing being dragged follows; the right button also pans. A new fit starts from the middle of its box.
- **Snap to grid** is a separate toggle among the Designer tools (off to begin with) and is not the same as *Snap*: *Snap* pulls points and handles to half units, to the mirror line and to the alignment guides while they are edited; *Snap to grid* makes **a shape that is moved whole** (move-shape mode) land with its top left corner on a grid line, **a pivot** land on a grid crossing, and **a frame** (moved, or a side or corner dragged) land on the lines, whatever the pointer did on the way. The lines are the ones that are drawn, so the grid size is whatever the lines show at that zoom. Points and handles are not pulled to the grid.
- **Inspector** (`.cmInspector`, in the editor toolbar) with up to nine tabs (`role="tablist"`, icon and label, Left and Right arrows move between them, the strip wraps when it is narrow): **Shape**, **Rig**, **Ranges**, **Fields**, **Forms**, **Presets**, **Colour**, **Proportions** and **Frames** (below). **The tabs follow what is open**: the model alone has no Rig tab (a rig belongs to a group) and no Forms tab (forms belong to an object, and so to its shapes); a tab that was open when it goes falls back to Shape. With nothing selected the Shape tab shows the model's counts and the **Mirror line** (a stepper: the x that mirror editing reflects about, 40 to 160, 100 to begin with). Shape tab: the name, and for an object its layer; for a shape its view (both, front only, back only), its opacity, its template, and its points as numbered chips with the four **point modes** (curve, sharp, curve then sharp, sharp then curve), add a point after the selected one, delete it, reset its handles. Below the points, for a shape: **Pair**, **Links** and **In the front / back view** (all described below). For an object, Pair too; for a group, Pair is in the Rig tab.
- **Previews** (`.cmStrip`, a region named *Previews* in the drawer, in the view being edited): the selection, or with nothing selected the whole model, at random sizes, tilts and backgrounds, **with every slider at a random value inside its range** (the slider's `rand` range when it has one); *Random* with a re-roll, *Smallest* and *Largest* as stress previews (Smallest also puts every slider at its minimum, Largest at its maximum), and three sizes. The tiles pair a shape token with a ground token so each is readable on all four backgrounds.
- **Checks** (`.cmCheck`, a region named *Checks* in the drawer): the size of a character's copy of the model, what stops Publish, which limits the model is at or past, and the **rig check** (a slider that drives nothing and slides no anchor and loosens no shape, an anchor that does not keep its group where it is drawn at rest, a shape that leaves the frame when a slider is at its minimum or maximum; at most eight lines), the **notes about forms** (a form that changes a shape which has an outline of its own for the back shows from the front only; a form with another number of points than the base is followed by anchors, links and sliders by the base outline; a field that changes the gradient of a shape that has none, or turns a radial one, or has the same amount at both ends and at rest, has no effect) and the **forms-by-views grid** (`.cmGrid`, a table with a row for each form: what it does from the front, and what becomes of it from behind).
- **Model files**: the model strip has an **Export** icon (the open model's draft) and the list of models has **Import** and **Export all**. A file (`scriblrCharacterModels`, version 1) holds drafts; an import adds each as an **unpublished draft** (a name that is taken becomes *Name (copy)*), skips what is not a valid model and says what it skipped in a notice (`.cmToast`) with a Dismiss button; a file that is not a model file is refused with an alert.

## Pairs, links and the back view

- **Pair** (`PairPanel`): *Make a twin* gives a shape, an object (with its shapes) or a group (with everything in it and under it) a **twin**: a derived mirror image about the mirror line, with no points of its own, that follows every edit of the original and every slider that moves it. A twin is marked in the tree (`.cmTwin`, the swap icon) and named *Name (twin)*. Its outline is the reflection of the original's, in the group's own space, so a pair of eyes in one group tilt with the head. A twin **group** does what its original does, mirrored, from its own parent: with the same parent for both (the torso) a lean of the torso carries both arms alike; with twin parents (left arm and forearm) it is the plain reflection. A twin has no pivot, anchor, template or point list of its own, and a slider may not drive it (use the original). *Break the pair* makes it a shape, object or group of its own, drawn as it was a moment ago: its sliders are copied mirrored (a turn the other way, recorded points moved the other way across, a bend the other way), its anchor is mirrored onto the twin of the original's shape, and its point links are not copied. Breaking a twin group breaks the twin groups under it too. Deleting an original deletes its twin ("Its twin went with it").
- **Links** (`LinksPanel`): select a point and choose **Weld** (exactly on a point of another shape), **Follow** (stay where it is, move with a point of another shape) or **Lock to an edge** (keep its place along the edge of another shape, as far out as it is now), then click the shape on the canvas (the point or the edge nearest the click; Escape stops). The link is kept on the point that follows and is solved when the model is drawn, so the clothes follow the body whatever turns it, across groups. The list shows each link, with steppers for the distance from the edge and the spot along it (or the offset of a follow) and *Unlink* (the point stays where it is drawn). A shape cannot be linked to itself, to a circle of shapes, or from a twin; a model may have 1200 links (a warning above 600). Adding or deleting a point of a shape cuts the links to it, with a note.
- **Looseness**: a shape can take its looseness from a slider (*Make a slider* makes one, 0 to 200%, resting at 100%): the distance of its links (outward from an edge, or a follow's offset) is stretched by the slider over its rest value, so 200% drapes the shirt twice as far from the body and 0% pulls it tight.
- **The back view**: the view toolbar's *Back view* turns the figure round about the mirror line. Anchors, links and sliders are the same in both views (they read the front outline, so one edit moves both). A shape shows its front outline from behind until its points are edited in the back view; it then has an **outline of its own for the back** (*Use the front outline again* undoes that). Its depth is its own in the back view once it is moved there: *In front* and *Behind* (in the panel *In the front / back view*) move it one place among the shapes shown in the view being edited, and give that view its own depths (the back follows the front until then). Recording points is done in the front view.

## The rig

The **Rig tab** is for the selected group (or the group an object or shape is in):

- **Pivot and parent**: the chain from the root as a breadcrumb (each parent a link), *Level n of 9 in the pivot chain* (a warning above 6), a **Hangs from** select (every group that is not below this one), the pivot's place, and a button that puts it in the middle of the group. Everything in the group turns and scales about the pivot.
- **Attached to a shape**: a group with a parent can be fixed to one shape of the parent or of a group above it, by one of four **anchors** (chips, `aria-pressed`): *Point* (one point of the shape; a stepper), *Edge spot* (a spot along one edge; steppers and a slider), *Slides* (along one edge, where a **slider** puts it), *Inside* (a relative place across and down the shape). **Pick on the canvas** lets a click on a shape of the parent choose it. Attaching puts the pivot on the anchor without moving the drawing. A parent move drags the group along with the anchor.
- **Motion**: the sliders that turn, scale or slide this group; each is a link that opens it in the Ranges tab.

## Sliders

The **Ranges tab** lists the model's sliders (`.cmSlider`), by default those that have to do with the selection (*This selection | All*). **New slider** offers four kinds, each a menu item that says why it is unavailable (*Select a group first*):

- **Turn the group**: turns the selected group about its pivot, −20° at the minimum and +20° at the maximum to begin with.
- **Scale**: scales the selected shape or group (80% and 120%), or the whole model about its feet when nothing is selected (*Size*); the chips say whether across, up and down, or both.
- **Move points (record)**: the canvas edits a copy of the selected shape with every slider at rest; **Set maximum** (or *Set minimum*) writes how far each point moved, **Cancel** drops it. Adding or deleting a point is not recorded.
- **Bend the shape**: a smooth bend along a length of the shape (two steppers for where it starts and ends).

A slider has a **Try it** range (the test value is only for looking and is never saved; the *n off rest* button in the bar of what is open puts them back), a **sweep** button (minimum to maximum and back, still running with reduced motion because it was asked for), steppers for its **minimum, rest and maximum** (no typed numbers), a **show to writers** toggle (an eye) and a delete that asks in place. Under them, what it drives, one row per **drive** with its own steppers for the amount at each end, and **Add what it drives**. A drive's amount is zero (or 100% for scale) at rest, the stated amount at the maximum and the other at the minimum, in straight lines between.

### Posing the two sides apart

A twin keeps the same *shape* as its original (it follows every edit of the outline, its points and what bends it), but a pose belongs to one side: **a slider that turns a group moves only its own side** (`mirror: false` on the Turn drive; the twin keeps still). Sizes and lengths (a Scale or Length drive) are measures of the body and still change both sides. The twin gets its pose from a slider of its own, which is made the moment its part is grabbed in Pose mode, or by the **Pose the other side apart** button (`.cmSides`): a second slider, *&lt;name&gt; (other side)*, with the same span and the same drives on the twin groups. A slider that moves only its own side says so (*Moves only its own side: the twin keeps still*) and has both that button and **Pose both sides together**, which makes the twin turn with it, mirrored, again. A model made before this still has sliders that turn both arms: the Ranges tab then offers **One side each (n)**, which makes every such slider move only its own side (published models stay as they are until a draft is published again). The drawing stays mirrored; only the pose is separate, so the original raises alone and the other side's slider raises the twin as the mirror image, and a forearm hanging from a twin arm follows that arm. A pair of sliders says so (*Posed apart from ...*), and **Pose both sides together** takes the other side's slider away (and its value in the presets) and makes the drives mirror again. A drive that moves points or bends a shape still follows the original side, and the button says so. A shape that shows with the slider (a crease) shows on the twin with the other side's slider. In Pose mode either side can be grabbed: a twin whose original moves only its own side is given its slider (one undo step, and the status says so); a pair posed together is turned by the one slider, the twin the other way round.

## Forms

The **Forms tab** is for an object (the open object, or the object of the open shape; open the object in the tree to choose among a group's objects). An object has **axes** (`.cmAxis`: the shape of an eye, an expression, a style), each with **forms** (`.cmFormCard`, buttons with `aria-pressed`, each with a small picture of the object in that form and *· default* on the one shown unless another is chosen). A form gives some of the object's shapes **an outline of their own**; every other shape keeps its base outline, and an empty outline hides the shape in that form. An object shows one form of every axis; when two axes give the same shape an outline, **the first axis in the list wins** (the arrows reorder axes).

- **Randomise changes it only so often** (since phase 6c): an axis has a **chance** (*Randomise changes <axis>*, a stepper in percent: 5 a click, 25 with Shift, 100 to begin with, so a model made before it rolls every axis every time). At **100** the Roll button and the writer's Randomise pick a form of the axis every time; at **0** the axis keeps the form it shows (the beard stays off, a hair cut stays the cut chosen); in between, the dice are thrown for the axis first and a form is picked only when they say so. **Rolled in** (chips, one for each genre of the model; none ticked: every genre) limits the axis to figures of those genres: a figure that has no genre, or another, never has it rolled, and the chance above applies inside the genres (a horn is rolled only for Fantasy, and then rarely). A kind of preset that sets a form of an axis with a chance below 100 or with genres is left alone by the writer's Randomise too (the axis has its own chance), as one that takes something off is. Deleting a genre takes it off every axis.
- **Choosing** a form is only for looking, like the Try it sliders: it is not saved, it counts in the *n off rest* of the bar of what is open, and *Reset test values* puts every axis back on its default form.
- **Editing**: while a form is chosen the canvas edits **that form's outline**, not the base outline: a banner in the tab and the line under the canvas say *Editing form Wide of Eye shape*, and moving a point of a shape the form does not change gives the shape an outline of its own in the form. The base outline (what anchors, links, recorded moves and the rig always read) is left alone, so a form may have another number of points than the base. Choose the default form to edit the base outline again. A shape the form hides cannot be edited (the line under the canvas says so); *Use the base outline* brings it back.
- **Add a form from the current one** copies the outlines drawn now that differ from the base; *Rename*, *Make default*, *Move earlier / later* and *Delete* (asks in place; an axis keeps at least one form) are under the chosen form, with a list of what it does to each shape (*own outline*, *base outline*, *hidden in this form*) and the buttons *Hide … in this form* and *Use the base outline for …*. *Add an axis* starts one with a single form, *Standard*.
- A **twin** follows the form its original shows (use the original's tab); in the back view a shape with an outline of its own for the back shows that one whatever the form.

## Presets

A preset is a named set of **slider values** and of **objects and groups switched off** (the *Presets tab*, `.cmPreset`). A plain preset does not keep which form is chosen; a **variant** (see *Variants* below) can. Its **kind** is a free name (Pose, Expression, Age, Build, Outfit, …) and is its slot: **applying a preset puts back whatever any other preset of the same kind controls, then sets its own**, so a pose replaces the last pose and an expression leaves the arms alone. A preset whose sliders and hidden things are what the figure shows now is marked **On** (a tick and the word, within a hundredth of a slider's unit).

- **Save the current as a preset** takes a name and a kind (the kinds in use are suggested) and keeps the sliders that are away from rest and the things switched off. Each row has **Apply**, *Update from the current*, a name field and a delete that asks in place; chips filter by kind.
- A preset that names a slider or a thing that is gone is refused at Publish; deleting a slider or a thing removes it from the presets.

## Variants

A **variant** is a preset with no owner (the model's own card lists them, the *Variant* kind by default; Age, Build and Outfit are variants too). Since phase D3a it is **a bundle of choices over the same shapes**, not a copy: besides the sliders and the things switched off it can keep **the expression of each object** (the form chosen on each axis, away from its default), **the style that is on for each object** and **the default colour of each slot**. The *Save the current as a preset* section of the Presets tab has an **Also keep** row of three chips (*Expressions*, *Styles*, *Colours*, `aria-pressed`, all off by default so a plain pose stays plain); the plus on the model's card (*Save the current look as a variant*) keeps all three. *Update from the current* keeps the parts the variant already had, and any more that are pressed. A row says what it keeps ("1 expression, 1 style, 1 colour").

- **Applying a variant** first puts back what the variants of its kind controlled (sliders, things hidden, expressions, the default colours picked, the styles turned on: every style of those objects goes off), then sets its own, then turns its styles on over that. Applying a style that sets the same axis wins over the variant's own expression of it, so a style should not control an axis a variant also sets.
- **Colours** are default colours of a slot only: a swatch of a palette is the character's own, so it cannot be bundled and *Colours* skips it. A colour already chosen keeps its brightness and opacity when a variant picks it again. A variant is **On** when every part is as it sets it (a brightness is not a pick).
- Deleting a style, an object, a slot or a default colour removes it from the variants that named it; a variant that names one that is gone is refused at Publish.

## Group states

A **state** (phase D3b) is a named switch on a **group**: *Front* or *Back*, *Open* or *Closed*, *Grip*. Each of its **options** sets several things of the group at once: **slider values** (the sliders listed under the group, or a group under it), **objects and groups switched off**, and **the form chosen on axes of the group's objects** (and the member of a family shown). A group has several states and they combine; the options of one state replace each other. It is made and chosen **only on the card of the group** in the tree, under *Poses* (the *States* section, `.dzStates`; a twin group has none, it follows its original):

- **Add a state to <group>** (a plus beside the *States* heading) makes a state named *State n* with one option, *Option 1*, that **keeps how the group looks now**. Set the group up as another setting (sliders in the Ranges tab, the eye on an object, a form in the Forms tab), then **Add an option to <state>** (the plus in the state's row) keeps that look as a new option, *Option n*.
- Each state is a row (`.dzState`: a name field, *Add an option*, *Delete state*) and each option a row under it (`.dzOption`): **Set <state> to <option>** (a play button, a tick and `aria-pressed` when it is on), a name field, **Update <option> from the current look** (a save icon) and **Delete option** (a state needs an option, so the last one's button is off). Names are typed where they are.
- An option keeps, of the look now: the group's sliders that are away from rest, the objects and groups **inside** it that are switched off (not the group itself) and the forms away from their default on its objects. **Choosing an option puts back whatever any option of the state controls** (the sliders to rest, the things shown, the forms to their default) and then sets its own, so the rest of the figure is left as it is. An option is **on** when choosing it would change nothing, so an option that sets nothing (the group as drawn) is on while the state's other sliders, things and forms are at rest.
- Which option is chosen is **test state**, like a pose: it is not saved, it counts in *n off rest* through the sliders, things and forms it sets, and *Reset test values* puts it back. A pose or a variant keeps what the sliders, things and forms come to. The previews draw a random option of each state of each group now and then.
- Deleting a slider, an object or a group removes it from the options that named it; an option that names one that is gone is refused at Publish, and so is a twin group that has states.

## Fields

A **field** (phase D3c) is a slider that changes **one property of one feature**; a **fieldset** gathers the fields of one feature for one owner. They are made **in the Fields tab** of the editor toolbar's inspector (`FieldsTab.tsx`, icon `ListIcon`), from what is selected. The slider is the model's own, so the Ranges tab lists it too (*Sets width of Plate to 50% at the minimum and 150% at the maximum*), with a Try it range and steppers for its span.

- **A feature** is one of: a **whole shape**, **one point** of a shape, **one edge** of a shape (the side between a point and the next), or a **whole object**. The tab offers what the selection can hold (`.cmFieldMake`, chips *This shape*, *Point n*, *Edge n* for the point picked on the shape, *This object*); the **owner** is picked beside them: *This object*, *Its group*, a *Variant*, or a *Style* of the open object. **Add a fieldset** makes a fieldset named after its feature with no field yet. A twin follows its original, so a feature of a twin is refused with the reason (*give the fields to the original*).
- **The properties** a feature has (`engine/props.ts`; a new field's slider shows the property itself): a shape or an object has **Position across** and **up and down** (model units), **Width** and **Height** (percent of the width and height it is drawn with, about its middle), **Rotation** (degrees, about its middle), **Curve the ends** (model units: lifts the two ends of it against its middle, a smile, or drops them, a frown; it bends whatever points the outline has, so it works under any form), **Length** (percent, 100 at rest, 0 to 1000: stretches the points that hang from a root, those with a weight, away from the roots of the shape or object, so a lock of hair grows or shortens whatever points it is drawn with; the roots stay where they are locked), **Texture** (0 to 100, 0 at rest: waves the weighted points from side to side, a little for 33, ringlets for 66, tight coils for 99; each shape waves out of step with the next), **Opacity** (percent of its own opacity), **Fill hue**, **saturation** and **lightness** (degrees and points of shift of the colour it is painted with, the lightness held inside the slot's brightness range), and, for a shape with a gradient, **Corner roundness** (how far the sharp corners are rounded, 0 to 20, added to the shape's own radius), **Gradient reach** (percent of the way across the shape at which the second colour is reached, 100 at rest), **Gradient blend** (how much of the second colour it blends to, 100 at rest), **Gradient direction** (degrees a linear gradient is turned; a radial one has none) and **Second colour hue**, **saturation** and **lightness**. A point has **Position across** and **up and down**. An edge has **Push the edge** (both its points move along its normal, outward for a positive amount). An object's properties act on every shape of the object, about the middle of the object at rest. Each property has an amount at rest (0, or 100%) and the least and most it can be.
- **A field** is a row in its fieldset (`role="group"` named *Field Width*): its name (the slider's), a **Changes** select (the other properties of the feature that have no field yet; choosing one gives the slider that property's span and amounts), the Try it range, steppers for the **amount at the minimum** and **at the maximum** (the slider's two ends; the amount at rest is the property's own), an eye (*Show to writers*, as for any slider), a button that opens the slider in the Ranges tab, and a delete (undoable). **Add a field** takes a property from the select beside it; a fieldset may not have two fields for one property.
- **Who is offered a fieldset.** An object's or a group's fieldset is offered whenever the model is. A **variant's or a style's** is offered **while that variant or style is on** (the fieldset says *Offered while Elder is on*, and *Offered now* once it is). The designer's Try it sliders always show every field; the gate is for the writer's editor, which offers the fieldsets of what the figure wears.
- **What is drawn.** At rest a field changes nothing. A shape-level field moves, scales and turns the shape's points (anchors, links and a twin follow, a twin as a mirror image; the recorded moves and bends of other sliders are applied first), so a field of a shape lets it be looked at but not edited while the slider is away from rest, as other sliders that move points do. Opacity fades with the shape (as a crease does). The colour fields shift the colour a shape is painted with (after the parts of it an object holds), shading shapes of the same object follow, and the gradient fields change the gradient a shape already has (they are noted as having no effect on one that has none). The canvas, the previews, the pictures of forms and the writer's figure all draw from the same colouring, which carries the slider values.
- **Cascades.** Deleting a shape, an object, a group, a variant or a style deletes the fieldsets bound to it or owned by it, **with their sliders**; deleting a slider takes it out of its fieldset (and out of the values presets and group states set); deleting a fieldset deletes its sliders. A slider that changes something that is gone, a fieldset whose owner or feature is gone or a twin, one that lists a slider that is not there or one that does not change a property of its feature, is refused at Publish.

## Classes

A **class** (the *Class* section at the top of the Shape tab for an object, `.cmClass`) turns one object into a **pattern** and lets you build **members** from it: *Make a class* picks what the class is, **a family** (members are interchangeable and one is shown at a time: almond, round and hooded eyes; a character uses one) or **copies** (every member is drawn, each in its own group: the left and the right hand). The pattern is **only a pattern**: once it has a member it is not drawn at all (before that it is drawn like any object), and a tag on its tree row (a stamp) and on each member (a link) says what it is.

- **Add a member** makes an object right after the pattern (or the last member) **in the pattern's group**, with a shape for every shape of the pattern. Those shapes **follow the pattern**: they have no points of their own, so an edit of the pattern, or a shape added to it, reaches every member at once. A member can be moved to another group with the *Group* dropdown of its panel (the other hand's group).
- **A member may**: give a shape **an outline of its own** (move a point of the shape: the *Follow the pattern again* button drops it), **add shapes of its own** (with the + beside the object), **choose its own form on each of the pattern's axes** (its Forms tab lists the pattern's axes, read-only, and the choice is the member's), and **leave out an inherited shape** (*Hide in this member*). A shape that follows the pattern has no copy or delete button in the bar of what is open.
- **The ghost** (the *Pattern ghost and frames* layer in the view toolbar) draws a dashed line, behind a member's shape that has an outline of its own, where the pattern's outline is.
- A family shows **one member at a time**; the pattern's panel lists the members as chips, and *Show this member* on a member's panel chooses it. Which member is shown is test state like the chosen forms (not saved, counted in *n off rest*), and a selected pattern or member is always drawn so it can be looked at and edited. The previews choose a member at random.
- **Detach from the class** (asks in place) copies what the member follows once: it keeps its look, with the pattern's outlines and axes as its own, and stops following. **Delete the class** detaches every member. Deleting the pattern detaches the members first (they keep their look). Selecting a shape that follows and pressing Delete is refused: hide it in the member instead.
- A **twin** of a member follows it, and goes off with it when another member of the family is shown.

## Styles

A **style** is a preset that **belongs to** an object or a group (the *Styles* section at the end of the Forms tab, for an object and for a group; it is listed in the Presets tab too, as *Style of Eye*). *Save the current look as a style* keeps, for what its owner holds: the sliders listed under it that are away from rest, the things inside it that are switched off, the forms chosen away from their default on its objects (and on members), and **which member of a family is shown**. A group's style can hide the objects in it. Applying a style sets those choices and puts back whatever any other style of the same owner and kind controls, so a style replaces the last style of its owner and leaves the rest of the figure as it is. A style that names a form, a member, a slider or a thing that is gone is refused at Publish.

Since phase D3a a style **of an object** can also **rearrange the object's shapes**: the Presets tab has a *Shapes of <object>* section while an object (or one of its shapes) is open, listing the shapes **front first**, each with *Move forward*, *Move back* and an eye that switches that shape off (a twin goes with its original), and *Back in the tree's order*. These are test state like the sliders (they count in *n off rest*); *Save the current look as a style* keeps the order the object's shapes are drawn in (`order`, back to front; shapes the order leaves out go behind the ones it lists, and what lies between them and other objects' shapes stays where it was) and the shapes switched off (a style's `hidden` may name shapes of its object). A twin object is drawn in its original's order.

A style can also **hold parts of a colour** while it is on: the *Colour locks* button of a style's row opens the same locks as the Colour tab (see *Colour*). A style that sets nothing of the look (such as one that only holds colour parts) is **On once it has been applied** (and off again when another of its kind is), because there is nothing else to recognise it by.

## Colour

A model is **gray as saved**: a shape that is painted from no colour slot, or whose slot has no colour chosen, is drawn in a neutral gray, and a fixed colour in a model is a gray (white for an eye white, black, a dark gray). Hue only ever comes from a slot's **default colours** or from the **palette** of the character. The *Colour tab* (the sixth, `BucketIcon`) has seven parts:

- **This shape** (or *Every shape of <object>* when an object is selected): the **slot** the shape is painted from, as chips (`No slot` and one per slot). A shape with no slot has a **Fixed gray** stepper (5 per step, Shift 25; *Neutral gray* puts it back). A twin follows its original's paint, and a shape that follows a class's template its template shape's, until it is given a slot of its own.
- **Locked parts of <object>** (phase D3a; for an open object or the object of an open shape): for each colour slot the object's shapes are painted from, the **hue**, the **saturation** and the **lightness** can each be **locked** (a lock button, `aria-pressed`, that starts at the value the colour has now) at one value, or left **free**. A locked part **replaces that part of whatever colour is chosen for the slot** (a default colour or a swatch of the palette, and whatever *Roll* gives), for the shapes of this object only; a locked lightness is held inside the slot's brightness range. A slot with **no colour chosen stays gray**, whatever is locked: the model as saved is gray. A twin holds what its original holds; **the object's own locks win over those of a style that is on**, which only locks the parts the object leaves free. In *Test colours* below, the brightness slider is put away for a slot whose lightness the open object holds, and a line says which parts it holds.
- **Test colours**: the colours of the *test character* (never saved, like the Try it sliders; they count in *n off rest* and *Reset test values* puts them back to gray). Pick a **slot** to colour, then **Gray**, one of its **default colours** (round swatches, an `.cmSwatch` ring on the chosen one) or a **swatch of the palette**; a **Brightness** slider (inside the slot's range) and, when the slot has room for it, an **Opacity** slider appear. A default colour keeps its own brightness until the slider moves it; a swatch is at the slot's resting brightness. The **Genre** dropdown limits the default colours shown, and **Roll colours and forms** (also the dice in the toolbar) rolls the whole look.
- **Palette**: the swatches a character will own, each a **hue** (the app's `ColorRange`) and a **saturation** slider (no typed numbers; brightness and opacity belong to the slot), 1 to 10 of them. This is a *test copy*: **Use as the starter palette** saves it in the model (the palette a new character starts with) and **Start over from the starter palette** drops the copy. Editing a swatch recolours every slot that uses it.
- **Slots of the model**: each slot opens to its **default colours** (round swatches; click one to edit it: a name, **hue**, **saturation** and **lightness** sliders and the **genres** it fits, delete asks nothing because a colour is cheap), its **brightness** and **opacity** ranges (a smallest, a resting and a largest value, one stepper each, the smallest never passing the resting one) and a delete that asks in place and says how many shapes will go gray. A new model starts with the **standard slots** (Skin, Hair, Eye, Lip, Clothing, Shoes, with the default colours of the portrait engine's own swatches; the fantasy skins and eyes fit only Fantasy); an older model has *Add the standard slots*.
- **Genres of the model**: tags (Fiction, Fantasy and Non-fiction to begin with, at most 12) a default colour may fit; a colour with none fits every genre. A character made for a project of a genre will draw its random colours from those that fit (the project's own genre setting arrives with the writer's editor).

**Rolling** (*Roll the look*, a dice in the bar of what is open and in the Colour tab) gives the test character a default colour that fits the genre or, 30 % of the time (never for a skin or an eye), a swatch at a brightness in the middle half of the slot's range, a **form on every axis** and the **member of every family**. A **lock** (in the bar of what is open for a group or an object, `aria-pressed`) keeps a group or object, with everything in it, as it is: its forms, its family's member and the colours of the slots it is painted from. A lock is not saved and does not count as off rest.

**On the canvas** every shape is drawn in the test colours, in the previews (each tile has a palette and a colouring of its own; Smallest and Largest put brightness and opacity at the ends of each slot's range) and in the pictures of forms. Shapes have **no outline of their own** (phase D4): the only outline is the **selection ring**, drawn round the selected shape in the **accent colour** (a narrow `accent` stroke over a wider `on-accent` one, `.cmRing`; the pair the contrast audit already holds at 4.5:1 as *Text on the accent*, so the ring reads on a shape of any colour whatever accent the user has picked). A selected object or group is shown by dimming everything else. The *Shape outlines* layer of the view toolbar shows or hides the ring and the hover outline. A helper shape, which has no fill, keeps a dotted line so it can still be picked.

## Paint effects

Since phase 4b a shape can be more than one flat colour. The **Paint** section of the Colour tab (under *This shape*, for a selected shape) holds what is done to its colour; all of it is the model's own data, saved with the shape, and the test character only chooses the colours underneath it.

- **Tone**: *Darker*, *Base* or *Lighter* (a step is 12 darker or 9 lighter in lightness, held to 2 to 98 %).
- **Derived tone**: the shape is a *Shadow*, a *Mid tone* or a *Highlight* of its own colour (a brow is a shadow of the hair), by an **Amount** stepper (0.02 to 0.8, a hundredth a step, Shift a tenth); *Own colour* takes it away.
- **Gradient**: *No gradient*, or a direction (*Down*, *Up*, *Left*, *Right*, *Radial*, from the middle outward); the second colour is *Darker of itself* or **another slot's colour** as the test character has chosen it.
- **Blend mode**: *Normal*, *Multiply*, *Screen* or *Overlay*.
- **Clip to** and **Cut by**: a shape can be kept inside another (*Not clipped* or a chip for each shape of the object and every helper), and other shapes can cut a hole in it (a chip each, at most 8). **Pick on the canvas** does the same by clicking a shape (Escape stops); the status line under the canvas says what is being picked.
- **Helper shape**: never drawn in the previews and pictures of forms; on the canvas it is a dotted outline with no fill, so it can still be picked and edited. A helper that nothing clips to or is cut by is named in the Checks.
- **Add shading**: a **shadow**, **mid tone** or **highlight** band along the **left**, **right**, **top** or **bottom** edge of the shape, whichever edge faces that way. It is a new shape in the object whose six points are **locked to that edge** (edge links), so it follows the shape wherever sliders, forms and poses take it, and it lies just above the object's top shape. A *shading shape* is a tone of its slot's colour at full strength, kept inside the other shapes of its object (the union of those that are drawn, not helpers); it shows *Shading* instead of tone and gradient, with its kind and an **Amount** stepper. At most 10 in an object (a twin's is not counted); a shape that has a twin gets the twin of its band on the other side. The band follows the edge of the shape's **base outline**, as anchors and links do: a form that draws the shape another way still shows the band along the base's edge (inside the drawn shape, since it is kept inside).

A shape that **takes its paint from another** (a twin, a shape that follows a class's template) uses all of these from that shape, and what it clips to or is cut by is turned into its own twin or member's shape; the section says where the paint comes from instead of letting it be changed, until the shape is given a **colour slot of its own**. Breaking a pair and detaching a member **keep** the paint the shape had.

**Forms may change paint.** In the Forms tab each shape of the object has *Colour slot* and *Tone* lists for the chosen form (*Same as the shape* leaves its own; *No slot (gray)* paints a gray; *Use the paint of <shape>* puts it back). The first axis, in order, whose chosen form has a look for the shape wins; a member of a class takes the template's looks under its own choice of form. Since phase D4 it also has a **Corners** list (*Same as the shape*, *Sharp*, *Slightly rounded*, *Rounded*, *Very rounded*, *Fully rounded*: the radius the shape's sharp corners are rounded to in that form). A form cannot change a gradient, a blend or a clip.

There is **no outline** setting and shading is always at full strength: the strokes on the canvas are only the Designer's chrome.

### Corner roundness (phase D4)

The **Corners** stepper of the Paint section (*Corner roundness*, *Sharp* at 0 up to 20; Shift steps by 5) rounds the **sharp corners** of the selected shape: each sharp point with a straight side either side of it is replaced, in the drawing, by two points that radius along those sides joined by a short curve. The radius is held to 45 % of the shorter side, so neighbouring corners never meet. A curve point, a sharp point beside a curved side and the two ends of an open outline stay as drawn.

- **Only the drawing changes.** The shape keeps the points it was drawn with: editing, anchors, links, point drives and the hover handles all read them, so rounding never moves a link.
- **One radius per shape.** A **form** may give a shape another radius (the Corners list above); an **authored field** may add to it (*Corner roundness* in the properties of a shape or an object, 0 to 20, held between none and the most); a **style** keeps the form it chose, and so the radius; a **twin** and a **shape that follows a template** are rounded like the shape they copy (the section says so and offers no stepper).
- A model with no rounded corner pays nothing for the feature.

### Fill patterns (phase D4)

A **fill pattern** is built from shapes, then applied to a base shape: the **marks** (stripes, dots, scales) are ordinary shapes drawn by hand, and from then on they are **kept inside the base** (clipped to it, as a shape clipped to another) and **switched on and off together**. There is no tiling, size or angle: a mark is where it was drawn.

- **Make one**: select the base shape, and in *Fill patterns* (the bottom of the Paint section) choose its marks from the other shapes of the same object (a twin, a helper shape, a shading shape, a shape that is a mark already or the base of a pattern is not offered), give it a name and press *Make a fill pattern on <shape>*. The marks are moved to just after the base in the object and lifted to its depth, so they are drawn over it. A mark that has a clip of its own keeps it.
- **A pattern row** (`.cmPattern`, a group named *Fill pattern <name>*) has the name, an eye (*Switch off / Switch on*, a test choice like the eye of an object: a preset, a variant or a group state keeps it), a delete (the marks stay, as ordinary shapes) and the marks as chips (press one to take it out, an unpressed chip to add it; a pattern keeps at least one mark). A mark shows a small grid badge in the tree and says in its Paint section that it belongs to a pattern; the pattern is changed from its base.
- A shape is **a base or a mark, not both**, so marks never clip to each other in a circle. A twin follows the mark it mirrors (clipped to the twin of the base, switched off with it); breaking the pair keeps the clip. Deleting the base deletes the pattern; deleting a mark takes it out; deleting the last mark deletes the pattern.
- Hiding a pattern's id is how it is switched off, so `hidden` in a preset or a group option may name patterns as well as objects, groups and shapes.

### Worn under (phase 6b)

A garment can be **worn under** others, so a shirt never shows outside a jacket and underwear never shows outside the clothes. It is set **in the Rig tab, for the selected object** (the *Worn under* section, `WearSection.tsx`): the other clothing objects (the *Clothing* layer, not twins or class members) and the families whose template is clothing are chips (*Jacket*, *Coats (family)*; press to put it under, press again to take it out). Nothing else about the object changes.

- **What is drawn.** While any of what it is worn under is **shown**, every shape of the garment (a shading shape too; a helper is never drawn) is **trimmed to the union of their outlines**: the shapes' own outlines, ignoring their cut-outs, so an open neckline still shows what is under it. With none of them shown (switched off, hidden by a preset, a group state or a frame, or by a form) nothing is trimmed and the garment is drawn whole. A family counts through whichever of its members is shown. All the shapes of an object share one SVG clip (`-w-<object>`), in a group around the path (and around a shading shape's own group).
- **Twins and members.** A twin object is worn under what its original is, each on its own side (a left sleeve under the left sleeve of the jacket); breaking the pair keeps the list. A member of a class that names nothing follows the template's list, and keeps it when it is detached; naming a garment gives it a list of its own.
- **Refusals and checks.** A garment cannot be worn under itself, under something that is worn under it already (each would trim the other), or under more than 24 things; a twin is changed on its original. A list that names something that is gone is refused at Publish (*Object Shirt is worn under something that does not exist*; deleting an object or a family takes it out of the lists), and two garments that are each worn under the other (by an imported file) get a line in the Checks.
- It shows in the canvas, the previews, the pictures of forms and every figure a writer sees, because they all draw through the same items.

## Proportions

Since phase 5a a model has a **skeleton of measured lengths** and says how far a writer may change each proportion. The **Proportions** tab (the body icon) holds both; the Rig tab says how an object fits the body, and the Ranges tab has the Length slider.

- **Bones**: a bone runs from the **pivot of a group** to the **pivot of another group** or to a **point of a shape**. *Add a bone* starts one from the selected group to a group that hangs from it; its start is chosen from the original groups (a twin follows its original), its end by a list, a stepper for the point, or *Pick its end on the canvas* (a marker on every group's pivot, or a click on a shape, which takes the point nearest the click; Escape stops). Each bone reads **live**, with the Try it sliders: its length in units (the model's own space, before the whole-model Height), in **heads** and as a **percent of its length at rest**. *Use as the head* makes a bone the unit: the tab then says how many heads tall the figure stands (the same at any Height).
- **Length sliders**: *Add a Length slider* on a bone (or *Length of a bone* in the Ranges tab's New slider and Add what it drives menus) makes a slider whose drive stretches the bone's group **along the bone** (a percent of its length at rest at the slider's minimum and maximum, 80 and 120 to begin with) and **everything that hangs from it follows**; the other side's twin follows too. A bone that has no length at rest, because its two places are on top of each other, cannot be stretched: the Checks say so.
- **What a writer may choose**: for every slider that is offered to writers, two steppers, *Writer's smallest* and *Writer's largest*, inside the slider's own range. The resting value is always allowed, so the steppers stop there; a span that reaches both ends of the slider is no limit, and the round arrow lets writers choose anywhere again. The same steppers are on the slider in the Ranges tab. Random previews stay inside this span when the slider has no span of its own.
- **How an object fits the body** (Rig tab, for the selected object): *Scales with its group* (the default), *Keeps its size* (its group carries and turns it, but the scale of the group and of the groups above it is not applied to its drawing: the eyes keep their size when the head grows) or *Stretches to a shape* (a list or *Pick on the canvas*: it grows and moves as that shape's box does since rest, and does not turn). Only the drawing changes: anchors, links and recorded moves read the outline as the group holds it. A twin fits as its original does. The Checks name every object that uses a mode other than the default.
- **Age and build** are presets: *Age* (Infant, Child, Teen, Adult, Elder) and *Build* (Slim, Average, Broad, Heavy) set the Height, Head size, arm lengths and shoulder width sliders together.

## Frames

A **frame** is a named crop of the figure: a rectangle in the model's space and the groups and objects left out when the figure is seen through it (a bust leaves out the legs). The **Frames** tab (the card icon) lists them; while it is open every frame is a rectangle on the canvas (`.cmFrame`: dashed, solid with eight handles for the one being edited).

- **A size is never typed**: *Edit on the canvas* selects a frame, then **drag its border** to move it and **its handles** to resize it (the opposite side stays, and a frame is never smaller than 8 units); with the border focused the arrow keys nudge it by 1 (**Alt**: 5) and **Shift** with them resizes it. *Add a frame* starts one round the selection (the whole figure when nothing is selected), in the view being edited; from behind a frame is mirrored about the axis, so it stays on the same part of the body.
- **Leaves out**: chips for every group and object (`aria-pressed`).
- **View through**: the canvas is cropped to the frame, on the **card colour**, without what the frame leaves out; editing is off while looking through; *Whole figure* goes back. The previews have the same choice (*Whole* and a chip per frame): every tile is cropped to the frame on the card colour.
- **Card colour**: hue, saturation and lightness sliders for a test colour (never saved); *Use as the starter card colour* keeps it as the model's `tile`, the colour new characters start with, and *Start over from the starter colour* drops it.

## Bridges

A **bridge** is skin between two parts, as at an elbow, a shoulder or a wrist, so the body does not read as doll parts. It is a shape of four points locked to the edges of the two parts it joins; its two sides leave one part along the direction of that part's edge and arrive at the other along the direction of its edge, so the outline runs on smoothly through the bend however far the parts have turned.

- **Add a bridge** (the Links section of the Shape tab, the bridge icon): click four spots on the canvas: one on the edge of the first part where one side of the bridge leaves it, one on the other part where that side arrives, then the other side (an edge of either part, then the other part). Each click goes to the edge nearest it. The bridge goes into the object of the second part, just above its top shape, painted as that part is, and when both parts have twins the other side gets the twin of the bridge. Escape stops; a click on the wrong part says what it wants.
- **Leaves along the edge** (a chip on each edge-locked point in the Links list): any edge link can leave its point along its edge, not only a bridge's. A side between two such points locked to *different* shapes is a smooth run from one edge to the other; a side between two on the same shape (the end of a bridge inside a part) keeps its own handles. The Checks say when a point has the flag but no partner.
- A bridge is an ordinary shape: it can be moved in the order, given a slot, shaded, and faded (below).

## Creases

Any shape can **show with a slider**: fully hidden when the slider is at one value, fully drawn at another, in proportion between (either way round, so a shape can also fade out). The **Shows with a slider** row of the Paint section (Colour tab) has the slider and two steppers, *hidden at* and *shown at*, inside the slider's own span; it starts hidden at the slider's resting value and shown at its largest. A crease in a bend is a shading band along the inside of the joint that is faint until the elbow is bent. A twin and a shape that follows a template fade as the shape they copy does, and a shape's fade goes with it when its slider is deleted. The Ranges tab lists the shapes a slider fades under it, with a way to each. A faded shape is still there to be selected and edited: it is only faint.

## Fall

Hair, scarves, capes, tails and sleeves **hang under their own weight**.

- **Weights** (the weight icon among the Designer tools, or the stepper under *Points* in the Shape tab): with the tool on, a click on a point of the selected shape steps its weight (none, a quarter, half, three quarters, full, and round to none), Enter does the same on the point that has focus and the arrow keys step it; a ring round a point shows its weight (bigger and stronger the heavier it is). A weighted point is free to hang; a point with no weight holds still.
- **A chain**: a run of weighted points hangs from the unweighted point before it and, in a closed outline, from the one after it too (the run is shared between the two, so both sides of a strip hang). **Each segment keeps its length**: its direction turns from the way it was drawn toward the way things fall, by the point's weight and the strength of the fall, so the ends never stretch or shrink. A fall to the *back* also narrows what hangs, as it would seen from the front.
- **Strength**: *Let the shape fall* (New slider and Add what it drives in the Ranges tab) makes a slider whose drive lets the shape hang from none at rest to all of the way at the maximum (the steppers change that); the writer's limit steppers cap how far a writer may let it fall, as for any slider. *Let &lt;object&gt; fall* adds the drive for every shape of the selected object that has weighted points. The Ranges tab refuses it until the shape has weighted points, and the Checks say when a shape falls but nothing holds it up, or has weights that nothing lets fall. While the slider is away from rest the shape is shown, not edited, like a shape whose points a slider moves.
- **The side**: chips among the Designer tools (*Down*, *Left*, *Right*, *Behind*) choose the side things fall to, for looking: straight down, toward the picture's left or right, or behind the figure. It is a test choice like the forms (counted in "off rest", put back by Reset, rolled by Roll unless something that hangs is locked, drawn at random in the previews), and a preset or style keeps it when it is not down (a style only when something in its scope hangs). From behind the picture's left is the figure's right. A twin hangs the same way as its original, mirrored.

## Hands

The **Hands and fingers** section of the Rig tab (`HandSection.tsx`) hangs hands from the bottom of the selected group's lowest shape, usually the forearm. Every part is a group with an object and a shape, painted as the shape it hangs from, so it can be reshaped and re-wired like anything else.

- **Add a finger** (three joints) and **Add a short finger** (two): a chain of joints, each hung from the bottom of the one before, and one **Curl** slider that turns every joint toward the thumb side (the front view cannot show a finger folding toward the viewer, so a curl is a sideways bend); the last joints close further, as a real finger does.
- **Add a thumb**: two joints on the side edge of the shape (an *Edge* anchor), toward the middle of the figure, with its own wider range: **Thumb swing** (out from the palm), **Thumb curl** and **Thumb across** (folding over the palm).
- **Add a whole hand**: a palm, four fingers of three joints, the thumb, and the sliders of all of them with a **Finger spread**, fifteen groups, objects and shapes and eight sliders. When the arm has a twin the other hand is built too and every hand slider is made apart, so each hand is posed on its own. **Pose the other hand apart** does that for a hand built before the arm was twinned.
- A slider carries a **role** (`curl-index`, `spread`, `swing-thumb`...; the other side's has `~` added) that the starter poses look for. The Check panel says once, not for every fingertip, that many groups are more than six levels deep.
- **Add the standard hand poses** (Rig tab, and the Presets tab): Open, Fist, Point, Pinch and Grip as presets of kind **Hand**, and the same for the other hand as kind **Hand (other side)**, so each hand takes one pose on its own. Poses already there are left alone. The previews draw the hands in these poses.

## Grip points

A **grip point** is the spot of a hand where a held item is taken, and the way it points (0 = straight down, positive toward the picture's right). It is carried by its group, so it moves and turns with the hand. The section lists the grips on the selected group and what is under it: *Add a grip point* (the middle of the group's lowest shape), a name, **Across**, **Down** and **Points** steppers, **Held** (the grip shown as held now), *Make it on the other hand* (mirrored, on the twin group) and a delete that asks in place. The canvas draws a dot and a short line for each (`.cmGrip`, accent when held) while a grip is held, in the Rig and Presets tabs and in Pose mode. A hand pose can **hold with** a grip (a select in the Presets tab): applying it makes that grip the held one, and applying another pose of the same kind lets go. In Pose mode dragging a grip **reaches** with the arm. A grip on a group that is gone, or a pose that names a grip that is gone, stops Publish.

## Show and hide

The **eye** (the top-right button of a card, in its hide mode; `aria-pressed`: pressed while shown) switches a group or an object off for looking: its shapes are not drawn on the canvas or in the previews, the card is dimmed, and it still does its part in the rig (anchors and links read it). Switching a group off hides the groups hung under it. Like the Try it sliders it is not saved with the model; a preset keeps it. It counts in *n off rest*.

## Pose mode

The hand among the Designer tools (**Pose mode**, `aria-pressed`) lets the figure be grabbed instead of edited. Every drag is the inverse of what a slider does, so the sliders follow (watch the Ranges tab) and the result is only test values; *Save as preset* in the **pose bar** (`.cmPoseBar`) keeps a pose as a preset of kind Pose (without the hidden things). Nothing here changes the model.

- **Drag a part** to turn its group about the pivot: the slider whose Turn drive turns that group (or the nearest group above it that has one) is set from the angle the pointer sweeps, held to its range. A ring (`.cmJoint`) marks each pivot that can turn, on both sides of a pair. Grabbing the part of a twin whose original moves only its own side makes the slider of that side first (*Pose the other side apart* does the same from the Ranges tab); a pair posed together turns with the one slider.
- **Drag a square** (`.cmScaleHandle`) on a group that has a Scale slider to scale it; **drag a circle** (`.cmSlideHandle`) on a group fixed along an edge by a slider to slide it.
- **Reach**: with the *Reach* button on, or **Alt** held, drag a hand or a foot and the limb bends at its joints (the Turn sliders of the grabbed group and the groups above it, at most six; the joints of the other side that lack a slider are given theirs) to bring that point to the pointer, within each slider's range; a target out of reach leaves the limb stretched toward it.
- **Escape** in the middle of a drag puts everything back; *Reset pose* puts the sliders at rest. The pointer is only a shortcut: every pose is reachable with the Ranges tab's sliders.

## The writer's own models (phase 7a)

The Designer is not only the admin's. A writer has a **library of their own models**, kept apart from the ones Scriblr ships and the admin designs, and designs them in **the same console**: *My models* in the Characters console (the mannequin icon on its bar, which is there even before the project has a character; the region is named *My models*). The cast list and the character's own sections give way to it, and so does the character's Save cluster (the Designer has its own).

- **One library for every project.** The models are kept in one file in the app's data folder (`my-models.json`, next to `character-models.json`), in the same shape: a draft that saves like any other (the Save button, and autosave when it is on), published as numbered versions (*Publish*, *Unpublish*, *Discard changes*, *Versions*), at most ten earlier versions. Every id starts with `my-`, which is how a character knows which library a model is from; the server refuses a save that has another. A name must be free among the library's own published models (the catalog's do not count).
- **Starting a model.** With none yet the console says so and offers **Duplicate a Scriblr model**, **New model** (the blank starter) and **Import** (a model file). With some, the same three are among the model level's tools (the mannequin icon, the plus and the import arrow, in the list of all the models); there is no *Add the basic model*, since the basic adult is one of the models that can be duplicated. *Duplicate a Scriblr model* opens a list of the models the catalog has **published** (name, shapes, version; a draft the admin has not published is not offered) and keeps a **copy as a draft of the writer's own**, named so that it does not repeat a name in the library; the original is never touched. Escape or a click outside closes the list. *Export* writes a model file and *Import* reads one, as in the admin's.
- **Using one.** A model of the library that is published can be chosen for a character in the same places as the catalog's: *Make a mannequin*, *Use another model* and *Start with* beside the plus (all in the Characters page) list it after Scriblr's models, marked *(mine)*. A character that uses it keeps its **own copy** in the project, so editing or publishing the model again changes no character until *Use this model* is pressed (the same rule as for the catalog's models).
- **What it is not (yet).** There is no review or hand-in to the admin, and the whole designer is shown: the limits are the admin's.

## The basic model

**Adult (basic)** is the model that comes with Scriblr: an adult in everyday clothes that a character can be made from before any model has been designed. A fresh install has it **published** (the server adds it the first time the app starts, once: a model that is deleted does not come back), and **Add the basic model** (the mannequin icon among the model level's buttons, and in the writer's Mannequin panel when nothing is published) adds it to a catalog that has none of it. It is data, not code: `backend/app/seed/basic_model.json`, written by `frontend/scripts/seed-model` (`npx vite-node scripts/seed-model/run.ts`), which builds it from the old portrait engine's exact shapes and the mockup's body, and which can be run again to change it.

- **The face** is the old drawing: the head, ears, eyes, brows, nose and mouth, each an object with one **form** for every option of the old engine (13 head shapes, 12 ears, 14 eyes, 13 brows, 14 noses, 13 mouths). The old outlines were curves sampled at many points; they are **fitted again with a fifth of the points** (each outline stays within about 0.16 of a unit of the old one, on a head 54 units tall). A left and a right outline that mirror each other are one shape and its twin (the eyes, the brows, the ears: each an object and its twin object, so *Eye size*, *Eye spacing* and *Eye tilt* move each eye about its own middle). The old drawing lit both eyes from the upper left; the twin's highlight is the mirror of the first.
- **The body** is the mockup's mannequin, point for point: the neck, torso, arms, hands, legs and feet, with the bridges of skin between the parts (trapezius, obliques, hip, deltoid, elbow, wrist, thigh), grouped and rigged (a twin of the left arm and the left leg), with sliders for the head, the shoulders, the waist and the hips, the arms, the legs and the face (*Smile* uses *Curve the ends*).
- **The presets**: nine **Age**s (infant to elderly: the figure's height, the head against it, the legs, and how large the eyes, nose, mouth and ears read), four **Build**s and thirteen **Expression**s (the old engine's: how far the lids are open, how high and how steeply the brows sit, the curve of the mouth, the tilt of the head). An open mouth with teeth, which the old engine drew for a laugh or a surprise, is not drawn: the lips only curve.
- **The hair** (since phase 6c-2) is the mockup's: the head is cut into **six planes** (front, top, left side, right side, back, nape) and, for the planes that have them, **three sides** (left, centre, right), fourteen **sections** in all, each an object of the *hair* layer (*Hair: Front, centre*, ...) whose pieces are **rooted on the edge of the skull** (the points at their roots are locked to it, so a piece follows the head). Every section has its own **Length** and **Texture** sliders (the sliders of the Length and Texture properties; the length slider runs from no hair at all to ten times what is drawn at rest, and the left and right planes may be shortened to nothing), a **Cut** axis (blunt, rounded, layered, tapered ... the section's shape, as forms) and, for the planes behind the head, a shade; one **Hair fall** slider lets the back and the sides hang. Alone, a section is as the cut of the default adult leaves it: a short cut. A **centre** section whose left and right are switched off spreads over all three. **Hairstyles** are fourteen presets of the kind *Hair style* that set the sections together (what is long, what is textured, what is cut how, and which are left off): Short, Buzzed, Pixie, Undercut, Mohawk, Shaved on one side, Bob, Shoulder-length, Long and straight, Long and wavy, Curls, Coils, Afro and Bald. **Randomise leaves a section's length, texture and cut where they are** (a style sets them together; rolled one by one they would cut every section differently).
- **Facial hair** is two objects of the *hair* layer, **Beard** and **Moustache**, each with a **Style** axis of the old engine's fourteen options (none, stubble, moustache, handlebar, horseshoe, goatee, circle beard, Van Dyke, soul patch, chin strap, mutton chops, short, full, long), off to begin with, and fourteen presets of the kind *Facial hair* that choose both together (a goatee has a moustache over it). They are drawn in the hair slot's colour, as the old engine drew them without a colour of their own. The axes are rolled **15 percent** of the time, so a Randomise gives a face a beard or a moustache now and then, and the presets are left out of it.
- **The clothes** (phase 6c-3b) are on it to begin with (a shirt with short sleeves, trousers and shoes, and underwear under them) and are the mockup's, **draped**: every point of a garment follows a point or an edge of the body, at an offset and a thickness, so a garment is fitted to whatever body the sliders make, and a **looseness** slider for the top (*Top: how loose*) and the trousers moves them off or onto the body. The shirt has **fifteen necklines** (the old portrait's: crew, V, deep V, scoop, wide scoop, boat, square, sweetheart, henley, polo, mock, turtle, cowl, off the shoulder and strapless, drawn again to fit the mannequin): the neckline is a hole cut out of the shirt (a form of it for each, every point of it locked to the chest) with a collar round it, and the things a neckline brings (the buttons and placket of a henley, the flaps of a polo shirt, the drape of a cowl, the roll of a mock neck and a turtleneck) shown only in their own. Each also comes as a ready **Top** (strapless and off the shoulder have no sleeves). **Sleeves** are none, short or long. The **outer layer** is a **jacket** and a **coat** (open or buttoned, with lapels, long sleeves, and a coat that reaches the thighs), a **skirt** (mini, knee-length, midi, maxi) and a **dress** (round, V, scoop, boat or square neckline) are off until chosen (the Extras of the writer's first step), and **underwear** (top and bottom) is worn under whatever is put on. A shirt and its sleeves are worn **under** a jacket and a coat, so they never show outside them, and underwear is trimmed to the clothes over it. Six ready **Outfit** presets take things off and put them on (Everyday, Jacket, Coat, Skirt and top, Dress, Underwear only). Randomise never changes a garment (the shirt's neckline and the sleeves are rolled like any form; the jacket, coat, skirt and dress never). The clothes have slots of their own: *Clothing* (the shirt, the dress), *Trousers and skirts*, *Jacket and coat*, *Underwear* and *Shoes*.
- **The held items** (phase 6c-3b) are the old drawing's, one of fourteen for each hand: a staff, a crooked staff, a crystal staff, a wand, a book (closed and **open**), a scroll (rolled and **unrolled**: the open one is new), a lantern, a torch, a candle, a goblet, a flower and a quill. Each hand has an **item of its own** (*Held item (left hand)* and *Held item (right hand)*: an object with one axis of forms, off until chosen, so the writer reaches it from the Extras of the first step; Randomise never changes it), in a **group hung from the hand**, so the item goes where the hand goes. The group turns about the **grip** (the middle of the palm; each hand has a grip point) by a slider, *Item angle*, which a pose uses to keep the item upright whatever the arm does; a band of skin over the item is the fingers. The items are drawn a quarter larger than the old portrait drew them and none rises more than about 34 units above the hand (a staff is held a good way up its shaft), so that an item held up beside the head stays inside the bust. The old drawing's literal colours became slots: *Held item* (the item's own colour), *Wood*, *Paper and parchment*, *Flame* (the flames of a torch and a candle, and the glow of a lantern and a wand), *Drink* (a goblet's), and a crystal is a *Gem*. **Each arm is posed on its own** now: *Arm raise*, *Elbow bend* and *Wrist turn* have one slider for the left arm and one for the right ("(left)" and "(right)"), and Randomise moves them only a little. Four presets of the kind **Pose** (*Arms down*, *Left hand up*, *Right hand up*, *Both hands up*) raise a hand beside the head (the arm up to the side, the elbow bent as far as it goes) and turn the item upright. A preset that sets a slider Randomise never moves (the item angle: its random span is a single value) is the writer's to choose, so Randomise draws no Pose.
- **The fantasy parts and the necklaces** (phase 6c-3a) are the old drawing's, each with every one of its options: **Fantasy ears** (pointed, elf, long elf, goblin, bat, fins) and **Eye style** (glowing, solid colour, slit pupils, goat pupils, blank white) are axes of the ears and the eyes themselves (they win over the ear shape and the eye shape when chosen, and only change what differs); **Extra eyes** (a third eye, four, six, two on the cheeks, two at the temples), **Horns** (nine, with stag antlers), **Crown** (circlet, gem circlet, diadem, tiara, spiked, royal), **Laurel wreath**, **Flower crown**, **Hood** (up, deep, pointed, cowl, and down, which hangs behind the body) and **Necklace** (a fine chain, choker, beads, pearls, pendant, gem amulet, locket, medallion, torc) are objects of their own, off until chosen. Every one of these axes is **rolled only for the Fantasy genre, and then 6 percent of the time**, so a random character stays a person unless the writer picks Fantasy. Their colours are slots of their own (*Horns*, *Metal and jewellery*, *Gems*, *Hood*, *Leaves*, *Blossoms*) with the old drawing's lists. One eye (a cyclops, which would make the two eyes of the face one) and two-coloured eyes (the twin of an eye cannot have a colour of its own) are not in the model.
- **Frames**: Full body, Bust, Face, Hands, Feet. The bust and the face leave room above the head, so a child's enlarged head fits.
- It is **about 1090 KB of the 2048 KB** a copy may be (the server counts a copy with every default written out, which is a third more than the browser's count, 785 KB; the warning is at 1200 KB, and the held items are the last large part). It has 613 of the 800 shapes a model may have. Its clothes follow the body with about 400 point links (the model may have 1200, with a warning above 600).
- **Not yet** in the model: shading and creases on the body, the open mouth with teeth, hair that follows the chosen head shape (the roots lock to the oval head: on a narrow or wide head they stand a little off the skull), and the old drawing's traced hair.

## Tokens

Surfaces: `surface-raised`, `surface-deep`, `hairline`; text: `ink`, `ink-muted`, `ink-faint`; the Admin accent for the selection, the active row and the primary buttons (ink `on-accent`); `alert` for the danger ring and a refused action. A shape's fill is the character's own colour, set inline as `--shape-fill` (and the colour of a swatch as `--sw`), like the portrait's: only that is data; the chrome on it (the selection ring, handles, labels) stays on tokens (the ring is `accent` over `on-accent`); the rings round weighted points are chrome on `accent`; the card colour behind a framed figure is data in the same way (inline `--tile`), and the frame rectangles, their handles and the pivot markers of a bone pick are chrome on `accent`, `ink-muted` and `surface-raised`, and a shape with no colour falls back to `ink-muted`. A gradient is a definition of the drawing's own (ids carry a prefix per drawing, since inline SVG ids are page-wide) and a clip or a cut-out is a clip path or a mask drawn from the outlines of the shapes involved; none of them adds a colour of its own. The preview tiles draw a hairline in the ground's ink so any colour reads on all four grounds. All text is at least 11px, and every pair is already in the contrast audit.

## Where a writer meets a model

A published model is what the Characters console offers under *Make a mannequin* (Portrait section). A writer's character keeps a **frozen copy** of the model (see Characters, the mannequin): the sliders with *Show to writers* on are the ones a figure may move, held to the writer's smallest and largest; the presets of the kind **Age** are the ages the cast section offers; the frames are the crops (the smallest one named Face or Head is a scene's small head); the genres and default colours decide what a roll may take; the starter palette and the card colour (`tile`) are where a new figure starts. The authored fields are among the sliders: a field with *Show to writers* on is one a figure may move (a variant's or a style's while it is on). Publishing again changes nothing for a character that already has a copy.

## Don't

- Don't type coordinates or amounts: a point is dragged or nudged with the arrow keys, a pivot too, and every number of a slider has a minus and a plus.
- Don't save a test value: the Try it sliders, the form chosen on each axis and the eye are for looking (a preset is how a set of them is kept).
- Don't give a form its own anchors or links: they always read the base outline.
- Don't edit a twin: edit its original, or break the pair.
- Don't drag a linked point: unlink it, or adjust the link with the steppers or the arrow keys.
- Don't save a test colour: the colour chosen for a slot, the test palette, the genre and the locks are for looking (the starter palette and the slots are how a model keeps colour).
- Don't give a model a colour with a hue of its own outside a slot's default colours: a fixed colour in a model is a gray.
- Don't recolour the selected shape to show it is selected: the selection is a ring, in the accent colour (and a shape has no outline of its own to recolour).
- Don't tile a fill pattern or set its size or angle: a pattern is shapes drawn by hand, kept inside a base; draw one more mark.
- Don't make a mark the base of another pattern, or a base a mark: a shape is one or the other.
- Don't trim a shirt to a jacket by clipping each of its shapes by hand: put the object under the jacket, and the trim follows whatever is shown.
- Don't wear two garments under each other: each would trim the other.
- Don't round a corner by drawing the curve points yourself when the shape only needs softening: set its corner roundness (the points stay where they were drawn).
- Don't make a field for what a slider already drives (a turn, a scale, a bend, recorded points, a length): add the drive to that slider. A field is for one property of one feature, and its amounts are the property's.
- Don't give a field to a twin: give it to the original, which the twin follows.
- Don't save which option of a group state is chosen: it is for looking (a pose or a variant keeps what it comes to).
- Don't lock a colour to give a model a colour: a lock holds a part of a colour that is chosen, so a slot with no colour chosen stays gray.
- Don't draw an outline into a model: there is none (the selection ring on the canvas is the Designer's, not the figure's, and a shape has no stroke settings).
- Don't trim a shading band by hand: it is kept inside the shapes of its object when drawn.
- Don't change the paint of a twin or of a shape that follows a template: change the original, or give the shape a slot of its own.
- Don't type a frame's size or a bone's length: drag the frame's rectangle, and lengthen a bone with a Length slider.
- Don't scale a limb by scaling the whole model or its group: give its bone a Length slider, so the group is stretched along the bone and what hangs from it follows.
- Don't save the test card colour: *Use as the starter card colour* is how a model keeps one.
- Don't give an object that keeps its size or stretches to a shape its own anchors: they read the outline as the group holds it, not as it is drawn.
- Don't type a weight or where a crease fades in: click the point (or use the stepper), and use the two steppers of the fade.
- Don't build a bridge on a twin part or hang a twin by itself: build and weight the original, and its twin follows, mirrored.
- Don't save the side things fall to as part of the model: it is a test choice (a preset or a style is how a set of them is kept).
- Don't build a hand on a twin group: build it on the original arm and its twin gets a copy.
- Don't pose one arm by moving both: a Turn slider moves its own side, and the other side has a slider of its own (grab it in Pose mode, or *Pose the other side apart*).
- Don't type where a grip point is: use the steppers, or drag the arm with Reach.
- Don't lock two shapes by hovering: a crossing of two outlines is locked onto the edge, welded and freed by three clicks, and only with the sliders at rest.
- Don't lose the canvas behind a toolbar: the side toolbars fold away and are dragged narrower, and the view is fitted in what they leave uncovered.
- Don't list the cards of a level while one is open: the open card stands alone with its children, and pressing it steps back up.
- Don't put the order of the cards the way it is stored: they are listed topmost first, the front of the figure at the top of the list.
- Don't offer a tab that has nothing to say for what is open: the model has no Rig or Forms tab.
- Don't hide the figure with the layer toggles: it is a layer to move (the gridlines can lie over it), not one to switch off.
- Don't hide a fault: Publish stays disabled, with the reason, until the draft is valid.
- Don't let a new version of a model change characters that already use it.
