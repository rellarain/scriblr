# Character models

Character models are the shapes characters will be drawn from: the body, the clothes and the hair, arranged in **groups** of **objects** made of **shapes**. They are **designed in the Admin console** (Configuration > Characters) and saved as drafts and published one by one, the way the Plot presets are. The designer draws shapes, arranges them, and (since the second phase) **rigs** them: groups nest and turn about pivots, are fixed to a shape of their parent, and are driven by **sliders**. It also **pairs** left and right (a twin that mirrors another shape, object or group), **links points** (clothes that stay on the body, and loosen with a slider) and shows the **back view** with outlines of their own. Since the third phase an object can have **forms** (alternate outlines along axes such as the shape of an eye), the model keeps named **presets** (poses, expressions, ages, builds, outfits), objects and groups can be **switched off** with an eye to look at the rest, and the figure can be **posed by dragging**. Since the fourth step of that phase an object can be the pattern of a **class** (a family of eyes, or the two hands) whose members follow it, and a **style** keeps a named look of an object or a group. Since phase 4 a shape is painted from a **colour slot** (Skin, Hair, Eye...) whose default colours and ranges the model holds, and the designer works on a gray **test character** that can be coloured by hand or **rolled**. It stores nothing about any character.

## The page

`.cmSplit` holds the list (`.cmList`) and the open model (`.cmPane`). The page keeps the Admin's accent. Below 860px of its own width the list folds into one picker button (`.cmPick`) above the model, and the tree, the canvas and the inspector stack; below 1180px the inspector drops under the canvas.

- **List**: one row per model, its name and a **status badge** (`.cmBadge`): *Published v3*, *v3 · unpublished changes*, *Unpublished (was v2)*, *Not published*. Each state has a shape as well as a colour (a tick, a filled dot, a hollow dot, a grey dot). Rows reorder by their grip or Alt + Arrow.
- **Tools** (`.cmTools`): the model's name, its status, then **Publish**, **Unpublish**, **Discard changes**, **Versions** (the published version and the ten before it, each with *Bring into draft*), **Duplicate** and **Delete** (which asks in place first). Publish stays disabled, and the Check panel says why, while the draft is not valid.
- **Tree** (`.cmTree`) with a **Layers | Rig** switch (`.cmTreeModes`, two icon buttons with `aria-pressed`). *Layers*: three layers, **Body, Clothing and Hair**; under each, the groups that have an object on it, their objects and the objects' shapes. A row selects what it names; the buttons add a group, an object (+ beside a layer) or a shape (a template menu beside an object), copy and delete. Groups, objects and shapes **reorder by their grip** (`.cmGrip`; drag, or Alt + Up / Down), and a shape's up and down arrows do the same one step at a time; the order in the tree is the **draw order** (a shape lower in the list is drawn in front, unless its `z` says otherwise). *Rig* (`.cmRigTree`): the groups nested under the group they hang from, each with its level in the pivot chain (`.cmCount`, bold at six, the most); **drag a group's grip onto another to hang it there**, onto the empty space to unhang it, or press **Alt + Right / Left** on the grip to indent or outdent it. A move that would make a circle or a chain longer than six is refused, with the reason beside the canvas. It has a search. Counts beside groups and objects turn bold with a bar under them above the warning limit. Every group and object row has an **eye** (`aria-pressed`, pressed while it is shown): see *Show and hide* below.
- **Canvas** (`.cmCanvas`, an SVG): the shapes in a neutral token (the selection in the accent, the rest dimmed), a grid, the **mirror line** at the centre, and, for the selected shape in the point editor, its **points** and the **curve handles** of the selected point. The shapes are drawn **where the rig puts them**: a shape in a turned group is drawn turned, its points and handles drawn through the group's matrix and a drag undone through it, so a shape can be edited with a slider anywhere on its range. A shape whose points a slider moves, with that slider away from rest, can only be looked at (a note says which, and the toolbar's reset puts the sliders back). The selected group shows its **pivot** (`.cmPivot`: a cross in a ring, drag it or focus it and press the arrow keys; Shift for bigger steps; for a group fixed to a shape the arrow keys step the anchor: to the next or previous point, along the edge, or across and down the shape) and, if it is fixed to a shape, the **anchor** (`.cmAnchor`, a diamond). The mirror line is drawn at the model's own axis. A **twin** is drawn with a dashed outline and its points dimmed: it is shown, not edited. A **linked** point (`.cmPoint--linked`) has a heavier ring in the accent and is not dragged: the arrow keys adjust its link instead (left and right slide it along the edge or change a follow's offset across, up and down change how far out it sits or the offset down; Shift: five times as much). Left button: select a shape, drag a point or a handle, or (in move mode) the whole shape. Double-click an edge: add a point there. **Right button: a click on the grid unselects everything and shows the end result; a click on a shape moves through point editor, move shape and unselected; a drag pans.** Arrow keys move the selected point (Shift: bigger steps; in move mode the whole shape), Delete removes the point, Escape unselects.
- **Toolbar** (`.cmToolbar`, icon buttons with a label and a title): undo, redo, point editor, move shape, **Pose mode** (the hand; see below), mirror editing, snap, grid, zoom in and out, fit the whole figure, **Front view** and **Back view** (two icon buttons with `aria-pressed`; the back view turns the figure round about the mirror line), new shape from a template, and the inspector drawer. There are **no typed number fields**: points are moved by dragging and by the arrow keys.
- **Inspector** (`.cmInspector`, a drawer the toolbar folds away) with eight tabs (`role="tablist"`, icon and label, Left and Right arrows move between them, the strip wraps when it is narrow): **Shape**, **Rig**, **Ranges**, **Forms**, **Presets**, **Colour**, **Proportions** and **Frames** (below). With nothing selected the Shape tab shows the model's name, description and the **Mirror line** (a stepper: the x that mirror editing reflects about, 40 to 160, 100 to begin with). Shape tab: the name, and for an object its layer; for a shape its view (both, front only, back only), its opacity, its template, and its points as numbered chips with the four **point modes** (curve, sharp, curve then sharp, sharp then curve), add a point after the selected one, delete it, reset its handles. Below the points, for a shape: **Pair**, **Links** and **In the front / back view** (all described below). For an object, Pair too; for a group, Pair is in the Rig tab.
- **Previews** (`.cmStrip`, always on, in the view being edited): the selection, or with nothing selected the whole model, at random sizes, tilts and backgrounds, **with every slider at a random value inside its range** (the slider's `rand` range when it has one); *Random* with a re-roll, *Smallest* and *Largest* as stress previews (Smallest also puts every slider at its minimum, Largest at its maximum), and three sizes. The tiles pair a shape token with a ground token so each is readable on all four backgrounds.
- **Checks** (`.cmCheck`): the size of a character's copy of the model, what stops Publish, which limits the model is at or past, and the **rig check** (a slider that drives nothing and slides no anchor and loosens no shape, an anchor that does not keep its group where it is drawn at rest, a shape that leaves the frame when a slider is at its minimum or maximum; at most eight lines), the **notes about forms** (a form that changes a shape which has an outline of its own for the back shows from the front only; a form with another number of points than the base is followed by anchors, links and sliders by the base outline) and the **forms-by-views grid** (`.cmGrid`, a table with a row for each form: what it does from the front, and what becomes of it from behind).
- **Model files**: the model's toolbar has an **Export** icon (the open model's draft) and the list has **Import** and **Export all**. A file (`scriblrCharacterModels`, version 1) holds drafts; an import adds each as an **unpublished draft** (a name that is taken becomes *Name (copy)*), skips what is not a valid model and says what it skipped in a notice (`.cmToast`) with a Dismiss button; a file that is not a model file is refused with an alert.

## Pairs, links and the back view

- **Pair** (`PairPanel`): *Make a twin* gives a shape, an object (with its shapes) or a group (with everything in it and under it) a **twin**: a derived mirror image about the mirror line, with no points of its own, that follows every edit of the original and every slider that moves it. A twin is marked in the tree (`.cmTwin`, the swap icon) and named *Name (twin)*. Its outline is the reflection of the original's, in the group's own space, so a pair of eyes in one group tilt with the head. A twin **group** does what its original does, mirrored, from its own parent: with the same parent for both (the torso) a lean of the torso carries both arms alike; with twin parents (left arm and forearm) it is the plain reflection. A twin has no pivot, anchor, template or point list of its own, and a slider may not drive it (use the original). *Break the pair* makes it a shape, object or group of its own, drawn as it was a moment ago: its sliders are copied mirrored (a turn the other way, recorded points moved the other way across, a bend the other way), its anchor is mirrored onto the twin of the original's shape, and its point links are not copied. Breaking a twin group breaks the twin groups under it too. Deleting an original deletes its twin ("Its twin went with it").
- **Links** (`LinksPanel`): select a point and choose **Weld** (exactly on a point of another shape), **Follow** (stay where it is, move with a point of another shape) or **Lock to an edge** (keep its place along the edge of another shape, as far out as it is now), then click the shape on the canvas (the point or the edge nearest the click; Escape stops). The link is kept on the point that follows and is solved when the model is drawn, so the clothes follow the body whatever turns it, across groups. The list shows each link, with steppers for the distance from the edge and the spot along it (or the offset of a follow) and *Unlink* (the point stays where it is drawn). A shape cannot be linked to itself, to a circle of shapes, or from a twin; a model may have 200 links (a warning above 100). Adding or deleting a point of a shape cuts the links to it, with a note.
- **Looseness**: a shape can take its looseness from a slider (*Make a slider* makes one, 0 to 200%, resting at 100%): the distance of its links (outward from an edge, or a follow's offset) is stretched by the slider over its rest value, so 200% drapes the shirt twice as far from the body and 0% pulls it tight.
- **The back view**: the toolbar's *Back view* turns the figure round about the mirror line. Anchors, links and sliders are the same in both views (they read the front outline, so one edit moves both). A shape shows its front outline from behind until its points are edited in the back view; it then has an **outline of its own for the back** (*Use the front outline again* undoes that). Its depth is its own in the back view once it is moved there: *In front* and *Behind* (in the panel *In the front / back view*) move it one place among the shapes shown in the view being edited, and give that view its own depths (the back follows the front until then). Recording points is done in the front view.

## The rig

The **Rig tab** is for the selected group (or the group an object or shape is in):

- **Pivot and parent**: the chain from the root as a breadcrumb (each parent a link), *Level n of 6 in the pivot chain*, a **Hangs from** select (every group that is not below this one), the pivot's place, and a button that puts it in the middle of the group. Everything in the group turns and scales about the pivot.
- **Attached to a shape**: a group with a parent can be fixed to one shape of the parent or of a group above it, by one of four **anchors** (chips, `aria-pressed`): *Point* (one point of the shape; a stepper), *Edge spot* (a spot along one edge; steppers and a slider), *Slides* (along one edge, where a **slider** puts it), *Inside* (a relative place across and down the shape). **Pick on the canvas** lets a click on a shape of the parent choose it. Attaching puts the pivot on the anchor without moving the drawing. A parent move drags the group along with the anchor.
- **Motion**: the sliders that turn, scale or slide this group; each is a link that opens it in the Ranges tab.

## Sliders

The **Ranges tab** lists the model's sliders (`.cmSlider`), by default those that have to do with the selection (*This selection | All*). **New slider** offers four kinds, each a menu item that says why it is unavailable (*Select a group first*):

- **Turn the group**: turns the selected group about its pivot, −20° at the minimum and +20° at the maximum to begin with.
- **Scale**: scales the selected shape or group (80% and 120%), or the whole model about its feet when nothing is selected (*Size*); the chips say whether across, up and down, or both.
- **Move points (record)**: the canvas edits a copy of the selected shape with every slider at rest; **Set maximum** (or *Set minimum*) writes how far each point moved, **Cancel** drops it. Adding or deleting a point is not recorded.
- **Bend the shape**: a smooth bend along a length of the shape (two steppers for where it starts and ends).

A slider has a **Try it** range (the test value is only for looking and is never saved; a *n off rest* button in the toolbar and a *Reset test values* button put them back), a **sweep** button (minimum to maximum and back, still running with reduced motion because it was asked for), steppers for its **minimum, rest and maximum** (no typed numbers), a **show to writers** toggle (an eye) and a delete that asks in place. Under them, what it drives, one row per **drive** with its own steppers for the amount at each end, and **Add what it drives**. A drive's amount is zero (or 100% for scale) at rest, the stated amount at the maximum and the other at the minimum, in straight lines between.

## Forms

The **Forms tab** is for an object (the selected object, or the object of the selected shape; with a group selected it offers the group's objects). An object has **axes** (`.cmAxis`: the shape of an eye, an expression, a style), each with **forms** (`.cmFormCard`, buttons with `aria-pressed`, each with a small picture of the object in that form and *· default* on the one shown unless another is chosen). A form gives some of the object's shapes **an outline of their own**; every other shape keeps its base outline, and an empty outline hides the shape in that form. An object shows one form of every axis; when two axes give the same shape an outline, **the first axis in the list wins** (the arrows reorder axes).

- **Choosing** a form is only for looking, like the Try it sliders: it is not saved, it counts in the toolbar's *n off rest*, and *Reset test values* puts every axis back on its default form.
- **Editing**: while a form is chosen the canvas edits **that form's outline**, not the base outline: a banner in the tab and the line under the canvas say *Editing form Wide of Eye shape*, and moving a point of a shape the form does not change gives the shape an outline of its own in the form. The base outline (what anchors, links, recorded moves and the rig always read) is left alone, so a form may have another number of points than the base. Choose the default form to edit the base outline again. A shape the form hides cannot be edited (the line under the canvas says so); *Use the base outline* brings it back.
- **Add a form from the current one** copies the outlines drawn now that differ from the base; *Rename*, *Make default*, *Move earlier / later* and *Delete* (asks in place; an axis keeps at least one form) are under the chosen form, with a list of what it does to each shape (*own outline*, *base outline*, *hidden in this form*) and the buttons *Hide … in this form* and *Use the base outline for …*. *Add an axis* starts one with a single form, *Standard*.
- A **twin** follows the form its original shows (use the original's tab); in the back view a shape with an outline of its own for the back shows that one whatever the form.

## Presets

A preset is a named set of **slider values** and of **objects and groups switched off** (the *Presets tab*, `.cmPreset`). It does **not** keep which form is chosen. Its **kind** is a free name (Pose, Expression, Age, Build, Outfit, …) and is its slot: **applying a preset puts back whatever any other preset of the same kind controls, then sets its own**, so a pose replaces the last pose and an expression leaves the arms alone. A preset whose sliders and hidden things are what the figure shows now is marked **On** (a tick and the word, within a hundredth of a slider's unit).

- **Save the current as a preset** takes a name and a kind (the kinds in use are suggested) and keeps the sliders that are away from rest and the things switched off. Each row has **Apply**, *Update from the current*, a name field and a delete that asks in place; chips filter by kind.
- A preset that names a slider or a thing that is gone is refused at Publish; deleting a slider or a thing removes it from the presets.

## Classes

A **class** (the *Class* section at the top of the Shape tab for an object, `.cmClass`) turns one object into a **pattern** and lets you build **members** from it: *Make a class* picks what the class is, **a family** (members are interchangeable and one is shown at a time: almond, round and hooded eyes; a character uses one) or **copies** (every member is drawn, each in its own group: the left and the right hand). The pattern is **only a pattern**: once it has a member it is not drawn at all (before that it is drawn like any object), and a tag on its tree row (a stamp) and on each member (a link) says what it is.

- **Add a member** makes an object right after the pattern (or the last member) **in the pattern's group**, with a shape for every shape of the pattern. Those shapes **follow the pattern**: they have no points of their own, so an edit of the pattern, or a shape added to it, reaches every member at once. A member can be moved to another group with the *Group* dropdown of its panel (the other hand's group).
- **A member may**: give a shape **an outline of its own** (move a point of the shape: the *Follow the pattern again* button drops it), **add shapes of its own** (with the + beside the object), **choose its own form on each of the pattern's axes** (its Forms tab lists the pattern's axes, read-only, and the choice is the member's), and **leave out an inherited shape** (*Hide in this member*). A shape that follows the pattern has no copy or delete button in the tree.
- **The ghost** (the *Ghost of the pattern* toggle in the toolbar) draws a dashed line, behind a member's shape that has an outline of its own, where the pattern's outline is.
- A family shows **one member at a time**; the pattern's panel lists the members as chips, and *Show this member* on a member's panel chooses it. Which member is shown is test state like the chosen forms (not saved, counted in *n off rest*), and a selected pattern or member is always drawn so it can be looked at and edited. The previews choose a member at random.
- **Detach from the class** (asks in place) copies what the member follows once: it keeps its look, with the pattern's outlines and axes as its own, and stops following. **Delete the class** detaches every member. Deleting the pattern detaches the members first (they keep their look). Selecting a shape that follows and pressing Delete is refused: hide it in the member instead.
- A **twin** of a member follows it, and goes off with it when another member of the family is shown.

## Styles

A **style** is a preset that **belongs to** an object or a group (the *Styles* section at the end of the Forms tab, for an object and for a group; it is listed in the Presets tab too, as *Style of Eye*). *Save the current look as a style* keeps, for what its owner holds: the sliders listed under it that are away from rest, the things inside it that are switched off, the forms chosen away from their default on its objects (and on members), and **which member of a family is shown**. A group's style can hide the objects in it. Applying a style sets those choices and puts back whatever any other style of the same owner and kind controls, so a style replaces the last style of its owner and leaves the rest of the figure as it is. A style that names a form, a member, a slider or a thing that is gone is refused at Publish.

## Colour

A model is **gray as saved**: a shape that is painted from no colour slot, or whose slot has no colour chosen, is drawn in a neutral gray, and a fixed colour in a model is a gray (white for an eye white, black, a dark gray). Hue only ever comes from a slot's **default colours** or from the **palette** of the character. The *Colour tab* (the sixth, `BucketIcon`) has six parts:

- **This shape** (or *Every shape of <object>* when an object is selected): the **slot** the shape is painted from, as chips (`No slot` and one per slot). A shape with no slot has a **Fixed gray** stepper (5 per step, Shift 25; *Neutral gray* puts it back). A twin follows its original's paint, and a shape that follows a class's template its template shape's, until it is given a slot of its own.
- **Test colours**: the colours of the *test character* (never saved, like the Try it sliders; they count in *n off rest* and *Reset test values* puts them back to gray). Pick a **slot** to colour, then **Gray**, one of its **default colours** (round swatches, an `.cmSwatch` ring on the chosen one) or a **swatch of the palette**; a **Brightness** slider (inside the slot's range) and, when the slot has room for it, an **Opacity** slider appear. A default colour keeps its own brightness until the slider moves it; a swatch is at the slot's resting brightness. The **Genre** dropdown limits the default colours shown, and **Roll colours and forms** (also the dice in the toolbar) rolls the whole look.
- **Palette**: the swatches a character will own, each a **hue** (the app's `ColorRange`) and a **saturation** slider (no typed numbers; brightness and opacity belong to the slot), 1 to 10 of them. This is a *test copy*: **Use as the starter palette** saves it in the model (the palette a new character starts with) and **Start over from the starter palette** drops the copy. Editing a swatch recolours every slot that uses it.
- **Slots of the model**: each slot opens to its **default colours** (round swatches; click one to edit it: a name, **hue**, **saturation** and **lightness** sliders and the **genres** it fits, delete asks nothing because a colour is cheap), its **brightness** and **opacity** ranges (a smallest, a resting and a largest value, one stepper each, the smallest never passing the resting one) and a delete that asks in place and says how many shapes will go gray. A new model starts with the **standard slots** (Skin, Hair, Eye, Lip, Clothing, Shoes, with the default colours of the portrait engine's own swatches; the fantasy skins and eyes fit only Fantasy); an older model has *Add the standard slots*.
- **Genres of the model**: tags (Fiction, Fantasy and Non-fiction to begin with, at most 12) a default colour may fit; a colour with none fits every genre. A character made for a project of a genre will draw its random colours from those that fit (the project's own genre setting arrives with the writer's editor).

**Rolling** (*Roll the look*, a dice in the toolbar and in the Colour tab) gives the test character a default colour that fits the genre or, 30 % of the time (never for a skin or an eye), a swatch at a brightness in the middle half of the slot's range, a **form on every axis** and the **member of every family**. A **lock** (beside the eye on every group and object row, `aria-pressed`) keeps a group or object, with everything in it, as it is: its forms, its family's member and the colours of the slots it is painted from. A lock is not saved and does not count as off rest.

**On the canvas** every shape is drawn in the test colours, in the previews (each tile has a palette and a colouring of its own; Smallest and Largest put brightness and opacity at the ends of each slot's range) and in the pictures of forms. The **selection is a ring** (a wide `ink` stroke under a narrow `surface-raised` one, `.cmRing`), so it reads on any colour; the rest of the figure is dimmed as before.

## Paint effects

Since phase 4b a shape can be more than one flat colour. The **Paint** section of the Colour tab (under *This shape*, for a selected shape) holds what is done to its colour; all of it is the model's own data, saved with the shape, and the test character only chooses the colours underneath it.

- **Tone**: *Darker*, *Base* or *Lighter* (a step is 12 darker or 9 lighter in lightness, held to 2 to 98 %).
- **Derived tone**: the shape is a *Shadow*, a *Mid tone* or a *Highlight* of its own colour (a brow is a shadow of the hair), by an **Amount** stepper (0.02 to 0.8, a hundredth a step, Shift a tenth); *Own colour* takes it away.
- **Gradient**: *No gradient*, or a direction (*Down*, *Up*, *Left*, *Right*, *Radial*, from the middle outward); the second colour is *Darker of itself* or **another slot's colour** as the test character has chosen it.
- **Blend mode**: *Normal*, *Multiply*, *Screen* or *Overlay*.
- **Clip to** and **Cut by**: a shape can be kept inside another (*Not clipped* or a chip for each shape of the object and every helper), and other shapes can cut a hole in it (a chip each, at most 8). **Pick on the canvas** does the same by clicking a shape (Escape stops); the status line under the canvas says what is being picked.
- **Helper shape**: never drawn in the previews and pictures of forms; on the canvas it is a dotted outline with no fill, so it can still be picked and edited. A helper that nothing clips to or is cut by is named in the Checks.
- **Add shading**: a **shadow**, **mid tone** or **highlight** band along the **left**, **right**, **top** or **bottom** edge of the shape, whichever edge faces that way. It is a new shape in the object whose six points are **locked to that edge** (edge links), so it follows the shape wherever sliders, forms and poses take it, and it lies just above the object's top shape. A *shading shape* is a tone of its slot's colour at full strength, kept inside the other shapes of its object (the union of those that are drawn, not helpers); it shows *Shading* instead of tone and gradient, with its kind and an **Amount** stepper. At most 10 in an object (a twin's is not counted); a shape that has a twin gets the twin of its band on the other side. The band follows the edge of the shape's **base outline**, as anchors and links do: a form that draws the shape another way still shows the band along the base's edge (inside the drawn shape, since it is kept inside).

A shape that **takes its paint from another** (a twin, a shape that follows a class's template) uses all of these from that shape, and what it clips to or is cut by is turned into its own twin or member's shape; the section says where the paint comes from instead of letting it be changed, until the shape is given a **colour slot of its own**. Breaking a pair and detaching a member **keep** the paint the shape had.

**Forms may change paint.** In the Forms tab each shape of the object has *Colour slot* and *Tone* lists for the chosen form (*Same as the shape* leaves its own; *No slot (gray)* paints a gray; *Use the paint of <shape>* puts it back). The first axis, in order, whose chosen form has a look for the shape wins; a member of a class takes the template's looks under its own choice of form. A form cannot change a gradient, a blend or a clip.

There is **no outline** setting and shading is always at full strength: the strokes on the canvas are only the Designer's chrome.

## Proportions

Since phase 5a a model has a **skeleton of measured lengths** and says how far a writer may change each proportion. The **Proportions** tab (the body icon) holds both; the Rig tab says how an object fits the body, and the Ranges tab has the Length slider.

- **Bones**: a bone runs from the **pivot of a group** to the **pivot of another group** or to a **point of a shape**. *Add a bone* starts one from the selected group to a group that hangs from it; its start is chosen from the original groups (a twin follows its original), its end by a list, a stepper for the point, or *Pick its end on the canvas* (a marker on every group's pivot, or a click on a shape, which takes the point nearest the click; Escape stops). Each bone reads **live**, with the Try it sliders: its length in units (the model's own space, before the whole-model Height), in **heads** and as a **percent of its length at rest**. *Use as the head* makes a bone the unit: the tab then says how many heads tall the figure stands (the same at any Height).
- **Length sliders**: *Add a Length slider* on a bone (or *Length of a bone* in the Ranges tab's New slider and Add what it drives menus) makes a slider whose drive stretches the bone's group **along the bone** (a percent of its length at rest at the slider's minimum and maximum, 80 and 120 to begin with) and **everything that hangs from it follows**; the other side's twin follows too. A bone that has no length at rest, because its two places are on top of each other, cannot be stretched: the Checks say so.
- **What a writer may choose**: for every slider that is offered to writers, two steppers, *Writer's smallest* and *Writer's largest*, inside the slider's own range. The resting value is always allowed, so the steppers stop there; a span that reaches both ends of the slider is no limit, and the round arrow lets writers choose anywhere again. The same steppers are on the slider in the Ranges tab. Random previews stay inside this span when the slider has no span of its own.
- **How an object fits the body** (Rig tab, for the selected object): *Scales with its group* (the default), *Keeps its size* (its group carries and turns it, but the scale of the group and of the groups above it is not applied to its drawing: the eyes keep their size when the head grows) or *Stretches to a shape* (a list or *Pick on the canvas*: it grows and moves as that shape's box does since rest, and does not turn). Only the drawing changes: anchors, links and recorded moves read the outline as the group holds it. A twin fits as its original does. The Checks name every object that uses a mode other than the default.
- **Age and build** are presets: *Age* (Infant, Child, Teen, Adult, Elder) and *Build* (Slim, Average, Broad, Heavy) set the Height, Head size, arm lengths and shoulder width sliders together.

## Frames

A **frame** is a named crop of the figure: a rectangle in the model's space and the groups and objects left out when the figure is seen through it (a bust leaves out the legs). The **Frames** tab (the card icon) lists them; while it is open every frame is a rectangle on the canvas (`.cmFrame`: dashed, solid with eight handles for the one being edited).

- **A size is never typed**: *Edit on the canvas* selects a frame, then **drag its border** to move it and **its handles** to resize it (the opposite side stays, and a frame is never smaller than 8 units); with the border focused the arrow keys nudge it by 1 (**Alt**: 5) and **Shift** with them resizes it. *Add a frame* starts one round the selection (the whole figure when nothing is selected), in the view being edited; from behind a frame is mirrored about the axis, so it stays on the same part of the body.
- **Leaves out**: chips for every group and object (`aria-pressed`).
- **View through**: the canvas is cropped to the frame, on the **card colour**, without what the frame leaves out; editing is off while looking through; *Whole figure* goes back. The previews have the same choice (*Whole* and a chip per frame): every tile is cropped to the frame on the card colour.
- **Card colour**: hue, saturation and lightness sliders for a test colour (never saved); *Use as the starter card colour* keeps it as the model's `tile`, the colour new characters start with, and *Start over from the starter colour* drops it.

## Show and hide

The **eye** on every group and object row (`aria-pressed`: pressed while shown) switches it off for looking: its shapes are not drawn on the canvas or in the previews, the row is dimmed, and it still does its part in the rig (anchors and links read it). Switching a group off hides the groups hung under it. Like the Try it sliders it is not saved with the model; a preset keeps it. It counts in *n off rest*.

## Pose mode

The hand in the toolbar (**Pose mode**, `aria-pressed`) lets the figure be grabbed instead of edited. Every drag is the inverse of what a slider does, so the sliders follow (watch the Ranges tab) and the result is only test values; *Save as preset* in the **pose bar** (`.cmPoseBar`) keeps a pose as a preset of kind Pose (without the hidden things). Nothing here changes the model.

- **Drag a part** to turn its group about the pivot: the slider whose Turn drive turns that group (or the nearest group above it that has one) is set from the angle the pointer sweeps, held to its range. A ring (`.cmJoint`) marks each pivot that can turn. A twin cannot be posed (pose its original).
- **Drag a square** (`.cmScaleHandle`) on a group that has a Scale slider to scale it; **drag a circle** (`.cmSlideHandle`) on a group fixed along an edge by a slider to slide it.
- **Reach**: with the *Reach* button on, or **Alt** held, drag a hand or a foot and the limb bends at its joints (the Turn sliders of the grabbed group and the groups above it, at most four) to bring that point to the pointer, within each slider's range; a target out of reach leaves the limb stretched toward it.
- **Escape** in the middle of a drag puts everything back; *Reset pose* puts the sliders at rest. The pointer is only a shortcut: every pose is reachable with the Ranges tab's sliders.

## Saving and publishing

Two layers, as in the Plot presets: every edit goes to the **draft** through the shared autosave (off by default; the Save button and its toggle are in the page's head), and **Publish** copies the draft to the published copy, refusing a draft that is not valid (no name, a name another published model has, a shape of fewer than three points, an object or group that points at something missing, a shape in two objects, a pivot chain deeper than six, an anchor whose shape is missing or not in the parent or above it, an anchor that slides on a slider that is not there, a slider that drives something that is not there, recorded point moves for points a shape no longer has, a twin of something that is missing, of a twin or of itself, a slider that drives a twin, a link to a shape, point or edge that is not there or to its own shape or round in a circle, a looseness slider that is not there, a back outline of fewer than three points, more than 200 point links, an axis whose default form is missing, a form that changes a shape that is not in its object or a twin or has an outline of one or two points, a preset that sets a slider or hides a thing that is not there, a shape that uses a colour slot that does not exist, a default colour that fits a genre that does not exist, a clip, cut-out or gradient that points at a shape or colour slot that does not exist, a form that changes the paint of a shape that is not in its object or a twin or from a colour slot that does not exist, more than 10 shading shapes in an object, a bone that starts or ends at a group, shape or point that does not exist or starts at a twin group, a slider that lengthens a bone that does not exist, a head bone that does not exist, an object that stretches to a shape that does not exist, is its own or has none chosen, a frame that leaves out something that does not exist, a class whose template is missing, a twin, a member or the template of two classes, a member of a class that is not there or of the class it is the pattern of, a shape that copies a shape that is not in the pattern of its member's class or is a twin, more than 24 members in a class, a style that belongs to something that is not there or chooses a form or a member that is not there, a copy over the size limit). **Discard changes** returns the draft to the published copy; a model never published is cleared. New versions change nothing in characters that already use a model: a character will keep its own copy.

## Tokens

Surfaces: `surface-raised`, `surface-deep`, `hairline`; text: `ink`, `ink-muted`, `ink-faint`; the Admin accent for the selection, the active row and the primary buttons (ink `on-accent`); `alert` for the danger ring and a refused action. A shape's fill is the character's own colour, set inline as `--shape-fill` (and the colour of a swatch as `--sw`), like the portrait's: only that is data; the chrome on it (the selection ring, handles, labels) stays on tokens; the card colour behind a framed figure is data in the same way (inline `--tile`), and the frame rectangles, their handles and the pivot markers of a bone pick are chrome on `accent`, `ink-muted` and `surface-raised`, and a shape with no colour falls back to `ink-muted`. A gradient is a definition of the drawing's own (ids carry a prefix per drawing, since inline SVG ids are page-wide) and a clip or a cut-out is a clip path or a mask drawn from the outlines of the shapes involved; none of them adds a colour of its own. The preview tiles draw a hairline in the ground's ink so any colour reads on all four grounds. All text is at least 11px, and every pair is already in the contrast audit.

## Don't

- Don't type coordinates or amounts: a point is dragged or nudged with the arrow keys, a pivot too, and every number of a slider has a minus and a plus.
- Don't save a test value: the Try it sliders, the form chosen on each axis and the eye are for looking (a preset is how a set of them is kept).
- Don't give a form its own anchors or links: they always read the base outline.
- Don't edit a twin: edit its original, or break the pair.
- Don't drag a linked point: unlink it, or adjust the link with the steppers or the arrow keys.
- Don't save a test colour: the colour chosen for a slot, the test palette, the genre and the locks are for looking (the starter palette and the slots are how a model keeps colour).
- Don't give a model a colour with a hue of its own outside a slot's default colours: a fixed colour in a model is a gray.
- Don't recolour the selected shape to show it is selected: the selection is a ring.
- Don't draw an outline into a model: there is none (the stroke on the canvas is the Designer's, not the figure's).
- Don't trim a shading band by hand: it is kept inside the shapes of its object when drawn.
- Don't change the paint of a twin or of a shape that follows a template: change the original, or give the shape a slot of its own.
- Don't type a frame's size or a bone's length: drag the frame's rectangle, and lengthen a bone with a Length slider.
- Don't scale a limb by scaling the whole model or its group: give its bone a Length slider, so the group is stretched along the bone and what hangs from it follows.
- Don't save the test card colour: *Use as the starter card colour* is how a model keeps one.
- Don't give an object that keeps its size or stretches to a shape its own anchors: they read the outline as the group holds it, not as it is drawn.
- Don't hide a fault: Publish stays disabled, with the reason, until the draft is valid.
- Don't let a new version of a model change characters that already use it.
