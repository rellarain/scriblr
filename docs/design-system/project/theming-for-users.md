# Theming, for the people who use Scriblr

Scriblr changes its look with the time of day, and you choose the colours.

## The four times of day

| | Looks like | Starts (you can change it) |
|---|---|---|
| **Dawn** | light, with dark text, soft colours | 4:00 AM |
| **Day** | light, with dark text, vivid colours | 10:00 AM (always on) |
| **Dusk** | dark, with white text, vivid colours | 4:00 PM |
| **Night** | dark, with white text, soft colours | 10:00 PM |

You choose which times of day to use (at least one): with two or more on the app changes at the times on the day bar; with one on it always looks like it. The small painted sky at the top of the window shows where you are: the sun or moon button steps to the next time of day that is on and holds it there; the time and date go back to following the clock.

## Four colours

In **Settings** (on the Dash) you keep a list of up to ten **hues** on a 0 to 360 colour track and drag them onto the four colours of each time of day. Nothing changes in the rest of the app until you save. The four colours are:

- **Theme**: the quiet colour of backgrounds and panels;
- **Accent**: buttons, the open tab, anything you can press;
- **Alert**: what needs your attention, such as "Save failed";
- **Admin accent**: for administrators only.

You choose only the hue. The time of day decides how bright and how strong each colour is, so the same hue is soft at night and vivid at noon. Whatever hue you pick, **text stays readable**: if a colour you choose would make its text hard to read (a bright yellow button, say), Scriblr nudges its brightness a little, or switches the text from white to dark, so it reads. You will not be able to make unreadable text.

## Your books have colours too

Each project, book, arc and chapter can have its own colour, again a hue from 0 to 360 (a series shows the project's). A book stays within 90 degrees of the project's colour, an arc within 60 of its book's and a chapter within 30 of its arc's, so a book's parts look like family; a square button beside the slider sets a colour back to the one above it. The levels of the Writer are filled with their colour, and everything inside a book (the page edges, the tabs, the spine on the shelf) takes it. A book with no colour of its own is orange.

Older projects that used a darker or lighter shade of a colour keep the hue; the shade is gone, because it made text harder to read.

## The page

In the Draft and the preview the page is paper: light with dark text by day, dark with light text at night. Its cards get a little deeper each time, and its text, error messages and reaction hearts follow your accent and alert colours.

## Save and restore

Your colours are saved with your other settings. The **Save** and **Restore** buttons in Settings keep or undo the changes you have made since you last saved.
